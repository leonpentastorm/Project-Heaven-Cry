## 1. The fantasy and the loop

A squad of one to four makes characters at level 1 and walks a branching road of fights, shops, forges, training grounds, shrines and rests to a Boss; beating it, they take **a big passive boon** and move to **another tile of the world**: a new setting, a fresh path, harder. **Survival is endless in stages; how far you get is the score.** Every fight is generated for this squad at this depth and stage, from the enemy table and the balance contract; ordinary enemies fall behind as you level (you feel the power you gained), Elites and Bosses keep pace and, stage by stage, pull ahead. After each fight: XP, credits, ammunition, and a weapon from three (or credits instead). **No Narrator is needed:** the engine decides everything; a Narrator in the room only adds flavour.

**The loop of a node:** walk on along your lane, or, at the camp, a junction or the vault's fork, choose the next lane by what it holds ahead (rules 8 and 9) → it plays (a fight on a generated map; a shop or a Forge, full screen; a rest, a training ground or a shrine; a cache; a Crossroads) → rewards → level up if earned → the map. **The loop of a stage:** 15 nodes → the Boss → pick one boon of three → pick the next tile of two or three, or retire with the score.

**Worked example: seed `PHC-0001`, two players, stage 1, the first four nodes.** Kade (built for Durability: the soldier kit, the service rifle, Class 3) and Ha (built for Empathy: the `mender` kit, a pistol and an Attuner), level 1, 150 credits each. Stage 1's tile: the AHOC outpost (its gate and Boss in the same setting).

## 2. Rules

Adjudication: every rule is `engine`, except the optional Narrator flavour (rule 33), which decides nothing.

**The mode and the run**
1. **Survival is a MODE chosen in the room** (section 8.1), not a campaign: `settings.play` is `survival`, `roguelike` or `campaign` (`data/modes.json`; only Survival is available). The run's state is `g.run` beside `g.campaign` (rule 36). Why not a generated campaign: the campaign format is Narrator content loaded once (`arc`, `opening` required; `campaign/load`), while a run is state changed by every action and replayed from the event log; the Proving Ground already shows the working pattern (`g.sandbox`, its `sandbox/*` actions, Narrator and story actions refused). The run carries a generated campaign *shell* (id `survival`) whose `zones` are the fights' generated maps, so every map reader (`zoneMap`, fog, objects) works unchanged.
2. **Who plays:** one to four players, one character each, all **level 1**, made in the room or brought as a level 1 draft (a draft above level 1 is refused: "Survival starts at level 1."). Each builds its **loadout at the camp** before node 1 (rule 28; rules 1.52: one free weapon, one free focus, a budget of 300 credits at the shop's prices); what it does not spend is its purse (`start_credits` is 0 since rules 1.52; it was 150), **its own**; every reward after is its own too (rule 47).
3. **Stages:** a stage is **15 nodes** on any path (`stage.length`), the 15th its Boss. Winning the Boss gives a boon (rules 30 to 31) and the next stage on a new tile (rule 32). Levels, gear, credits, boons and Devotion carry; the stage number raises the budget (rule 19).
4. **The end:** only by **losing** (a fight ends `loss`: `checkOver`, every character Down or dead, nobody standing to Raise; a solo character Down is a loss) or by **retiring** after a Boss (`run/retire`, host). Either way the summary keeps the score: stages cleared, nodes walked, levels, boons (rule 35), and each character is **kept as a draft at the level it reached** for other rooms (Leon, 13 Nov; a run itself always starts at level 1, rule 2).
5. **Death inside a run** (Leon, 13 Nov): a character who dies in a fight is **out for the rest of that fight** (it can't be Raised; it waits for the team). **If the team wins, it comes back at 1 HP** at the fight's end, with its gear, and takes its full share of the fight's rewards (XP, credits, the ammunition pack, its pick): the team won. If the team loses, the run is over (rule 4). Downed characters in a won fight stand at 1 HP (`unitToCharacter`, as today). A death costs nothing past the fight it happened in (question 4).
6. **Level 20 is the cap:** XP past it counts only to the score (`renown`).

**The path map** (rules 1.48, Leon's third batch: lanes; it replaces rules 1.40's rows)
7. **Rows and lanes:** 15 depths, a node a depth on any route. The stage starts at **the camp** (before depth 1), from which **every lane** can be taken: 3 or 4 lanes (`map.lanes`). Depth 1 holds one node a lane, **plain fights**. **Row 8: the gate**, one ELITE node every lane passes. **Row 13: the final junction**, one node every lane passes. Row 14: one SHOP and one REST, both reached from the final junction. Row 15: the BOSS. About 43 nodes, and the vault's two.
8. **Lanes and junctions:** a lane node has **exactly one way forward**. Lanes meet only at **junctions**: two or three neighbouring lanes (`junction.sizes`) merge into one node and split again onto the same lanes; there, at the gate and at the final junction, and only there, a party changes lane. A lane's run between two meeting nodes (a **segment**) is 2 to 4 nodes (`junction.segment`): before the gate a group of lanes meets once, at depth 3, 4 or 5; after it every lane runs straight to the final junction. Links never cross. The whole map, with every node's KIND, is visible from the start; what a fight holds is rolled on entry.
9. **Kinds and the generator.** Each segment takes a **theme** (`map.themes`, names *(Suggested)*: the hard road holds an Elite; the market road a shop, its other nodes fights and now and then a cache; the armourer's road the Forge; the drill road a training ground, now and then a shrine; the quiet road a rest, now and then a Crossroads), and the segments leaving one node take **different themes and never offer the same things**, so the choice is made by reading ahead. A junction node rolls its kind (`junction_kinds`: fight 8, shop 2, rest 2, event, cache, shrine, training 1 each). Placement: row 1 plain fights; no Elite before depth 4 or in rows 7 and 9; no shop and no rest in row 13 (row 14 offers both); no special kind linked to its own kind. **Guarantees, repaired deterministically** (a fight on the routes that lack it becomes the kind, the one on most of them first; a draw that cannot meet them is drawn again from the next stream, `map#<stage>#<n>`, at most 8 times, more than 95% of maps are the first draw):
   - (a) **Every route passes a shop and a rest** before row 14 (`every_route`): a party can no longer step sideways to find one, so no lane may lead past both. **One vault** (rule 13): a spur off an edge lane node of depths 6 to 10, never a junction.
   - (b) **No two of the same special kind in a row:** an Elite, shop, Forge, training ground, shrine, rest, cache or Crossroads never links to another of its kind.
   - (c) **A shop within reach:** from the camp and from every junction a shop lies at most `map.shop_gap` 6 nodes ahead on some route; and the **reference route** (sections 5.4 and 11) exists: a shop in rows 3 to 7 and another in 9 to 12, never more than 6 nodes between its shops (row 14's counts; the gap runs on into the next stage).
   - (d) **On the reference route:** a Forge in rows 3 to 13 and a rest in rows 3 to 12.
   - (e) **A shop and a rest before the Boss:** the final junction leads to row 14's shop and rest. *Why two lane-ends and not one camp that is both:* the choice stays a choice (refill, or heal before the Boss), and both are the node kinds the engine already has; a camp that is both would make every route pass both and say nothing.
   **What a stage's map holds** (measured over 1,000 seeds × 5 stages, rules 1.48; a route's mean, the 5% to 95% band in brackets): fights 18.3 a map, **5.75 a route (4 to 8; 85% of routes hold 5 to 8)**; Elites 2.9 and the gate, 1.5 a route; shops 6.1, 2.2 a route (1 to 4; row 14's counted half); rests 5.8, 2.0; Forges 2.2, 0.7; training grounds 2.1, 0.7; shrines 1.0, 0.4; caches 1.1, 0.4; Crossroads 0.9, 0.3; the vault's guard and chest 1 each (off every route). Section 3.1 has each kind's gain and cost.
10. **The tile's setting** is the stage's (one of the six, section 3.3); the Boss, the gate and the safe nodes' faces (section 3.4) belong to it.
11. **Choosing (co-op):** a lean matters only where the way forks (the camp, a junction, the vault's fork; rules 1.48): on a lane with one way forward the first player's click walks the squad on. Where it forks, each player marks a reachable node (`run/lean`); the marks show live; when every connected player has leaned, the node with most leans is entered; a tie goes to the host's lean; the host may go at once (`run/go`). Solo: a click goes. Built like the Proving Ground's READY marks. The boon (rule 30) is picked the same way per character, the next tile (rule 32) the same way as a node. **Leaving a shop or a Forge** works the same way: each player's "Ready to leave" (`run/leave`) is a mark; when every connected player is ready the party returns to the map; the host may close it at once (section 8.3).
12. **Seeded:** each stage's map comes from the run's seed through its own stream (`rngFor(seed, 'map#' + stage)`, section 4.2); each node's encounter, offers and stock from `rngFor(seed, node id + purpose)`. A shared seed gives everyone the same run (a daily seed later). Fights' dice come from `g.rng`, as every fight's.

**Node kinds**
13. **FIGHT, ELITE, BOSS:** generated encounters (rules 15 to 22); an ELITE has at least one Elite; the BOSS a Boss and its guard. **SHOP** (rules 25 to 27): refills, rolled gear, selling, the grade step at full price, gives. **FORGE** (rule 44): the grade step cheaper, the Relic step, mods, worn pieces, armour fitting. **TRAINING GROUND** (rule 45): one big training each. **SHRINE** (rule 46): Devotion, a knowledge piece or a patron's token. **REST** (rule 24): the heal and one small training. **VAULT GUARD and VAULT** (rules 1.48, Leon's third batch; one a map, rule 9a): the guard is a fight with an Elite fight's budget × `vault.guard_factor` 1.3 (bulk and pressure) and the setting's named guard (an Elite, `settings[].vault_guard`; the template `vault_watch`); it pays as an Elite and counts for XP, the tally and the stage's log like any fight, and a wipe there ends the run. The vault is a chest: each character takes `credits.vault` 2 × c(d) and **a pick of three weapons or foci one grade above the stage's pick cap** (`vault.grade_up` 1, at most `vault.grade_max` Relic: stage 1 Masterwork, from stage 2 Relic), or credits instead. It leads nowhere: afterwards the party walks on along the lane it left, at no cost and no time. Optional: the fork says "The trail forks: a guarded vault", and a party may pass it by. **CACHE:** a pick of three armour pieces or items (at least two of them lasting: a mod or a worn piece, rule 41), credits and ammunition, no fight. **EVENT** (the node card says **Crossroads**): a trade, no fight, no check (rule 42). Gives between players at every node with no fight (rule 47). *Cut on purpose:* trapped caches (a trap needs a check and a judged consequence; it comes with the roguelike's rooms).
14. **What each node gives** (section 3.1 has the numbers; section 11.3 what lasts): every won fight gives XP, credits, the ammunition pack (rule 23), drill for every kill (as today) and a weapon pick (three weapons, or credits instead); an Elite also an armour-or-item pick with a lasting piece in it; a cache an armour-or-item pick; a Crossroads a trade; a rest the heal and a small training (rule 39); a training ground a big training (rule 45); a shrine a rite, a piece or a token (rule 46); a shop refills and the grade step at full price (rule 37); a Forge the grade step cheaper, the Relic step and the kit (rule 44); a Boss a better weapon pick and the boon. **Every node leaves something that lasts, and each axis of growth has its own node** (section 11).

**The encounter generator** (it is the roguelike's too)
15. **Input:** the party (levels, size, who stands), the node's kind and depth, the stage and its setting, the seed. **Output:** foes (`FoeSpec`: tier, weapon, kind, level, caster, tags, scale, spawn zone) and a map (rule 21).
16. **The budget unit is the Soldier-equivalent (SE),** on two axes (section 5.1): **bulk** (rifle-turns to remove it ÷ a Soldier's) sets the fight's length; **pressure** (Soldier-turns it deals a round) caps its danger. Per tier at equal level (`tiers`): Vermin 0.3 / 0.3, Minion 0.55 / 0.75, Soldier 1.0 / 1.0, Elite 2.1 / 2.2, Boss 7.9 / 3.0.
17. **A fight's budget in stage 1:** bulk = `bulk_per_char_round` **0.25** × party size N × target rounds R; R (`rounds`): a plain fight 5 (depths 1 and 2: 4), Elite and gate 6.5, Boss 9. Pressure cap = `pressure_per_char`[N] × N (1.2 solo, 1.25 for two, 1.3 for three or four; rules 1.49: **2.0 solo, 1.7 for two**). Accepted with bulk within **±15%** (`bulk_band`) and pressure at most **+10%** over the cap (`pressure_band`). At most `max_foes`[N] foes: 4 / 6 / 8 / 10.
18. **The lag (Leon's rule, level-scale section 6):** fight level = the party's average, floored (`enemies.json levels.fight_level`); a foe's level = max(1, fight level − lag): **Vermin and Minions 3, Soldiers 1 (2 from fight level 10), Elites and Bosses 0** (the `lag` field on each tier row). The floor makes the lag grow as the run goes. Priced at ×`lag_factor` **0.83** a level of gap on bulk and pressure (section 5.2): a lagging fight keeps its length with more bodies, not stronger ones.
19. **The stage term (the endless curve)** (rules 1.49 fitted it by simulated runs: `stage.bulk` **0.42**, `stage.pressure` **0.35**, `elite_up` **per 2, max 4**; the numbers below are draft 4's, kept for the reasoning): stage s multiplies the bulk target by `stage.bulk` = 1 + **0.35** × (s − 1) (draft 2 had 0.25; Leon accepted the curve's target, and section 11.6 shows gear would let a party of four reach stage 5 at 0.25, so the dial moves to hold the target: question 3) and the pressure cap by `stage.pressure` = 1 + **0.15** × (s − 1); **Elites and Bosses stand `stage.elite_up` = min(s − 1, 3) levels above the fight level** (never above 20; the gap rule's to-hit both ways applies; priced ×1.2 a level, the lag factor's inverse). When `max_foes` is reached, or the pressure cap stops another foe, and the bulk is still short, every foe takes the rest as `scale.hp` (rule 20; HP adds bulk, not pressure). Difficulty grows about 35% a stage in length and 15% in danger, faster than what a stage adds to the party: its levels (ratio-neutral under the level scale, except the lag), one boon (about +10%) and its gear (a grade step, about +8%, in stages 1 and 2; a mod or a relic property after; section 11.6): a run ends.
20. **Party size and the big units** (rules 1.49: the solo Elite 0.75 / 0.75, the duo Elite 1 / 1, the duo Boss's damage 0.8; the solo Boss as Leon settled it): an Elite or Boss alone can exceed a small party's whole budget, so it spawns scaled (`share`, by N: Boss HP ×0.28 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1, damage ×0.4 / 0.6 / 0.8 / 1; Elite HP ×0.7 / 0.9 / 1 / 1, damage ×0.55 / 0.8 / 1 / 1) and costs its scaled bulk and pressure. The Elite-and-Boss guard (rules 1.17) holds on the scaled HP.
21. **Composition by template** (section 3.2, data): (a) eligible templates by node kind, depth, setting kind and N; weighted, never the last fight's; (b) its required slots (the anchor); (c) its optional slots in order, each the cheapest fill keeping pressure under the cap, until bulk is in the band or `max_foes`; (d) if the band can't be met: drop optional slots, swap a Soldier for two Minions, then the next template; (e) weapons from the setting's pools by tier and kind, casters from `enemies.json casters` / `soul_casters`, tags (`skulk` for an ambush) from the slot. **Never a Celestial** (the Accords: an uncaused appearance is a catastrophe).
22. **The map** (section 4.3): size from the units (`map_size`: up to 6 units 16 × 16, up to 10 20 × 20, else 26 × 26), built from the setting's stamps, checked (two lanes, start gap 10 to 14, cover share), `open_ground` cropped after 8 tries. Squad on the west edge; foes by the template. **Deterministic:** only the seeded stream and sorted lists; the same seed, party and node give the same fight (a resumed run re-enters it).

**Rewards, ammunition and the shop**
23. **Ammunition never runs out between shops:** after every won fight each character (the Downed and the dead too: they stand at 1 HP, rule 5) gets **a magazine's worth of rounds for every gun it carries** (hand or pack; `ammo_pack` 1, an Elite 2, a Boss 3; for loose-loaded guns a magazine's capacity of rounds), and **one standard core** if it carries a Sigil or Visualizer focus. Enemies' rolled drops (`loot_tables.json`) stay on the field's piles as today. The arithmetic is in section 5.4.
24. **REST (free):** `applyRest(g, 'long')` (all HP, MP full, toxins and other persistent class resources restocked, per-rest uses back, a day for Devotion's counter) and **a new session** (`beginSession`: Echo 1, per-session uses such as a Miracle back); **and each character takes one small training** (rule 39: drill, study, practice or Devotion), a lasting gain on top of the heal, never instead of it. The big trainings are the training ground's (rule 45). Gives as at every safe node (rule 47).
25. **SHOP** (a full screen, section 8.3). **Always stocked, never sold out, fixed fair price** (`shop.always`): rounds of **every calibre the engine knows** (rifle, .32 Decrkm, revolver, .50 frontier, .700, shell, arrow, bolt, 40 mm grenade, rocket, fuel canister, core charge: 12) and a magazine, speedloader, drum or belt for every gun the party carries; the four special rounds (`ammo_armor_piercing`, `ammo_hollow_point`, `ammo_incendiary`, `ammo_enhanced`); thrown consumables (frag, smoke, flashbang and incendiary grenades, throwing knives); **standard cores** (Sigil, Visualizer and the core rifle); **spare parts**, **stims**, **field kits**, ration packs; and the **restock** service (refill a persistent class resource, such as toxin doses, at `restock_price` a point). *Why these:* each is what some build spends every fight; a class must never be dead in the water because a shop rolled badly. **Rolled** (`stock.slots` 6: 3 weapons including foci and mana arms, 2 items, 1 rarer piece; draft 3's 8 included 2 armour pieces or mods, which are the Forge's now, rule 44), at the depth's price band (rule 26), rarity standard 70 / fine 25 / masterwork 5 (from stage 2: 50 / 35 / 15; from stage 4 relic 5), biased and priced by the setting's shopkeeper (section 3.4); shared stock, first buyer; each character pays from its own credits. **The grade step** (Leon, 13 Nov: "in the shop you can upgrade the grade of your weapon with many money"): to Fine or Masterwork at the full `grade_up.price` (rule 37); the Relic step is the Forge's (rule 44). **Selling** (rule 27). **Gives** between players (rule 47). Armour fitting moved to the Forge (rule 44).
25a. **A focus's rarity counts on its door** (Leon, 13 Nov, "for now"): a focus in a hand adds its rarity's `acc` (Fine +1, Masterwork, Relic and Celestial +2) to its door's rolls to hit (a Sigil's, an Outburst's) and to the save TN of its door's forced saves (a Bond's); the same number `rarity.acc` a weapon adds to its attack today (`combat.ts` unit build). Heals and shields are landed and gain nothing from it. One reading, no new number (section 4.2 item 9).
26. **The weapon pick:** three weapons rolled by price band (price at most 300 + 150 × depth + 1,000 × (s − 1); ×1.25 from an Elite, ×1.5 from a Boss) and rarity (`rewards.rarity`: a fight standard 75 / fine 25; Elite fine 60 / masterwork 35 / relic 5; Boss masterwork 70 / relic 25 / celestial 5), **capped by the grade ladder** (Leon, second batch, ruling 1; section 11.2): a roll above the stage's ceiling is offered at the ceiling (stage 1 Fine, stage 2 Masterwork, from stage 3 Relic, `grade_up.relic_from_stage`), so a pick never gives what the shop and the Forge could not yet sell; a picked Relic comes with its named property rolled from `relic_props` (rule 38), its worth on the boon cap (rule 31; a property that would pass the cap is not rolled and the weapon comes as Masterwork); **a Celestial roll is a Relic** (Celestial is Relic plus a quirk, and no quirk exists in data yet); **foci and mana arms count as weapons** (the Evoker, Emanator, Visualizer and Attuner at a better rarity; the core rifle, spellslinger pistol, Evoker blade, Emanator gauntlet, censer gun, thread banner, grand staff; the Emanator katana when it is in data); **one of the three is always for the character's own door or most-used weapon category**, so every class has something to want. **Or credits instead** (`instead_rate` 0.5 of the cheapest offer's price), so nobody takes junk. The armour-or-item pick (Elite, cache) works the same way from `items.json` armour, mods, tools and consumables. A pick taken is the character's own and may be given on later (rule 47).
27. **Prices** (one door, `priceOf(item, rarity)`): `price_credits` × `rules.json rarity.<r>.price`. Today **122 of 128** items and weapons have a price; missing: `city_id`, `bank_card`, `grimoire` (plot items: never stocked or sold) and `beast_bite`, `figment_claws`, `figment_edge` (`stock: false`). **To add:** a round price per calibre (`weapons.json calibres[].round_price`; section 7's table; the special rounds' `price_mult` reads it), a magazine's price (its rounds' + 10). **Selling** (Leon, 13 Nov): at `sell_rate` **0.25** of the price at the item's own rarity (`priceOf(item, rarity)`: a graded weapon sells for its grade, never for what its steps cost; its mods go with it); plot items and the unpriced can't be sold.
28. **The camp's loadout** (rules 1.52, replacing draft 4's starting kits and their "three spare magazines, enough for the first three fights"): creation carries no kit; at the camp each character takes one free weapon of `loadout.weapons` (melee, gun or bow; a gun loaded and with `loadout.gun.spare` 1 spare magazine, a bow with one quiver, read from its own magazine in `weapons.json`), one free focus of `loadout.focuses` (may be declined; a core-fuelled one brings `loadout.focus_with` one core), and spends `loadout.budget` 300 at `priceOf` on the shop's menu (`loadout.counter`: the counter's shelves but the restock; `loadout.stock`: a fixed starter list). Weight (the carry limit), the armour class's gate and a trait's cap, one body armour and one piece a slot hold, refused in words. Unspent budget is the purse. After node 1 the ammunition pack (rule 23) and the shop's counter keep a gunner fed (section 5.4).
29. **Carry between nodes:** HP, MP, ammunition, Strain and what else a fight leaves, Devotion and its XP, items, boons. A node is a new scene (`g.story.scene.n`): per-scene uses come back.

**Stages, boons and the world**
30. **The boon:** after each Boss every standing character picks **one of three boons** (section 3.5), rolled from the tile's boon pool; a boon is a **`relic` item** (the kind `effects/collect.ts` loads at run time with `addItems('relic', …)`; a character carries it in `c.relics`): the same thing the roguelike's relics and meta-progression unlocks will be. A boon lasts the run.
31. **Boon caps:** each boon is priced in percent of a rifle-turn (section 5.5); a character's boons add up to at most **`boon_cap` 50%**, **counting a Relic weapon's or focus's named property it carries, a shrine's token and a Crossroads relic** (section 11: one run-power budget, every source of it priced the same way); an offer that would pass it is replaced by a utility boon (no rifle-turn worth). No boon twice. Boons go past the balance ceilings on purpose (Leon, second batch, ruling 4); this cap is their bound.
32. **The next tile:** after the boon, two or three **world tiles** are offered (`world.offer` 3), each a setting not yet played this run where possible, showing its name, backdrop, Boss portrait and its **boon pool's theme**; picked like a node. The world is a row of tiles the party walks (`g.run.world: [{id, setting, stage, links, boons}]`), shaped as the roguelike's world map later: a tile is a node of a larger graph, with a setting and a pool, to which the roguelike adds points of interest and dungeons.
33. **A Narrator is optional flavour.** With an AI or human Narrator in the room, entering a node or a tile asks it for one to three sentences (kind, setting, template, foes given; no check, no number, no voice hook); shopkeeper and Boss lines are data first. The request never blocks play. Without one, the data lines carry the mode.
34. **Classes 3.0 test bed:** each of the nine classes is playable from node 1 (rule 28), restocked at every shop (rule 25); class at level 3 at about depth 3, rank II (level 6) at about depth 13 of stage 1 on the reference path (section 5.4), rank III (level 12) in stage 3.
35. **The summary and Report a run:** the summary shows stages cleared, nodes, the path, each fight's rounds, kills, Downs, levels, classes and boons. **Report a run:** the "Report a problem" bundle (`apps/web/src/game/report.ts`) with a `run` part: seed, stage tiles, builds at start and end, boons, gives between players, and per fight: node, stage, kind, setting, template, budget asked and spent, foes (tier, level, kind, weapon, caster, scale), rounds, outcome, per unit damage dealt and taken by system (weapon, Sigil, Outburst, prayer, Figment, Bond, Shadow, other), heals and shields, Downs, deaths, rounds fired: from a fight tally on the fx stream (section 4.2), kept in `g.run.stats`.
36. **State and saves:** `g.run` = `{seed, stage, world, nodes, at, path, state, leans, ready, offers, shop, stats, out, renown}`; every change is a `run/*` action (`run/start`, `run/lean`, `run/go`, `run/pick`, `run/buy`, `run/sell`, `run/fit`, `run/restock`, `run/upgrade`, `run/rest`, `run/train`, `run/shrine`, `run/trade`, `run/leave`, `run/boon`, `run/tile`, `run/retire`) or a `gear/give` (rule 47); the event log is the save; a room left and loaded resumes at the same node. Story and Narrator actions are refused as in the Proving Ground, except the flavour turn, `levelup/*` and `gear/*`.

## 5. Balance

### 5.1 The unit, from the contract
At level 1 (ratios hold at every level under the level scale): the rifleman kills a Soldier in **2.01** rifle-turns, an Elite in **4.26**, a Boss in **15.9**; a Soldier kills him in **7.84** turns, an Elite in **3.54**, a Boss in **2.64**. So an Elite's bulk is 4.26 / 2.01 = **2.1**, pressure 7.84 / 3.54 = **2.2**; a Boss's **7.9** and **3.0**. A Minion (70 HP, no armour, Defense 10) takes 2 of the plain hit's 45 against a Soldier's 3.5 of 40 and is hit 5% more often: **0.55**; it hits 55% with weapons averaging about 21 against the Soldier's 23.4 at 65%: **0.75**. A Vermin (15 HP, one hit) **0.3**; its bite 9 at about 45%: **0.3**. **Rounds from bulk:** the reference fights for four: standard (3 Soldiers, 1 Elite) bulk 5.1, 4.3 to 6.0 rounds; elite (2 and 2) 6.2, about 6.5; boss (1 Boss, 2 Soldiers) 9.9, 6.3 to 10.4: bulk ÷ (4 × rounds) = 0.25, 0.24, 0.29: **0.25**. **Pressure:** the standard fight for four brings 5.2 SE: **1.3 a character**, where the plain rifleman lasts about 3 rounds under focus; solo 1.2 (nobody to Raise).

### 5.2 The lag, priced
On the settled curve one level of gap is HP and damage ×0.899 and one point to hit each way (the party's hits ×1.077, the foe's ×0.923): bulk 0.899 / 1.077 = 0.835, pressure 0.899 × 0.923 = 0.830: **0.83** a level (0.69 for two, 0.57 for three); a level above: ×1.2. A test recomputes it from `enemies.json levels.table` and fails past a 3% drift.

A boon is priced like any item (`balance-contract.md` section 5): its share of a rifle-turn, all in. **The cap is 50%** a character (rule 31): four or five boons; a normal-share build at its ceiling plus 50% sits at about the Sigil Line's peak (150 to 200 is a different system's ceiling). The run must outpace them and the gear (section 11.6: about +35% bulk and +15% pressure a stage against about +10% a boon and up to +8% a grade step): **target, a squad of four ends in stage 3 or 4, solo in stage 2 or 3** (Leon accepted it, 13 Nov; measured, below). **Boons go over the balance ceilings on purpose** (Leon, second batch, ruling 4): they are run power, like a Miracle, exempt from the contract's guard and held by the survival test and the cap. The contract must say so before Survival ships (the line in section 11.6 item 5; a build to-do, section 10 step 9).

### 5.6 Measured before it ships

## 9. Out of the first version, on purpose; and what it must not block

**Out:** designed rooms; the five dungeons; the roguelike's full world map (Survival's row of tiles is its seed); missions; per-player meta progression and unlocks (an identity that outlives a room); companions and dialogue; account cosmetics; trapped caches; difficulty settings; body armour as items (Equipment 2.0); a respec or perk-swap service (no primitive yet; question 9); a two-sided trade offer with confirm (the plain give is enough among friends; question 15). (Draft 2's "no rest or train" is reversed: section 11.) **Must not block:** the node map as a dungeon (nodes are `{id, depth, col, kind, setting, links, room: null}`: a node can hold a designed room); rooms attached to nodes (the generator takes a room's slots in place of a template's); the shop NPC (the `faces` data: bias, prices, lines, portrait); settings as data (a row and a kit folder); the world map (tiles are nodes of a graph with a setting and a pool); relics and unlocks (boons are relics; `run_ammo` and `run_credits` are stats); a trade between strangers (the give's predicate takes a confirm later).

## 11. In-run progression (draft 3, spread over nodes in draft 4; Leon's ruling 7, and ruling 7 of the second batch)

Leon: *"progression is a thing, right now we only reach 'refill to continue', nothing really progresses except for level."* He is right about draft 2: a rest only healed, a shop mostly refilled, and the only lasting gains were levels, a weapon pick and a boon a stage. This section gives a run **six axes that grow, each paid for in its own way**, so that every node leaves something that lasts. Draft 3 still bundled most of them into the shop and the rest; Leon's second ruling (*"The progression systems are spread out into special nodes in the map so that you actually have choices each turn."*) gives **each axis its own node kind**, and the generator (rule 9) puts two or three kinds side by side at nearly every step, so the path itself is the progression choice. Every number here is **PROVISIONAL** and listed in section 11.7.

| Axis | What grows | Paid with | Its node (and where else) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Level | everything the level scale gives (as today) | XP from fights | Fight, Elite, Boss (a cache a little) |
| Grade | a weapon's or focus's rarity: Standard, Fine, Masterwork, Relic | **credits, a lot** (Leon's ruling 7) or a chit | **Forge** (80%, and the only Relic step; rule 44); the shop at full price (rule 37) |
| Kit | mods, worn armour pieces, the armour class | credits, or a pick | **Forge** (rule 44); cache and Elite picks (rule 41) |
| Training | drill, study, practice; Devotion | the node (no fight there) or 300 credits for a second | **Training ground** (rule 45); the rest a small one (rule 39); the **Shrine** for Devotion (rule 46) |
| Trades | a grade step, drill, a relic, an Elite-rarity pick | a cost the engine can take: credits, HP now, durability, max HP | **Crossroads** (rule 42) |
| Boons | a rule-changer for the run | beating a Boss; a small one at a shrine | Boss (rules 30, 31); the **Shrine**'s token (rule 46) |

The **shop** is the always-there node: refills, rolled weapons and items, selling, gives, and the grade step at its full price (Leon: *"in the shop you can upgrade the grade of your weapon with many money"*). The **Forge** is where growth is cheaper and the Relic is made; choosing it over a fight costs that fight's XP and credits, which is the choice.

### What other games do (the research behind this section)

A design note (the game names are for the design discussion only; no game text names them). The research came from wikis, guides, reviews and interviews; most shop prices in it are community figures and **unverified**, so we take shapes, never numbers.

| Game | What grows in a run | What we take | What we avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slay the Spire | the deck (picks, removal), card upgrades at rests, relics, max HP | a rest that gives a lasting gain; Elites as the opt-in risk for better rewards; an escalating price on a repeated service | heal **or** upgrade: in a party a skipped heal loses the run, so our heal stays free; gold left unspent at the end |
| Monster Train | a few units stacked with upgrades bought for gold | grading the one weapon you carry, for money: the closest precedent to Leon's ask | one carry hogging everything: our purses are per character and the step is one a visit |
| Wildfrost | companions, charms, a per-visit climbing price (45, 65, 85, reset each visit) | a per-visit limit, so purchases spread across the run | "strictly beneficial" events with no tension: ours each have a cost, but for one small free one a stage |
| Darkest Dungeon II | skill upgrades bought with a currency earned by fighting, one per skill per run | a capped, deliberate upgrade with a visible owner | one always-best spend (guides rank Mastery above trinkets) |
| Across the Obelisk | deck, gear, boons, card upgrades for a second currency | free upgrades found on the map (our armourer's chit) | two currencies to track in a co-op room: we keep one, credits, and one rare item |

| Risk of Rain 2 | uncapped item stacks; enemy strength and chest prices on one clock | one stage number driving difficulty and income together (`stage.bulk`, `stage.credits`) | uncapped stacking and late snowball |
| Dead Cells | stats by scroll, gear level and quality (+, ++, S), a forge for gold | a quality step bought at the forge, locked behind the previous one | flat prices that drift out of step with income (it moved to level-scaled costs) |
| Gordian Quest | a skill grid, gear; shrines that raise risk for reward | node rewards visible; risk as a choice | gold that only buys potions (the "refill" trap Leon names) |

### 11.2 The grade ladder (rules 37 and 38)

37. **The grade step (Leon's ruling 7).** At any **shop** or **Forge**, a character may raise **one weapon or focus it carries** (hand or pack) **one rarity step**: at a shop to Fine or Masterwork for the full `grade_up.price` of the grade it reaches; at a Forge for that price × `forge.rate` **0.8**, and the Relic step (rule 38) only there; **one step per character per visit** (`grade_up.per_visit` 1). The item keeps its mods and name (drill is kept anyway: it is per weapon id, `phys.drill[weaponId]`); a weapon's durability rises with the grade (`RARITY.dur`) and is restored to full (a focus has no durability). Foci and mana arms are graded the same way. Armour, shields and consumables are not graded (rarity does nothing to them today).
37a. **Repair (rules 1.50).** At any **shop** or **Forge**, a character brings **one carried weapon or shield** (hand or pack) **back to full durability** for max(`repair.floor` 10, `repair.rate` 0.1 × `priceOf` of the item at its grade × the share missing) × the place's factor (`repair.places`: shop 1, Forge 0.8), rounded up: the Core Rules' gunsmith (Weapons v2, Durability rule 4) priced by the wear. No limit a visit; a focus and armour do not wear. Refused in words (not here, nothing there, does not wear, already full, short N credits). On screen: the shop's "Repair and upgrade" tab and the Forge's Upgrade tab, a Repair shelf of cards (the durability bar now → full, the price, the reason); wear bars and a ⚠ WORN / ✕ BROKEN mark (at `rules.json weapons_rules.worn.share` 0.25 and 0) on the places' "you" panel and the path map's squad panel. *Why:* guns wore out in stages 3 to 4 with no way back but a grade step (rules 1.49 item 3609); a repair is upkeep, not growth, so it is cheap (a Standard rifle at 15% for 77, a Broken Masterwork one 720) and measured at under 0.2% of a stage's income.
38. **The Relic step.** From stage 3 (`relic_from_stage`), **at a Forge** (rule 44), a Masterwork weapon or focus may become a **Relic** for `grade_up.price.relic` **6,500** (Leon, second batch, ruling 2; draft 3 had 9,000; the Forge's 0.8 does not apply to it): Masterwork's numbers and **one named property, picked from three** rolled from `relic_props` for its category or door (the Core Rules' Relic: "Masterwork, plus one named property, written per item"). The property's worth comes off the carrier's boon cap (rule 31); a property that would pass the cap is not offered. It works while that weapon or focus is in a hand, and stays on the item when it is given (rule 47). **Celestial is never sold** (Core Rules: not for sale).

**What a grade does** is what `rules.json rarity` says today; nothing is added:

| Step | Price at a shop · at a Forge | What changes (`rules.json rarity`) | Share of income |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard → Fine | **1,500** · **1,200** | +1 accuracy, durability ×1.25 | 50% · 40% of stage 1 (÷ 3,010) |
| Fine → Masterwork | **3,500** · **2,800** | +2 accuracy in all (one more), durability ×1.5 | 91% · 73% of stage 2 (÷ 3,861) |
| Masterwork → Relic (from stage 3; a Forge only) | **6,500** | the same accuracy and durability, and one named property | 61% of stages 3 and 4 together (6,500 ÷ 10,725); 111% of stage 4 alone |

Where it counts: a weapon's grade on its main-hand attacks (`combat.ts` unit build reads `weaponState.rarity`, as today); a focus's in either hand (rule 25a). Rarity buys **no damage** (its `dmg` is 1 at every grade since rules 1.26). The Core Rules table's "+1 mod slot" is not read by the engine (`fitMod` fits one mod in each slot the weapon lists, whatever its grade); Survival keeps that (question 8).

**The ladder against income**, walked node by node on the reference path (shops at depths 5 and 12, the Forge at 11; upkeep half at each shop), a gunner buying nothing but steps: **Fine at stage 1's Forge** (1,710 in the purse at depth 11; at the shop's full price, at stage 1's second shop with 10 to spare); **Masterwork at stage 2's Forge** (3,561 at depth 11; at full price, stage 3's first shop); **the Relic step at stage 4's first Forge** (stage 3 saved whole leaves about 6,380 at its end, so any Forge of stage 4 from row 3; the 11.4 gunner, who buys mods and pieces in stage 3, reaches it at a Forge after stage 4's gate). So Leon's 6,500 lands the Relic in stage 4, inside the squad target (stage 3 or 4), where 9,000 put it at stage 5's first shop. Each step is about 2.3 times the last, the steep, published ladder the research recommends. **The choice:** stage 1's Forge visit spends 1,200 of a 1,710 purse on the grade, or the same on armour class 4 and mods; stage 2's Masterwork step takes nearly everything; a hybrid (a gun and a focus) wants four steps by stage 3 and can buy two or three; and **a path without the Forge pays a fifth more for each step at a shop**, so taking the Forge is worth a fight's credits (rule 9 puts one on every path; a second costs a node). The per-visit limit and the three or four shops and Forges on a path allow at most four steps a stage; credits allow about one.

**Picks climb the same ladder** (rule 26; Leon, second batch, ruling 1): the gate Elite's and the Boss's weapon picks are capped at the stage's grade (Fine in stage 1, Masterwork in stage 2, Relic from stage 3), and one of each three is for the character's door or weapon category, so a character who will change weapons reaches each grade about as early by picks as by credits. The step is for keeping the weapon you have: its mods, its name and, when the pick is another weapon id, its drill.

**Step or buy graded:** the shop sells a Fine weapon at 3 × its price and a Masterwork at 8 × (rule 27). For a weapon you already hold at price p, the step is the cheaper way to Fine when 3p > 1,500 at a shop (p over 500) or 3p > 1,200 at a Forge (p over 400); to Masterwork from Standard when 8p > 5,000 (p over 625) or 8p > 4,000 at a Forge (p over 500); from Fine when 8p > 3,500 (p over 437) or 8p > 2,800 (p over 350); selling the old one at 25% of its grade's price (rule 27) moves these a little. Rifles, foci (1,000), heavy weapons and most mana arms are cheaper stepped; a pistol (250) or a knife (60) is cheaper bought graded. Drill is per weapon id, so a graded copy of the same weapon keeps it, and the real decision is: grade the weapon you have, or buy a graded different one and start its drill over.

39. **The small training at a rest.** Each character takes **one**: **Drill** `train.drill` **100** on a weapon it carries (`addDrill`; marks at 300, 900 and 1,800: a third of mark I; not multiplied by the martial ×3, which is the kill path's); **Study** `train.study` **3** (a Sigil user: `addStudy`; a word at 6, then every 8: about two Evokes' worth); **Practice** `train.practice` **5** (an Outburst user: `addPractice`, section 4.2 item 12; a module at 5 and 15, then every 15: about two Releases' worth); **Devotion** `train.devotion` **2** XP (a Faithful character: `shiftDevotion`; a rest begins a session, so the session cap is fresh). A door with no growth counter yet (Bonds, Figments, Shadow) takes drill or Devotion until its own exists. *Why not heal or train* (Slay the Spire's fork): in a party a skipped heal is a lost run and an unhealed friend is a lost co-op evening; the heal stays free and the training is what lasts. The big trainings are the training ground's (rule 45), so the rest stays the place to heal with a little on top.
40. **Mods** are the second purchase after the grade: the Forge's rolled stock (rule 44), and the Elite's and cache's lasting picks (rule 41). They fit as today: one in each slot the weapon lists (`fitMod`, `mod_slots`; a rifle has up to four, a melee weapon two, a focus none), out of a fight, anywhere. Prices 80 to 900 (`items.json`).
41. **Lasting picks.** An Elite's and a cache's armour-or-item three hold at least `lasting_min` **2** lasting things: a mod for a slot the character's main weapon has free, or a worn piece (`items.json` armour: helmet, plates, greaves, gloves, boots) better than what it wears there; never two consumables. With no free slot and nothing better to wear, the rule falls back to the plain roll.
42. **Events** (the node card says "Crossroads" *(Suggested)*): two or three trades rolled from `events`, each a cost and a lasting gain, both the engine's to take and give (the wayside shrine alone is free, once a stage, and small); **each character takes at most one, or walks on**. ### 11.6 Gear in the difficulty curve

**A fully upgraded character against a bare one at the same level, under the balance contract:**

1. **Grade.** Rarity buys accuracy only (`rules.json rarity`: `acc` 0 / 1 / 2 / 2 / 2, `dmg` 1 at every grade since rules 1.26). One point to hit is ×1.077 on the party's hits (section 5.2, from the settled curve), so **Masterwork is ×1.077² = ×1.16**: a Masterwork plain rifle all in is 125 × 1.16 = 145, the best gun's ceiling. **The ceilings already hold Masterwork:** the contract's best gun and best blade are measured at Masterwork (`test/balance/builds.ts` `opt_gun` and `opt_blade`, `rarity: 'masterwork'`; `guns-measure.ts` defaults to it), and Relic and Celestial add no accuracy past it. **So a graded weapon reaches the best-gun (or melee) ceiling and never passes it**; grading only brings a Standard weapon up to where the ceiling was measured. (The contract's dial cell still says "Masterwork's damage"; rules 1.26 made it ×1.0, so the gun's dial is now its accuracy and each weapon's damage.) A Masterwork focus (rule 25a) gives a Sigil's or an Outburst's hits the same ×1.16 over what the contract measured with a Standard focus (question 5).
2. **Relic.** No accuracy past Masterwork; its property is run power (up to 10%), inside the boon cap.
3. **Mods** are not held by the contract yet (its section 7); estimated up to ×1.05 (a compensator or foregrip on Bursts, a scope on aimed shots); the simulated run measures them.
4. **Armour.** Fitting and worn pieces subtract a flat value from each hit (`ARMOUR` 5 a class, `landed` in `effects/output.ts`). Against a Soldier's hit of about 38 before armour at level 1 (23.4 landed through class 3's 15), class 4 is **−21%** taken; at level 7 (the hit ×1.1119⁶ = ×1.89, about 72) **−9%**; at level 16 (×4.9, about 189) **−3%**. Class 4 also costs one point of dodge (`ARMOUR[4].dodge` −1, felt only where Athletics gives a dodge point), about +8% hits taken, so for such a character it is a gain only early in stage 1. Armour carries stage 1 and fades by design of a flat value against a level-scaled hit, until Equipment 2.0.
5. **In all:** a fully upgraded character deals **×1.16 to ×1.22** a bare one's (grade and mods), **×1.28 to ×1.34** with a Relic property, and at most **×1.16 × 1.05 × 1.5 = ×1.83** with a full boon cap (Relic property and tokens inside it); draft 2 counted ×1.5 (boons alone). ### 13.2 Rules

48. **The starting boon.** At the camp, after `run/start` and before node 1, **every character picks one starting boon of three** (`start_boon.offer` 3), solo and co-op alike. The path map waits until every connected player has picked (each picks for its own character; the marks show live, as the boon pick after a Boss); the host may skip an away player, whose character then takes **the first card of its offer**, so nobody walks out of the camp without one. A starting boon is a `relic` item, carried in `c.relics` for the run like every boon, and its `worth` counts on the run cap (rule 31): a starting boon is worth at most `start_boon.max_worth` **6**, so it takes at most 12% of the 50 and leaves room for three or four Boss boons. The starting boons are their own pool (`start_boons`): never offered after a Boss, at a shrine or by the graft, and a Boss boon is never a starting boon, so the two never repeat. A run started from a level 1 draft is the same (Survival starts at level 1, rule 2).
49. **The offer, by the character's line.** A **line** is what a character runs on; there are nine, one for each attribute's alpha skill: **Guard** (Durability), **Momentum and blades** (Raw Power), **Pressure and guns** (Dexterity), **Shadow** (Ingenuity), **Sigil** (Brain Power), **Figments** (Visualization), **Bonds and Tending** (Empathy), **Outburst** (Will Power), **Faith** (Faith) (names *(Suggested)*; `start_boon.lines` maps each alpha skill to its line). No class exists yet at level 1 and the archetype is not saved, so the engine reads the sheet and the hands:
    - **The main line:** (a) a focus held in a hand whose door skill has 1 point or more gives its door's line (Evoker Sigil, Emanator Outburst, Attuner Bonds and Tending, Visualizer Figments); (b) else the line of the highest attribute's alpha skill, if the character has 1 point or more in it (a tie of attributes: the alpha skill with more points, then the order above); (c) else the main-hand weapon's line (a gun: guns; a blade or other melee weapon: blades; empty hands: guns).
    - **The second line:** the main-hand weapon's line if it differs from the main line; else rule (b)'s line if it differs; else the other weapon line.
    - **The three cards:** `start_boon.main` **2** drawn from the main line and `start_boon.second` **1** from the second, never the same row twice, from `rngFor(seed, 'camp#start#' + character id)`. So a Sigilist with a pistol is offered two Sigil boons and one gun boon; a rifleman two gun boons and one from its next attribute's line; a Channeler two Outburst boons and one gun boon (its revolver).
50. **The starting pool** (section 13.3): two or three rows a line, 24 in all; every row is **one number**, built as a `relic` on the closed lists of item ops, worth **4 to 6** (percent of a rifle-turn, section 5.5). **The shape is the same on every damage line** (guns, blades, Sigil, Outburst): one row of +1 to hit with the line's door (worth 6), one of +5% damage with it (5) and one of tempo or sustain (4). So no line's offer is strictly better than another's: a caster's best card is worth what a gunner's is, and the three cards on any offer sit within 2 worth of each other. The caster rows aim at what the measure found (misses, slow Lines, MP); what refills between fights is the supply pack's (rule 52), so no starting boon spends the pick on upkeep.
51. **Caster boons after a Boss** (section 13.4): six rows join `boons` (spec 3.5), each with `doors` (rule 30's offer serves only a character with a point in one of them) and the pools named; bigger than a starting boon (worth 8 to 13), on the same cap. **Bandolier** (spec 3.5) now doubles the whole supply pack: magazines, cores and the MP share (rule 52).
52. **The supply pack** (it replaces rule 23's ammunition pack; the reward screen says "Supplies"). After every won fight, and at a cache and the vault as rule 23 says, each character takes, by what it runs on, with **n** = `ammo_pack` by the node (a fight 1, an Elite, gate or vault guard 2, a Boss 3, a cache or the vault 1) × the stat `run_ammo` (Bandolier ×2):
    - (a) **Guns:** n magazines' worth for every gun carried (as rule 23).
    - (b) **Cores** (an Evoker or a Visualizer carried, `ammo_pack.core_doors`): **the cores it burnt in the fight, back**, at least `ammo_pack.cores` **1** × n and at most `ammo_pack.cores_max` **2** × n. Burnt = the charge it held before the fight (spare cores × 10 + the loaded charge of each focus) minus what it holds after, divided by 10, rounded up, never below 0. (Today 1 core whatever the node and whatever it burnt.)
    - (c) **MP** (an Emanator or an Attuner carried): `ammo_pack.mp_share` **0.3** × n of its max MP back, never above the max. (Today none.)
    - (d) **Flux** vents to 0 at every fight's end, as Strain resets (today Flux carries until a rest). **Charge at a rest:** a Rest node also fills each carried Evoker's and Visualizer's loaded charge to full (10), as it fills MP (today nothing restores it but a swapped core).
    - (e) **The rest, unchanged and the same for every build:** HP (carried; rests heal), Strain (0 after a fight), the meters (Guard, Momentum, Pressure, Shadow: each fight fresh), prayer waits (each fight fresh), Echo (1 a session), toxins and Supplies (restocked at a rest and at the counter), stims, field kits and spare parts (the counter).
    - *Why these numbers:* a gunner's magazine is about a fight's shooting (section 5.4); a Sigil Line every two turns burns 2 to 3.5 cores in a measured 5 to 9 round fight, so "what you burnt, 1 to 2 a fight" is a magazine's equal; 0.3 of max MP is about what a measured Channeler fight spends (118 MP; 41 for a full Begin, three Pumps and a Release). Upkeep (section 11.1) falls for a caster: the counter's cores are for a bad stretch, not for every fight.
53. *(Rules 1.52, Leon's loadout ruling (the "Settled by Leon" block on the loadout) supersedes this rule's kit parts: there are no kits in Survival, so no `rigger` or `mender` kit and no kit magazines; a character's armour is bought from the camp's budget. Reconciled (the camp's weapons block above): no free fitting survives beside the budget; the camp's order is the loadout, then the starting boon, then node 1.)* **The camp's issue** (no build pays for what another's kit gives free). At the camp, beside the starting boon, **each character may have its armour fitted up to class 3 at no cost** (`camp.issue_armour` 3), the soldier kit's class, through the Forge's fitting (rule 44) at price 0, once, before node 1; it may keep a lighter class (class 3 needs no Pain Threshold and costs half a tile of Movement). **Rule 28's two missing kits are built** in this step: `rigger` (a Visualizer, the service pistol, 4 standard cores) and `mender` (an Attuner, the service pistol, a field kit), so a Rigger and a Mender hold their focus at node 1 instead of finding a 1,000-credit one; and **every kit carries three spare magazines** for its gun (rule 28, unbuilt until now: the sigil, medic and soul kits carry two).
54. **Primed's caster doors are struck** (spec 3.5): with Flux at 0 and Strain at 0 when every fight begins (rule 52 d), "Flux −2" and "Strain −1" would do nothing. Primed keeps Guard, Momentum and Shadow as built; Pressure and the free Bond stay open (step 5's open item 1). The casters' fight-start help is Ward of Script (rule 51).

### 13.6 The simulator's caster policies (`test/balance/policies.ts`, `survival-run.ts`): what a sensible caster does each turn

Fixed before any rule above is measured, so each lever's share can be seen (section 13.7's order). Every choice is still a player's; every number the engine's.
