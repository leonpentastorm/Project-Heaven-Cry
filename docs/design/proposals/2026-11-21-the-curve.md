# Effort and consequence: PHC's own growth system (the lead's design, 21 Nov 2026, third draft)

**The motto (Leon): effort is always rewarded, and actions have consequences.**

For Leon to tear into. **Nothing here is built.** It replaces the second draft, which lifted other games' answers. The studies of D&D 5e, Baldur's Gate 3 and Pathfinder stay beside this file (`2026-11-21-study/`) as background; what PHC does today is in `2026-11-21-maps/`. Numbers below are first proposals to play, never lines.

## 1. What PHC has that those games do not

1. **Nine doors, each an engine with its own counter.** Guard's plates, Momentum's chain, Pressure's pips, Shadow's meter, the Sigil Line and its Flux, Figments and their charge, Bonds, the Outburst's stages and Strain, Devotion. Anyone opens a door through its skill; its class uses it better.
2. **Magic that mutates.** Forty words in Lines of two to four; an Outburst is a school, modules, a tone and up to twenty-five feelings. The number of spells is not a list.
3. **Mixed characters are real.** Two doors on one character is a build, not a mistake, and the advanced classes to come are pairs of attributes.
4. **Growth by doing already exists in pieces:** drill marks on a weapon, word study and mastery, feelings unlocked the first time they happen, modules learned by practice, Devotion earned by deeds, Achieved perks.
5. **Consequence already exists in pieces:** Flux and Strain, Heat and jams, the Miracle that costs Devotion, taboos and grudges, kills that raise the mana, Overreach's indulge or resist.

The old balance ignored all five and measured everything as a share of one rifle. This system is built out of them.

## 2. Effort is always rewarded: four laws

### Law 1. Depth: every point makes its engine bigger

A point in a door's skill never buys "+1 to hit and nothing else". It turns one of three dials, in a fixed rhythm up the ladder, so every point is felt:

- **Reach:** how much the engine can hold. More plates, a longer chain, a deeper Shadow, a longer Line, more Figments held, more Bonds, a higher stage, a higher Hymn.
- **Bite:** what one unit of the counter is worth when it is spent.
- **A verb:** a new way to build or to spend (the ladders already have these: Challenge, Grapple, Slide, Overclock, Cash in, Snap, Overdraw, Name the Sinner, the forks at 4 and 8).

The **attribute is the engine's size**: its card's promise (Strength +10% physical, Intelligence +10% Sigil power, and so on) applies to everything that door deals, and it sets how high the skill may go. The cards become true.

First proposal for Reach, by points in the door's skill (today most of these are the same number for everyone):

| Door | Counter | Reach at 1 | at 4 | at 7 | at 10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Guard | plates held | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 |
| Momentum | chain | x3 | x4 | x5 | x7 |
| Pressure | pips on a target | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 (and Broken lasts) |
| Shadow | meter | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| Sigils | glyphs in a Line | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| Figments | held at once | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Bonds | held at once | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Outbursts | stage | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| Prayer | by Devotion, as now | Whisper | Hymn I | Hymn II | Hymn III, the Miracle |

A 10 in a door is a different machine from a 5, not the same machine with a better die.

### Law 2. The cash-out: what you build, you spend, and building big pays best

Every door has a moment where the counter is spent: the Finisher, Slam, Break Them, the Break from Hidden, the Evoke, Cash in, Snap, the Release, the Hymn. **Spending more in one go always pays more than the same amount spent small.** First rule: each unit spent in one cash-out is worth a fifth more than the one before it (1, 1.2, 1.44, 1.73, 2.07, ...). So a x5 Finisher is worth about seven and a half plain steps, not five; a four-glyph Line beats two two-glyph Lines; a stage-4 Release beats two at stage 2.

That is the motto inside a turn: the player who sets up, holds, and picks the moment is paid for it, every time, with no pool or cap in the way. Reach (Law 1) is the only limit, and Reach is bought with points.

**The roll rewards mastery the same way: Margins.** PHC's target numbers climb in steps of four (8, 12, 16, 20). Proposal: **every full 4 you beat the Defense or the TN by is a Margin, and each Margin adds a quarter to what the roll does** (a natural 20 adds two). No skill point is wasted on a roll that "would have hit anyway": a master beats a weak foe by 8 and hits half again as hard; against an Elite the same roll just lands. This replaces the capped critical.

### Law 3. Practice: you get better at what you actually do

One rule for the whole game, built on what already exists: **everything a character uses earns Marks I, II and III by use**, shown as bars that are always filling.

| What | Earns marks by | A mark gives (first proposal) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A weapon (drill, exists) | kills and hits with it | I its signature technique; II and III a pick; III unlocks copying its Weapon Art (the planned system) |
| A door | cashing it out | I the door starts each fight primed with 1; II +1 Bite; III its Push costs nothing once a fight (Law 5) |
| A Sigil word (study, exists) | Evoking it in a Line | -charge, then a variant of the word, then it may sit twice in one Line |
| **A spell you found** | the first time a Line or an Outburst design you have never cast lands | it is written in your book, and study for its words. Magic's mutation becomes a collection: finding a new sentence is itself growth |
| A feeling (exists) | happening to you | unlocked; then its step grows; then it can be chosen, not only suffered |
| A Figment form | being held through a fight | hardier; a second order a turn; a trait slot |
| A prayer (Devotion, exists) | deeds your patron likes | as now |

This is what gives **satisfying growth at every stage for every character**: between two levels, six bars are moving, and they move because of how you played. A mixed character fills two doors' bars; a specialist fills one faster and reaches III sooner.

### Law 4. Crossings: two doors on one character connect

Any two doors a character holds are joined by one **Crossing**: a way to turn one counter into the other. Some exist already (Shield Bash turns a plate into Momentum; Share a Hit joins a Bond to Guard; Smetanita guns lay Pressure). Proposal: write all of them.

| Pair | Crossing (examples, to be written together) |
| --- | --- |
| Guard and Momentum | a plate spent on a blow adds a chain step: the wall that hits back |
| Pressure and Shadow | a shot from Hidden lays double pips; a Broken foe cannot spot you |
| Sigils and Outbursts | Flux you vent calms your Strain; an Outburst Released across your own Line takes its tone |
| Bonds and Guard | your plates soak for a Bonded friend at full value |
| Momentum and Prayer | a Finisher on a Judged foe is Witnessed |
| Figments and Sigils | a Figment can carry one glyph and stand as the Line's end |

Nine doors make **36 pairs**. With the nine doors doubled (the same attribute twice) that is 45: **exactly the advanced classes already planned** (the squared ones and the cross-core hybrids). So the system has room for them from the start:

- A **specialist** has Reach nobody else gets (Law 1).
- A **mixed character** has a Crossing no specialist gets, and two sets of bars.
- An **advanced class**, learned from a faction, takes one pair's Crossing and makes it a full engine with its own Evoker or Emanator. The squared kind deepens one door past 10's Reach; the cross kind marries two.

Neither path is the weak one. They are different machines.

## 3. Actions have consequences: two laws

### Law 5. The Push: you can always have more, and it always costs

Every engine can be **pushed past its Reach**, once a turn, for one more step, at a price that lands on your next turn or stays for the fight. Several exist (Overflux, Overclock, Overdraw, praying above your standing, a hot gun). Proposal: every door has one, named, with its price on the card:

| Door | The Push | Its price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guard | soak one blow past your last plate | an injury roll |
| Momentum | one chain step past your Reach | Off Balance until your next turn |
| Pressure | empty the magazine into one target | the gun is hot and may jam; you are Seen |
| Shadow | a second Break in one turn | you cannot Hide again this fight |
| Sigils | Overflux (exists) | the Flux you did not vent floods the air |
| Figments | hold one more than your Reach | it drinks charge every turn |
| Bonds | Snap every Bond at once | you feel half of it |
| Outbursts | Overdraw (exists) | Strain |
| Prayer | pray above your standing (exists) | Devotion |

It is Overreach, the rule the skills already have out of a fight (indulge and take the consequence, or resist and pay Echo), brought into the fight. Nobody is ever stuck with "I have nothing left": they have a choice with a cost.

### Law 6. The run remembers

What a squad does in one fight changes the next, in ways the map shows before you choose:

- **Mana.** Kills and vented Flux raise it (exists). A squad that burns magic walks into Rich air: stronger Outbursts for both sides.
- **Noise.** Loud weapons and long fights put the next node on alert: a harder ambush roll, a readied foe. Silent work keeps it asleep.
- **The patron.** Watches the tags (exists): liked deeds, taboos, grudges.
- **The body and the kit.** Injuries, wear, Heat, spent cores, an empty gun (your ruling) carry to the next fight until a rest.
- **The world's own levels.** An enemy's level comes from the stage it lives in, never from the party. Taking the long road makes you stronger than the stage; rushing makes you weaker than it. (This is the one piece taken from the studies, because it is what makes a level mean something: today Survival copies the party's level and the growth cancels.)

## 4. Gear in this system

- **A maker's line is a behaviour that feeds an engine**, as Items 2.0 already wrote: Smetanita lays Pressure, the Whisper line keeps a shooter Hidden, Hellraiser suppresses a line. The thirteen signatures left "shipping plain" are the point of the item system, not a leftover: each is a small Crossing between a weapon and a door.
- **A grade is how hard it does it:** Fine +1 to hit and x1.15, Masterwork +2 and x1.3 with a mod slot, Relic +3 and x1.5 with a property, Celestial +3 and x1.75 with two. Armour the same on its armour value.
- **A weapon earns marks** (Law 3), so a weapon you have carried for a stage is yours in a way a shop weapon is not.

## 5. What a character feels, and when

| When | What grows |
| --- | --- |
| Every roll | Margins: skill is never wasted |
| Every turn | the counter you built, and the choice to Push |
| Every fight | marks filling on what you used; a spell found |
| Every node | the run remembering; gear, a mod, a rest |
| Every level | a point: more Reach, more Bite, or a verb |
| Levels 3, 6, 12 | the class rank: your own door, used better |
| Every Boss | a boon of three: the run's big turn |
| Every stage | a higher grade can be found; the world one step meaner |

## 6. Enemies

They are built from the same engines (enemy Guard, Tag, Bonds and prayers exist since 0.33), with levels from the stage. A stage is made harder by what its fights are made of. When a stage reads too easy or too hard in play, **the stage changes; a player's reward is never taken back.**

## 7. Built from what the engine has

Reach and Bite are numbers on the door rows (data). The cash-out rule is one multiplier in the output formula where the counter is spent. Margins sit on the roll hook that marks a natural 20. Marks reuse the drill track's piece for every row that can be practised. A Crossing is the same kind of row as a maker's signature (one counter feeding another). The Push is a cost site with a consequence row. The run's memory uses the run state that already carries mana, wear and injuries. The stage level table is data in `survival.json`.

## 8. The order I would build it in

1. The world's own levels (Law 6, last point): levels start to mean something and no reward changes.
2. Depth: Reach and Bite by points, and the cards made true (Law 1).
3. The cash-out rule and Margins (Law 2).
4. Marks for doors and found spells (Law 3).
5. The Push for the doors that lack one (Law 5).
6. Crossings and the maker signatures, pair by pair, with you (Law 4).
7. Noise and the rest of the run's memory (Law 6).

## 10. Debate round 1: Leon's rulings (21 Nov 2026)

The frame, as ruled. Where this section and the text above disagree, this section wins.

1. **Four tiers of content** (one-shot, season, published campaign, epic): agreed.
2. **Attribute ceilings at levels 5, 11 and 17: refused** ("such random numbers, try again"). The lead's second proposal, not yet ruled: everything on fives (section 11).
3. **What "capped" means:** every system that grows power is at its maximum. Sigil words, Outburst modifiers and weapon drill are a grind, but never a whole 50-hour campaign for one weapon. **A capped character has gained about 80% of all the techniques, words and Outburst modifiers there are.**
4. **Survival's shape:** it **ends when all five maps are played**: a final boss, and a **permanent upgrade for the account** (when accounts exist). Then **endless** begins and keeps scaling until the squad dies or retires. **Tiers I and II are done at the final boss; after it comes tier III, the advanced classes' combat box.** The roguelike is timed so a character reaches **level 20 and then plays about 10 more hours at its peak**.
5. **Margins and the compounding cash-out: agreed.** A **mixed character uses more cash-outs for its AP, each weaker** than a character fully invested in one door. (So no "one big cash-out a turn across all doors" limit.) **The two kinds of advanced class are not two power levels:** the squared class and the hybrid have about the same power and express it differently.
6. **Advanced classes:** learned with **a special currency given out by that class's trainer**. Keep your reputation with them and you are one of their own and use the teaching as described. **Sully it and you keep the class but are damned: you use the teaching in a slightly different way** (the dark paladin).
7. **Nothing is built before the debate is done.**
