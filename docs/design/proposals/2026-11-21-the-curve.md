# The curve: a balance system for PHC, built the way D&D 5e and Pathfinder build theirs (the lead's design, 21 Nov 2026, second draft)

For Leon to tear into. **Nothing here is built.** It replaces the first draft of this file. It rests on two sets of checked material filed beside it: what PHC does today (`2026-11-21-maps/`) and how the four reference games do it (`2026-11-21-study/`: D&D 5e, Baldur's Gate 3, Pathfinder 1e with Kingmaker and Wrath of the Righteous, Pathfinder 2e; each brief fact-checked against the rules sites). Every PHC number below is a first proposal to play, never a line to fit to.

## 1. What those games do that we did not

| Game | Its answer | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **D&D 5e** | **Bounded accuracy.** The d20 bonus grows about +6 in 20 levels and enemy Armour Class grows with it: about 65% to hit an equal foe at every level. | Growth is put where it can be felt: hit points, damage, the number of attacks, new spell levels. A second attack at level 5 is +100% and everyone feels it; +5% is invisible. Low monsters stay dangerous in numbers. |
| **5e** | **Spikes at tier boundaries** (levels 5, 11, 17), every class on the same level. | Between spikes a level gives options, not numbers. |
| **5e** | **The monster table is built for an expected character**, and the game works with no magic items. | Gear is a bonus layer with a small cap (+3, three attuned items). |
| **Baldur's Gate 3** | **Items sell behaviour, not percentages.** A +1 is tiny; the item that matters adds a loop: do X, gain a stacking state, the state pays off. | Twelve levels feel big because of four or five jumps in kind. Difficulty comes from what a fight is made of, not from inflated stats (Tactician is only about +2 and +20 to 30% HP). |
| **Pathfinder 1e, Kingmaker, Wrath** | **Typed bonuses that do not stack.** Two bonuses of the same type: only the larger counts. Different types add. | This one rule lets each bonus be big and readable, and still lets the designer know the worst case. Also: the enemy numbers assume an expected set of gear by level. Wrath adds a second track of rare, large steps (mythic ranks) given by the story. |
| **Pathfinder 2e** | **Enemies have fixed levels, and the fight is budgeted by level difference.** A foe two levels under you is plainly weaker, two over is plainly dangerous. Beating the target by 10 is a critical, so every +1 counts twice. Weapon runes add whole damage dice at set levels. | Its known flaw is ours: when both sides rise together, numbers grow and the fight feels the same. It only feels like growth where the level gap is not zero. |

**Our mistake in their terms.** PHC already has 5e's bounded d20 (a skill gives -2 to +9) and Pathfinder 2e's level multiplier on damage and HP. But Survival sets an enemy's level from the party's, so the gap is always zero and the multiplier cancels; and every other reward was pinned to a share of one rifle. We kept the treadmill and removed everything that is felt.

## 2. The system for PHC: nine rules

### Rule 1. The roll stays bounded (5e)

The d20 is where a hit is decided, and it stays small: skill -2 to +9, a grade at most +3, the situation (cover, height, a condition) on top. An equal foe is hit about two times in three by someone trained. No level adds to hit. A skill point is +5% to hit and, with Rule 2, +5% to critical.

### Rule 2. Beat it by 10 and it is a critical (Pathfinder 2e)

Today only a natural 20 is a critical. Proposal: **a roll that beats the Defense or the TN by 10 or more is a critical too.** A master against a weak foe criticals often; against an Elite, rarely. This is the cheapest single change that makes every +1 felt, and it is what makes a 9 in a skill feel different from a 6. (The critical's size stays what the weapon and Perception say; the cap of x2 goes.)

### Rule 3. The world has levels of its own (Pathfinder 2e)

**An enemy's level comes from where it lives, never from the party.** In Survival, from the stage and the depth:

| Stage | Ordinary foes | Elite, gate | Boss | A squad that fights most nodes arrives at about |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 to 2 | 3 | 4 | level 3 to 4 |
| 2 | 3 to 5 | 6 | 7 | level 6 to 7 |
| 3 | 6 to 8 | 9 | 10 | level 9 to 10 |
| each stage after | +3 | +3 | +3 | +3 |

The engine already has the rest and it finally means something: each level of difference is +1 to hit for the higher side (up to 5), a foe 5 levels under you falls to any hit, and the level columns make a level about +11% damage and HP against a foe that did not level with you. Taking the long path and out-levelling a stage is a real choice; rushing under-levelled is a real risk. In a campaign the Narrator's places carry levels the same way.

### Rule 4. Levels give staying power and points; the big jumps come in kind, on set levels (5e, Baldur's Gate 3)

A level gives HP and points (as now). **Three levels are spikes for every class at once: 3, 6 and 12**, the class ranks, with a small one at 9 (the last attribute ceiling). A spike is something new to do or one more of something, never a percentage:

- **Level 3, rank I:** the class and its signature move (as now).
- **Level 6, rank II:** each class's "second attack": once a turn its own door's attack or cast costs 1 AP less (a Breaker's Finisher, a Skirmisher's burst, a Sigilist's Evoke, a Channeler's Release, and so on). In a 6 AP turn that is one more action: the 5e level-5 moment.
- **Level 9:** the attribute can reach 10; the skill's Mastered perk comes into reach.
- **Level 12, rank III:** the capstone move (as now), made to be worth the wait (Rule 7).

### Rule 5. Points keep their promise, in channels that do not stack with themselves (Pathfinder 1e)

Every bonus to damage or to a roll belongs to one of **nine channels**. Different channels add up (they multiply for damage). **Inside one channel only the largest applies.** That replaces the single +100% pool on target bonuses, the x2 critical cap and the "all-in is 1.25" line.

| Channel | What feeds it | First numbers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attribute | the attribute's own card | Strength +10% physical a point; Intelligence +10% Sigil; Expression +10% elemental; Determination +5% Soul; Insight +6% healing (built) |
| Skill | the alpha skill's card | Raw Power +5% heavy melee and bare hands a point; Dexterity +4% guns, light blades and thrown; the magic alphas as their cards say |
| Grade | the weapon or armour's grade | Rule 6 |
| Mod | fitted mods | one trade each |
| Perk | perks | the best one that applies: +15% at tier I, +30% at tier II while its condition holds |
| Class | class moves and passives | the move's own number |
| Faith | prayers, Witnessed | as their cards |
| Situation | the target's conditions, cover, height, flank | the best one |
| Boon | Survival's boons, trainings, shrine | the best one of each kind |

Because perks no longer pile on each other, each one can be big. A Strength 7 / Raw Power 7 Breaker swings a maul about twice as hard as a Strength 1 / Raw Power 1 character (today: a third harder). Guns stop reading Agility in secret; a gunner's damage is Dexterity and the gun.

### Rule 6. Gear: a grade is a step, a maker is a behaviour (Pathfinder 2e runes, Baldur's Gate 3 items)

**Grades are thresholds, like striking runes**, and the stage decides what can be found (as now: Fine in stage 1, Masterwork in 2, Relic from 3):

| Grade | To hit | Damage | Durability | Also |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | +0 | x1 | x1 | |
| Fine | +1 | x1.15 | x1.25 | |
| Masterwork | +2 | x1.3 | x1.5 | +1 mod slot |
| Relic | +3 | x1.5 | x2 | +1 mod slot, one Relic property |
| Celestial | +3 | x1.75 | x2.5 | +2 mod slots, two Relic properties |

Armour takes the same steps on its armour value. To hit stops at +3: the roll stays bounded.

**The maker's line is the item's behaviour.** This is the Baldur's Gate 3 lesson and Items 2.0 already wrote it down: Smetanita lays Pressure on a hit, Chezknov steadies Bursts, the Whisper line never gives a Hidden shooter away, Hellraiser suppresses a line. Thirteen of those signatures were left "shipping plain, primitive owed". They are not a leftover: **they are the item system's point**, and they hook into counters PHC already has (Momentum, Pressure, Guard, Shadow, Heat, Bonds, Strain). Proposal: build them as one general piece, a signature that feeds or reads one of those counters, and let Relic properties do the same. A weapon is then chosen for what it does with your door, and its grade is how hard it does it.

**Weapons inside a family differ by role**, at their designed numbers. Price buys handling and slots.

### Rule 7. A move must be worth pressing (all four)

A class move or technique whose set-up is met is worth about one and a half plain attacks of the same AP, in damage or in what it denies. Slam first (2 AP and 2 Guard for no damage beside Shield Bash). The class sheet is the list, gone through with you.

### Rule 8. The enemy table is built for an expected character, and fights are budgeted by level difference (5e, Pathfinder 1e and 2e)

**One table, enemy numbers by level and tier**, written for one stated attacker: *a specialist in their thing, in the grade their stage sells one step under its best.* Against that attacker an ordinary Soldier of the same level lasts about three to four hits and an Elite about twice that. Players then sit above or below it by their own choices, and nothing else in the game is pinned to it. (This is what the old "ceilings" were shorthand for: one table for the monsters, not a limit on every reward.)

**The budget counts level difference** (Pathfinder 2e's table, known for working), on top of the tiers Survival has:

| Enemy level minus squad level | -4 | -3 | -2 | -1 | 0 | +1 | +2 | +3 | +4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | 10 | 15 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 60 | 80 | 120 | 160 |

A fight is light at 15 a character, standard at 20, hard at 30, deadly at 40. Two foes of one level cost what one foe two levels higher costs. A stage gets harder by what its fights are made of (more bodies, casters, skulkers, a second wave, ground), not by adding to every number.

**When a stage reads too easy or too hard in play or in simulated runs, the stage changes.** A player's reward is not taken back.

### Rule 9. A second track of rare, large steps (Wrath of the Righteous)

Survival already has it: the boon of three after each Boss. Those become the run's big moments, one per stage, each changing how the squad plays, in their own channel. Trainings and shrines stay the small steps between.

## 3. Built from what the engine has

| Rule | Carried by | New |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | the roll and the skill modifier, as they are | nothing |
| 2 | the roll hook that already marks a natural 20 | one data number (the margin, 10) |
| 3 | `enemies.json levels.gap` (to hit per level, the 5-level rule), the level columns | Survival reads the enemy's level from the stage table in `survival.json`, not from the party |
| 4 | class ranks at 3, 6, 12 | the rank II "1 AP less, once a turn" as one cost-site row per class |
| 5 | the output formula's stat term and its bonus terms | the channel on each bonus row and "best in channel"; the profiles' slopes back in data |
| 6 | the rarity rows (Items 2.0), `variant_mods` signatures, the counters | the damage column; one general signature piece for the thirteen owed |
| 7 | class and technique rows | numbers |
| 8 | the enemy level table, `encounter.ts`'s budget | the cost by level difference as data |
| 9 | the boons | their channel |

## 4. The order

1. **Rule 3 and Rule 8's budget**: the world gets its own levels. This alone makes levelling felt and changes no reward.
2. **Rule 6's grades**, then **Rule 5** (channels and the cards made true).
3. **Rule 2** (critical by 10) and **Rule 4** (the rank II spike).
4. **The signatures** (Rule 6) and **Rule 7** with you, class by class.
5. Read Survival by stage and by class, and move the stages.

Each step is data plus one small engine change, re-records the damage tables on purpose, and can be switched back.

## 5. What I need from you

1. **Is this the system?** Bounded roll, a world with its own levels, spikes at 3 / 6 / 12, channels that do not stack with themselves, grades as steps and makers as behaviours, one enemy table for an expected character.
2. **Critical by 10:** yes or no. It changes the feel of every roll in the game.
3. **Rank II as "one action cheaper, once a turn":** is that the level-6 moment you want, or do you have each class's own in mind?
