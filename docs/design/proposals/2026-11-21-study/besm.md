# BESM / Tri-Stat: brief for PHC progression (fact-checked 21 Nov 2026)

Check marks used below: **[verified]** = read on the cited page; **[not found]** = could not retrieve; **[CORRECTED]** = the earlier brief was wrong.

## 0. Identification
**Most likely: Big Eyes, Small Mouth (BESM), Tri-Stat System** (confidence about 85%). It is the anime RPG with exactly three Stats: Body (physical), Mind (mental) and Soul (spiritual: will, luck, charisma). **[verified]** Dyskami's 4e preview says Combat Value is the average of Body, Mind and Soul, a "purposeful choice" reflecting what it calls a Eastern philosophy of harmony of the "Complete Self", and that balance was intentional from the start (http://dyskami.ca/downloads/BESM4_Kickstarter_Rules_Preview.pdf). Wikipedia lists Canadian-RPG categorisation and Dyskami's .ca site; publisher nationality is otherwise not stated there. (The brief's claim "the publishers are Canadian" is a reading from that, **not verified** on a page.)

Editions **[verified, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Eyes,_Small_Mouth]**:
- 1e 1997 (Guardians of Order, Mark C. MacKinnon). 1e stats: roll 2d6, add 10, split over Mind, Body, Soul.
- 2e 2001 (Guardians of Order; 2000 in some listings, not verified).
- BESM d20 2003, Revised 2004.
- 3e 2007 (ArtHaus Games, after Guardians of Order closed in 2006). The core engine was also released as Tri-Stat dX.
- 4e 2020 (Dyskami, Kickstarter, co-published with Japanime Games, retail October 2020).

**Runner-up, Japanese-made: Sword World 2.0/2.5** (Group SNE), about 10%.
- **[verified, https://gmbinder.com/share/-O9xxbHe0uFqFkdIEi5r]** A 2.5 primer says every character has Skill, Body and Mind, set by Background. A Sword World wiki says six numbers are added to the starting Body / Skill / Mind to give the ability scores (edition not stated).
- **[verified, fan wiki via search, https://swordworld.fandom.com/wiki/File:CSA.png]** SW2.0 lists six abilities: Dexterity, Agility, Strength, Vitality, Intellect, Spirit. The pairing of these under Skill / Body / Mind is from the rulebook as the earlier researcher knew it, **not verified** on a page.
- Mechanics from the earlier brief (modifier = score / 6; HP = level × 3 + Vitality; roll 2d6 + class level + modifier; double 6 auto-success, double 1 auto-fail): from the earlier fetch, not rechecked here.
- It is the 心技体 (mind / technique / body) triad, so no Soul and no point-buy. (reading)
- 2.0 was published in 2008 and 2.5 in 2018 (search summary of Wikipedia, https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sword_World_RPG).

**Other candidates, none known as a body/mind/soul core (all not verified):** Tenra Bansho Zero (five attributes, Kiai / Aiki / Kegare economy), Double Cross (Body / Sense / Mind / Social), Ryuutama (STR / DEX / INT / SPI), Golden Sky Stories, Arianrhod, Night Wizard, Alshard, Meikyuu Kingdom; OVA is US-made with no attributes. The first search for "anime tabletop RPG Body Mind Soul stats Japanese" returned Tri-Stat/BESM-related pages and Tenra Bansho Zero, nothing better.

The design point is that every Stat feeds everything:
- Combat ability comes from all three Stats, so a pure Mind character still fights.
- Health comes from Body and Soul.
- Energy comes from Mind and Soul.

## 2. Point-buy, Defects, growth, power levels

**Character Points (CP)** buy Stats, Attributes and (in 2e and 3e) Skills. 3e skills cost 1 to 3 points per level **[verified, https://rpgwithkaworu.wordpress.com/2022/10/02/besm-3rd-edition-a-review/]**. In Tri-Stat dX at least 2 CP must go into each Stat **[verified]**.

**Attributes** are bought in Levels 1–6, each with its own cost per Level (figures below from the earlier brief, **not rechecked**):
- 2e: Weapon Attack 1, Item of Power 2, Combat Mastery 2.
- Tri-Stat dX: Tough 2, Attack Combat Mastery 3, Defence Combat Mastery 2, Force Field 4, Healing 4, Extra Attacks 8, Own a Big Mecha 8, Dynamic Powers 10/15/20, Power Flux 5–20.
- 4e preview: Special Movement 1, Supersense 1, Summon Creatures 2, Superspeed 3.

**Defects** pay points back (not rechecked): 1e up to 3 Defects worth 1–2 each; dX mostly 1–3 Bonus Points each, no more than 8; 3e subtracted from cost; 4e Lesser and Greater Defects at −2 / −4 / −6.

**Advancement (flat costs: the same price as at creation):**
- **Tri-Stat dX [verified]:** the GM is encouraged to give one-quarter X (rounded up, X = die size) Advancement Character Points and Advancement Skill Points every two to five sessions. Points can be spent at once (GM's discretion) or banked. Cost is identical to creation. They can also buy off Defects. A new Attribute needs a story reason. The GM may also give an extra point for exceptional play and award more often for faster advancement.
- **Pushing Attributes (dX optional rule) [verified]:** spend 1 unused Advancement Point and make a Stat check. Success raises the Attribute 1 Level, or opens a new related use. On a failure the point is still allocated to the Attribute and there may be a mishap such as damage. More than one point may be spent at GM discretion.
- **3e:** the GM normally gives 1–5 Advancement CP a session (fan summary; not found on a primary page).
- **4e:** not verified.

## 3. The core roll
- **1e/2e:** roll 2d6 **under** the Stat, adjusted for difficulty. Attack and defence are 2d6 under Combat Value. Damage = Combat Value + weapon damage (not rechecked).
- **Tri-Stat dX [verified]:** roll 2dX at or under the Stat or Combat Value, one die size for the whole game.
  - A natural 2 ("snake eyes") is a critical success and cannot be negated by the defence; the defender gets no defence check. The rule may not apply in d4 games (GM's discretion). In the earlier brief: damage = 2 × maximum damage + Attack CV; the doubled-damage detail was not rechecked.
  - A natural 2X always fails (not rechecked).
  - Multiple attacks: each defence after the first takes a cumulative −4 (−4 second, −8 third, −12 fourth). **RIGHT.**
  - Damage by a percentage table (100 / 75 / 50 / 25% of maximum) plus Attack CV minus armour: not rechecked.
  - Width on d20: Stats reach 40 and the roll is 2–40 (follows from the table).
- **3e [verified, rpgwithkaworu]:** 2d6 + Stat + Skill **over** a TN (medium 12). Damage = weapon level × damage multiplier + Attack CV. Beating the defence by 12 or more doubles damage, by 18 or more triples it.
- **4e [verified, preview PDF]:** 2d6 + Stat, Combat Value or Attribute Level against a TN; the total must equal or beat the TN. The preview's table has seven named difficulties (Very Easy, Easy, Average, Difficult, Challenging, Unlikely, Improbable) with TNs 6 / 9 / 12 / 15 / 18 / 21 / 24; the PDF's table layout is garbled, and the text confirms Average = 12 (the label-to-number order is not verified beyond that).

    | Difficulty | Very Easy | Easy | Average | Difficult | Challenging | Unlikely | Improbable |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | TN | 6 | 9 | 12 | 15 | 18 | 21 | 24 |

    **Edges and Obstacles [verified]:** extra dice are added to the standard two six-sided dice and only the two highest (edge) or two lowest (obstacle) are used; the preview says difficulty modifiers are unified into this concept. The detail "3d6 or 4d6" and "two minor make a major; opposite kinds cancel" was **not** retrieved from the text seen (not verified; the rule itself is on a page the preview marks as "page ???").

## 4. Freeform powers
Powers are effects-based: one Attribute (Weapon, Force Field, Item, Power Flux, Dynamic Powers) can be magic, psionics or technology (reading, from the sources' framing).
- **3e:** Variables raise an Attribute's cost; Restrictions lower it (not rechecked).
- **Tri-Stat dX:** Restriction and Reduction Defects lower the cost. Force Field per-Ability and Disability protection modifier of ±20: not rechecked.
- **4e [verified in outline, preview PDF]:** a standard system of Enhancements and Limiters; Attribute entries list "counts as 2 Enhancements (Attribute effectiveness…)" and similar. The brief's rule that they change the Attribute's effective Level and never its Point cost is consistent with the PDF phrase "Attribute effectiveness" but the one-Enhancement = −1 effective Level rule was not retrieved verbatim (not verified). Weapons list modifiers inline, e.g. "Ki Blast (Drain: Body −1; Enervation −1; Hands +1; Range +1)": not rechecked.
- **Open-ended pools:** Power Flux (3e: 15 CP a Level for 4 points of temporary powers a Level) and Dynamic Powers (Tri-Stat dX: 10/15/20 CP a Level): not rechecked.

## 5. Energy Points
- (M+S)×5 in 3e **[verified]** and in Tri-Stat dX, where the rule is optional **[verified]**. Energy Bonus Attribute modifies the total **[verified]**; "+20 EP per Level for 2 CP" and "+5 for 2 (3e example)" not rechecked.
- **Uses [verified, dX]:** fuels Attributes with "Burns Energy" and Special Attacks with "Uses Energy". Combination Attack: 2 Energy Points per participant. At 0 EP the character falls unconscious from exhaustion. The text warns that Energy Points slow play and add bookkeeping.
- Recovery avg(M,S) per hour (dX): not rechecked.
- 3e emotional-intensity spending and 4e Energy rules: not verified.

## 6. Known balance problems
- **1e [verified, Wikipedia]:** a 1998 *Dragon* review (Lester Smith) says the largest weapon deals the most damage without penalty, so players would choose it, and combat can drag when both sides have strong defences. Extra Attacks capped at 6 actions and "Unique Attribute" as a catch-all: not rechecked.
- **2e:** roll-under favoured defenders and combat was long (forum players, not rechecked; the Wikipedia page does not call roll-under a flaw). Cheap Stats feeding every derived value: widely reported, not verified with a source.
- **3e (secondary, not rechecked this pass):** Stats at 10 CP a Level; Items "very, very abusable" (an Item costs half its built value); armour- and shield-piercing too cheap; builds over 300 CP "break the system"; Dynamic Powers open to abuse. Sources named earlier: EN World "Looking Back at BESM 3E" (https://www.enworld.org/threads/looking-back-at-besm-3e.664487/) and The Other Side blog (http://theotherside.timsbrannan.com/2010/05/anime-rpgs-besm-30.html); not retrieved this pass.
- **d20 edition [verified, Wikipedia]:** mixed critical response; the 2004 revision shipped without its full changes due to proofing and printing errors.
- **4e [verified, preview PDF]:** its own list of changes says it rebalanced Points for all Stat, Attribute and Defect costs while cutting 3e's totals, moved to roll-high from roll-low, unified difficulty modifiers into edges and obstacles, and (list line on Skill Groups; the earlier claim "replaced the separate Skill system with Skill Groups" is consistent with the preview's Skill Group entries) added Skill Groups.

## 7. Very different power levels in one party
(All of this section is from the earlier brief and was **not rechecked** this pass, except where noted.)
- The main tool is the shared tier plus Item and Companion budgets: a 4e Mythical "normal pilot plus huge mecha" counts its gear inside the total.
- The 4e Superhuman and Superpowered tiers say power can come from items or fate, not skill.
- Tri-Stat dX multi-target penalties scale by CP gap: none against targets 100+ CP weaker, −2 against 50+ weaker, −4 otherwise.
- Attribute against Attribute: the higher Level wins, or an opposed roll when close.
- Only player characters keep a slim guaranteed chance of success.
- No formal rule for scaling a mixed party; left to GM judgement and session zero. (reading)

## What it teaches PHC (all of this is the researcher's reading)
1. (reading) **Tiers by budget and ceiling, not by rule set.** Tri-Stat dX ties each tier to a point range and a hard Stat maximum, with a softer "talent threshold" below it that needs a story reason **[verified]**. PHC's one-shot / season / campaign / epic tiers could each be a pair, (starting level or XP budget, attribute ceiling), as PHC already does with 7 → 8/9/10 at levels 3/6/9. Keep the step between tiers about ×1.3–2, as 4e does (Human 25 → Heroic 100 → Godlike 250, **[verified]**), not 3e's ×10 (3e bands unverified).
2. (reading) **Flat costs plus regular grants make "effort is always rewarded" cheap to guarantee.** dX: the same price as at creation, ¼ die size every 2–5 sessions **[verified]**. 3e: 1–5 a session (unverified). Steady but flat. PHC's 50-hour target wants a mandatory pick at every level, which BESM never had: BESM shows the economy, not the pacing.
3. (reading) **Price freeform effects by effective Level, not by points (the 4e change).** For Sigils and Outbursts: each module or word that broadens the effect counts as −1 Level and each restriction as +1, with the price fixed. That keeps every design on one damage/cost ladder and allows a fit-by-measurement table. 3e's per-modifier cost changes produced its exploits (secondary sources).
4. (reading) **Watch the three classic exploits:** the stat that feeds every derived value (PHC: Agility feeds movement and initiative); gear priced below its built value (3e Items); and anything that ignores defence (3e armour-piercing). Each needs a ceiling written in data before it ships.
5. (reading) **Open pools (Power Flux, Dynamic Powers) were always the weak point.** If a feeling or a word makes a new effect in PHC, cap the total (BESM: effect Levels ≤ 2 × pool, none above pool Level: not rechecked) and hold it to the same tier ceiling.
6. (reading) **One shared Combat Value from all three Cores** matches the philosophy behind a Core showing its highest attribute only loosely: BESM's average **[verified]** is fairer to mixed builds; PHC's max rewards specialists. Choose one on purpose.
7. (reading) **Edges and Obstacles** (keep the best or worst 2 of 3–4 dice) **[verified in outline]** is a cleaner stand-in for stacked ± modifiers on a 2d6 curve. PHC's flat d20 doesn't need it, but it is the model if PHC ever adds advantage.
