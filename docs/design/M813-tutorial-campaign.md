# M813: the tutorial campaign

Design notes for `data/campaigns/m813.json`. M813 is PHC's first campaign and its tutorial, built on the lessons of Fallout: New Vegas's Goodsprings, the small town that teaches a whole RPG without ever feeling like a tutorial.

## What Goodsprings does, and what M813 takes from it

| Goodsprings | M813 |
|---|---|
| You wake up already in the story. The doctor tests you in fiction, not in menus. | You open as the guns stop. Sergeant Nine-Nine's first question ("How many of us can you carry?") is the whole campaign in one line. |
| A teacher (Sunny Smiles) walks you through shooting, sneaking and survival with tasks you'd want to do anyway. | Nine-Nine teaches like a sergeant: one instruction at a time, through things the medics want anyway (reach him, sort the wounded, cross no-man's-land, drive off the drones). |
| One system per beat, each optional and low-stakes. | Seven beats: voices, moving, points of interest, a first easy check, a small fight, Overreach, travel. Nothing on the shield line can kill a player character. |
| Every build has something to do: the town's problems open to Speech, Barter, Explosives, Medicine, Sneak and more. | Every one of the 27 skills has an approach in the first zone (table below), across Body, Mind and Soul. |
| The central conflict (the Powder Gangers) is prepared from many angles, and each recruit changes the showdown. | The ramp standoff on the rear pad has nine prepared angles (Keel, the platinum, a junior engineer, the hatch, the column, the fuel line), and every success changes the talk or the fight. |
| You can talk, trade, sneak or shoot your way through, and siding with the "bad guys" is allowed. | You can argue, bargain, threaten, sabotage or fight, and dumping the platinum or rigging the fuel line are allowed. |
| The town is where you learn to care, so the wasteland can threaten it. | You learn names on the shield line: Nine-Nine, Seven-Seven who counts, Hum who hums, Lantern with the child's drawing. The ramp, where the record says nineteen die, threatens exactly those names. |
| Soft walls steer you (cazadores to the north). | Exits lead east to the pad and north to the outpost; the ship's departure is the clock. |

## The story

1. **Beginning (session 1, the tutorial).** The shield line at dusk. Four medics of the 258th among twenty-three broken infantry robots. Nine-Nine teaches, the medics triage, and someone crosses mined no-man's-land for the fallen squad. Scavenger drones are the first fight. Then the column carries the wounded east to the last ship.
2. **Middle.** The rear pad. Chief Engineer Keel: "Not enough thrust for scrap. Shut yourselves down." The first real test, prepared from every angle. In the record, the engineers seal the hatch and kill two medics and nineteen infantry; the players can change it, at a cost either way. Left behind, the survivors rebuild an escape craft in Outpost-813's hangar from the fallen over years told in montage, and face the record's choice: who is rebuilt, and who becomes the parts.
3. **End.** Outpost-1 and Colonel Alexei Novikov. The demand: equality, and one law: a broken robot gets an arm to repair itself (Nine-Nine said it first: "If I had an arm, I'd fix myself and you could go"). The repair bay faces the black hole.
