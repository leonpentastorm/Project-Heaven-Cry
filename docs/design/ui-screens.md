# UI screens (prototype)

Prototypes live in `design/canvas/` as Design Component files (`.dc.html`), the format of the claude.ai design canvas: <https://claude.ai/artifact/9kVVNppZERUaCxBCMWbZgM>. **Prototype v2** (branch `ui-prototype-v2`) is generated by `design/canvas-build/build.py` from the repo's data and the canvas asset map (`blobs.json`): edit the templates and logic there, run the script, then publish the changed files to the canvas. Screens: Story (`Main`), Combat, Room, Level up, and the creation flow (`Create` plus one artboard per step). Each file is one screen: markup inside `<x-dc>`, logic in a `class Component extends DCLogic` script, with `{{holes}}`, `<sc-for>` and `<sc-if>` for templating. Treat them as interaction specs to rebuild in the real client, not as code to ship. The logic in `Create.dc.html` (point buying, auto-raise and cost preview) is a working reference for the creation rules.

Shared visual language: near-black ground; skills in their attribute's colour (9 hues), cores in Body amber, Mind cyan, Soul violet; Spectral for prose, Chakra Petch for UI. Every colour rule and token is in [`colour-system.md`](colour-system.md). Trait and perk icons are white Wick ink.

## Writing for the player

Leon, 14 Nov 2026, on a shop shelf headed "Every calibre" with the note "never sold out": "write to the PLAYER bruv not me". Every word a player reads is written to the player, never to the designer. A note that restates a design ruling or a rule's reason does not go on a screen. The Survival text pass (`handoffs/survival-text-pass.md`) has the full list of what changed.

1. **Say what the player can do or what they get**, in the fewest plain words. A label names the thing: "Ammunition", not "Every calibre — never sold out".
2. **Anything said in a place is said by its keeper**, in character and short, each keeper in their own voice, with a few lines each in data so they vary (`data/survival.json` faces `lines`, one bank for each tab, such as `stock` or `gear`). The quartermaster: "Rounds I always have. The good stuff, you take what's on the table." The shrine speaks as its patron's people do (`votaries`).
3. **State a rule once, where it applies, as a consequence**: "One upgrade per visit", "Can't carry more: 2.1 kg over". Never write it as a design note or a comparison with another screen. "Cheaper than a shop's" becomes the price, with the saving shown where you pay it: "960 · 240 less than a shop".
4. **No developer words**: node, stage cap, seam, provisional, spec, placeholder, step, "later", run power. Don't name lean or tie mechanics as such. Use the world's words (trail, camp, stop, fork, the Gate, the vault, boon room) and the game's own terms (credits, the grade names, HP, Echo).
5. **Empty and unbuilt things are hidden**, not labelled "later". A solo player has no Give tab. A tab with nothing in it says so in one plain line ("No one to hand anything to.").
6. **A refusal says what is wrong and what to do**, and names the character: "Kade is 74 credits short.", "Kade needs Brain Power 1 to study the Sigil words."
7. **System text has one voice**: second person, present tense, no exclamation marks, no jokes on buttons. Humour belongs to the keepers' lines.
8. **Numbers come from data**. A data text uses `{=placeholders}` (`tools/text_numbers.py`); never type a digit where the item has the field.
9. **Every label fits its box at 1280** (the screenshots of a text change are taken at 1280 wide).
10. **Tests read data, not sentences.** A browser check reads a data attribute or the data's own text (`data/modes.json` `coming`, a face's `lines`). It does not read a typed copy of a sentence that the next text pass will change.

## The three columns (every mode)

Leon, 14 Nov 2026 (rules 1.54 item 11; a standing rule): *"Make a rule that every mode from now on must maintain the left and right UI, each mode have different function in the right UI yes, but never erase them like Survival mode update just did."* His reference was the Proving Ground's play screen.

1. **Every mode's screens keep the left panel and a right panel; the mode's own content is the middle.** The left panel is the character sheet with all its tabs and Level up, the same in every mode. A mode chooses **what** its right panel holds, never **whether** it has one. The middle can be anything: a story, a path map, a shop, a camp.
2. **The right panel is data:** `data/modes.json` items carry `panels`, a list of panel ids, read by one panel registry (an id to what it draws). The modules keep their grips and their drag order (a per-browser preference, never game state).
3. **Excepted today, as not a mode's screens:** the fight screen (no side panels in any mode, the Proving Ground included) and the full-screen level-up and creation flows. Nothing else is full screen. A new full-screen screen is a ruling for Leon, not a builder's choice.
4. **A brief for any mode's screen that does not carry this rule is not sent** (`CLAUDE.md`, "Every mode keeps the side panels").
5. **The fight's turn panel (0.31.0, every mode's fight):** still no side panels (excepted above), but every information

   four rows: the blocks above it scroll or fold first. See "The fight panel (0.31.0, 0.31.1)" below.
6. **What the sheet offers follows the mode:** a Survival run never shows Ask (voices) or Rest; at Survival's camp the GEAR tab is read-only (the camp builds the gear).

**Skill texts on the three skill screens** (Leon, 14 Nov: *"Redo skill menu in character creation, ? menu text, and level up menu text, especially will power ... no proper line break."*; rules 1.54, survival-spec 14.1 R16):

1. **The bug first:** Will Power's per-point sentence was stored eight times (`skills.json` `per_point.text` and `epccg.effect`, written by a one-off tool whose guard never matched). It is deduplicated, the tool's guard fixed, and the validator fails when a skill's text holds the same sentence twice.
2. **The shape (the road map, lead's design of 14 Nov 2026; it replaces the plainer two-column text list first written here):** a skill's gains are **rows tied to points** (`skills.json` `per_point.rows`, the one copy), each `{at, text}`; `at` is one of `each`, `each from <n>`, `every 2`, `every 3`, `from <n>`, `at <n>` (n from 1 to 10). Numbers in a row's text are `{=placeholders}` reading the skill's own `per_point.numbers` (never a typed digit). `epccg.effect` and the prose `per_point.text` are gone. A validator check: every skill has rows, no row repeats, every n is 1 to 10. (`each from <n>` is the lane's addition to the lead's list: Athletics' Defense, Raw Power's counter-attacks and Improvisation's Field Repair grow with each point from 2 or 3, which no other kind can say.)
3. **The road map** (one helper, `roadMap` in `apps/web/src/screens/shared.js`, for all three screens): ten steps, points 1 to 10. A step holds the rows that land on it: `every 2` on 2, 4, 6, 8, 10; `every 3` on 3, 6, 9; `from n` and `at n` once on n; `each from n` once on n (said "each point from here"). `each` rows land on no step: they are the **head line**, "Every point: …" (an `each from n` row joins it with "from n"). Each step also carries what the skill's own `unlocks` put there: a named unlock (one with a `category`) as "Unlocks <name>", a perk step (`mastery` 1 to 4: M1 at 3, M2 at 5, M3 at 7, M4 at 9) with the skill's two perks by icon and name, and the Mastered perk (`mastery` 5, at 10) by icon and name. The points come from the data, never typed.
4. **The "?" pop-up (EPCCG card)** keeps its frame, portrait, quote and description; in place of the old black "What a point gives" box it shows the head line and the road map: a vertical track of ten rows, the row of the character's current points lit, the rows already owned bright, the rows ahead quiet; each row is the point number, its one or two short lines, and the perk icons on the mastery rows.
5. **Creation's Skills step:** each skill is a small card: its faceless portrait (`skill-<id>`, about 48 × 60 px), the name in its attribute's ink, the tag FIGHT / SURVIVE / TALK, the stepper, the roll modifier as a small chip, and **one line: what the next point gives** (the road map's step at points + 1, else the head line). The three columns Body / Mind / Soul and the nine attributes stay; both budgets stay in view. Hover or "?" opens the road map. The bottom strip shows the selected skill: a larger portrait, its voice line, the next point; no paragraph.
6. **Level-up's skill card:** the same card with its +1; under it **"Next point: …"** and the five mastery pips M1 to M4 and the star, with the perk names beside the ones in reach. The "Each point" paragraph is gone; the road map is one click away ("?").
7. **Type:** rules text never under 13 px on these screens (labels 11, tags 10); a row's text at most about 60 characters; every label fits its box at 1280; nothing scrolls sideways at 1440 × 900, 1280 × 720 and 1024 × 768. Tests read data attributes on the rows, not sentences.

## Story mode — `Main.dc.html`

- **Header:** wordmark, Story/Combat switch, Narrator chip ("AI · your key"), room code, settings.
- **Click to enlarge (every screen):** any character portrait, skill portrait or Wick trait/perk icon opens full size in a lightbox (800 × 1000 portraits, 512 px icons) with its name and kind, over everything including the EPCCG pop-up. Level-up's skill rows are the exception: the row itself is the pick button.
- **Narrator avatar:** Wick, fully 3D and rendered (flat white ink is only for trait and perk icons). Placeholder until the render arrives: a crop of the painted model study.
- **Left: character panel.** Portrait, HP/MP/XP bars, Echo, Supplies and Karma. Five tabs, which together cover the 2020 Excel character sheet:
  - **Skills:** core tabs, attributes and skills, modifiers, signature tags.
  - **Status:** derived combat stats, Leadership, Karma, Reputation per faction, traits, perks (each with its tier, its step out of 4 and its requirement chip in the attribute's UI ink), the **Mastered perk in reach** (the gilded card, locked, with a 10-pip track of the skill's points), buffs and debuffs.
  - **Trait and perk icons** zoom a little (×1.35) on hover everywhere they appear, so the small Wick drawing can be read; with reduced motion the zoom still happens, without the animation.
  - **Gear:** both hands, weapon mastery, the armour slots (head, torso, hands, footwear…; torso sets Armour Class), backpack load against carry weight, and inventory. The prototype shows the filled slots, a line of empty ones (the Veil included), the Essence of Experience with a **Crack the Essence** button that stays locked until someone in the squad qualifies, and **weapon mastery** per family: level, kills with a neutral progress bar toward the next level, attack bonus and weapon perks.
  - **Magic:** Sigils, Soul spells, Blessing.
  - **Bio:** the Character Bio sheet fields.

  The panel scrolls independently.
- **Centre: narrative.** A large opening lore picture and chapter title, then the lore text, then the scene. Prose is in Spectral, with coloured character names, sound-effect lines, co-op players' lines with avatars, inline roll results, and **Inner Voice cards** (for example "SENSORY [Easy 8 · Success]"). The input is docked at the bottom: suggested actions (each tagged with skill, TN and modifier), free text, a Roll button and Send. The story scrolls above it.
- **Right column:**
  - **Area map.** A top-down map drawn by the AI Narrator: a cube on an object, zones, a sound trail, fog over unexplored areas. A human Narrator gets the drawing tools (Select, Cube, Zone, Arrow, Label, Erase); players see it read-only.
  - **Speaking order.** Who is speaking now, who has spoken, who has requested a turn, and who is waiting, plus Pass and Request buttons.
- **Voices** (rules 1.1): each story beat can bring up to 2 private voice cards per player ("ANALYSIS [Passive 13 vs 12]" plus the line in that skill's voice). Clicking a skill in the sheet **asks** it (once per scene). When an Overreach fires, the skill's push appears as a card with two buttons: **Indulge (+1 Echo)** and **Resist (−1 Echo**, −2 if Mastered).
- **Input bar** (prototype v2): suggested actions call 1–3 skills each (combined-check rule). The text box has **Polish with AI** (your Narrator's model rewrites the draft as prose; Undo restores it) and a toggle, **Let the Narrator pick a roll**: whatever you send, the Narrator chooses the skills and the engine rolls on the dice table.
- **Maps:** the campaign runs on the **area map** (button in the right column, opens full size). When a fight starts the Narrator opens a **combat zone** on it; the right column's minimap is a small live view of the current zone, and clicking it (or the zone on the area map) enters combat.
- **Player colours:** in co-op every character carries its player's colour and initials (speaking order, tokens, cards), because one player can run several characters.
- **Dice table:** when the Narrator calls for a check, a pop-up opens with the call (skill, TN, modifier) over a dice table. The player throws the d20 (it tumbles in and lands), then sees the sum and the verdict (Success, Failure, Critical Success, Complication). Spend Echo throws again. **Customise:** table surface (Field felt, Mess-hall oak, Lumeri velvet, Outpost slate, Ship hull, Black-hole glass) and dice (Ivory, Obsidian, Brass, Sigil glass, Bone, Oil-red); choices follow the player to every room, and co-op players see each other's dice. Reduced-motion users get no tumble.
  - **Full implementation (not the prototype):** a real **3D table with 3D dice** that have materials and textures (each table and dice skin above is a texture set: albedo, normal and roughness), thrown by a physics simulation. The **result comes from the engine's seeded RNG first**; the physics throw is then steered to land on that face, so the dice never decide anything and replays and co-op views match. The dice engine **throws several dice at once** when the situation calls for it, one throw per roll the engine makes, each die tagged with what it is for:
    - a burst or full-auto attack: one d20 per attack roll or per target, plus damage dice;
    - initiative at the start of combat: one d20 per combatant the player owns;
    - an Echo reroll: only the rerolled die is thrown again;
    - injuries, scatter and other tables: the table's die (d6, d8, d12…) beside the d20;
    - group checks: each player's die lands on the shared table in their player colour ring.
    The prototype keeps its single 2D d20; the canvas doesn't model this.

## Combat mode — `Combat.dc.html`

- Initiative strip across the top.
- **The fight cards** (Leon, 14 Nov 2026: *"Flashing fight start and fight lost/win card in the same style as the current turn arrived menu UI."*; rules 1.62 item 4900, Leon on live 0.29.0: *"Need a Big ass Battle start, battle finish (won/lost) and a Free roam banner. appear on the screen for at least 1.25s"*; `game/turnIntro.tsx` BigBanner). Big banners in the middle of the screen (about a fifth of the way down, above the map, under the cut-in, well clear of the action bar), in the turn banners' slot: a turn banner waits for them and they queue one after the other. **BATTLE START** as a fight begins, with the fight's name, its setting when the run has one, and who goes first; **VICTORY** and **DEFEAT** when it ends, with the fight's name and, in a Survival run, the one line of what follows (`data/survival.json` `run.after_fight`); **FREE ROAM** after VICTORY as a won run fight's field opens, with its rounds (`survival.json run.after_fight.field`). Each is fully drawn for at least `rules.json combat.cards.hold_ms` (1250 ms): the combat speed changes only the slide in and out, never the hold. Never takes a click (pointer-events none), and fades without moving under reduced motion. BATTLE START plays before the first turn's banner; an end banner waits until the last effect of the fight has played and no spotlight cut-in is up. Only for a fight this page watched begin or end: a reload, a guest joining mid-fight or going back to a fight already on shows none. Not in the Proving Ground, whose WAVE banner and wave card are its own; an Extraction (neither won nor lost) has none. A run's won fight has no end card: VICTORY is its banner, and leaving the field (the vote, or its last round) goes straight to the map (rules 1.62 item 4903). On the field the turn banner, the cut-in and the YOUR TURN box say the field's round ("ROUND 1 OF 3", `survival.json run.after_fight.field.round`), and the field's bar says FREE ROAM and its round. A check reads the banner's kind from `data-phc-card` and its hold from `data-phc-card-hold` (`apps/web/e2e/fight-cards.mjs`).
- **Grid:** up to 26 × 26 tiles of 1.5 m (rules 1.16; the M813 zones and the penthouse are 16 × 11), with move range, half and full cover, round party tokens and square hostile tokens, and an Overwatch line.
- **The camera (roadmap 2c, built 11 Nov 2026; `apps/web/src/game/camera.ts` the arithmetic, `cameraView.ts` the page):** the map's window keeps the tile size players know (what a 16 × 11 map came to when it was fitted to the same window) and a bigger map scrolls inside it.   - **Camera move** (Leon, 12 Nov 2026: "a proper animation with beginning, start, zoom, duration and squash and stretch"; `camera.ts` planMove plans it, sampleMove samples it, `cameraView.ts` plays it on requestAnimationFrame). **Why it felt fast and jerky before:** every move took the same 250 ms at Fast whatever the distance (a cross-map move ran at about 4,000 px/s while a one-tile hop crawled), on a hard quadratic ease-in-out whose speed has a kink in the middle; a move that started while another played restarted from a standstill (its speed fell to zero in one frame: the visible jerk on an enemy turn, where the turn banner, the turn intro and the first beat each send the camera somewhere); the arrival stopped dead; and the only zoom was the your-turn pulse, a separate animation on the grid, not part of the move. **Now:** a short lean back, an eased departure (a sine speed-up), a cruise, a long eased arrival that runs a few pixels past the mark and comes back critically damped (no bounce); on a long move the view pulls back a little in the middle and comes back in on arrival (a transform on the grid about the middle of the window: nothing is laid out per frame, and no transform is left at rest, so the text is crisp); pan and zoom ride one clock. The length grows with the distance (a square root) and follows the combat speed; a move that starts during another blends from where that one is and how fast it goes; a drag, the wheel or a key takes over at once (an arc under way eases back to the map's own size in 0.12 s); a forced centring that would move the view less than a tile (your own turn, Home, a portrait) is not made. Under reduced motion (the system setting or the game's own) it is a plain 160 ms ease: no lean, no overshoot, no arc. **The numbers** (one block, `MOVE` at the top of the move section of `camera.ts`; times at Normal, × 0.85 at Fast, × 0.04 at the test pace):

    | Constant | Value | What it feels like (tune it so) |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `HOP_MS` | 300 ms | a hop of a tile or two; under ~220 ms it reads as a jump |
    | `CROSS_MS` | 860 ms | the cap, a move across the map; higher is more cinematic and every enemy turn waits on it |
    | `CROSS_TILES` | 18 | how far "across the map" is: the length and the arc reach their full size here |
    | `DEPART` / `ARRIVE` | 0.30 / 0.42 | the share of the move speeding up and slowing down; a longer ARRIVE lands softer |
    | `ANTICIPATE_PX` / `ANTICIPATE_SHARE` | 3 px / 0.14 | the lean back before leaving; over ~6 px the camera seems to hesitate; 0 turns it off |

    | `SETTLE_SHARE` / `SETTLE_STIFFNESS` | 0.20 / 7 | the last share of the move, coming back onto the mark; under 5 the end snaps |
    | `ARC_MAX` / `ARC_FROM_TILES` | 0.10 / 3 tiles | the pull-back in the middle of a long move (to 90%), from what distance; never below the smallest zoom; 0 turns it off |
    | `DEAD_ZONE_TILES` | 1 | a forced centring closer than this is not made (no twitch every turn) |

### Fight tabs: order, hiding, the focus rule (Leon, 16 Nov 2026)

- **Stable positions.** Each character keeps its own tab order (`apps/web/src/game/tabPrefs.ts`, localStorage `phc.fightTabs`, keyed by the character's id alone, so the arrangement follows the character into any room). The default is the action list's own order (`GROUP_ORDER`); until the player arranges the tabs, a tab the character gets later (a new skill point, a new focus) takes its place in that order. Once arranged, a new tab is appended at the end, so no tab the player placed moves on its own, and a tab that is absent for a while keeps its place. Rows inside a tab keep the engine's order (`actionsFor`).
- **Custom order and hiding.** The cog's **Fight tabs** panel (`game/fightTabsPanel.tsx`), in a fight and out of one (the map, a shop, the camp): the character's tabs with move up / move down, Show / Hide (the first tab always shows; at least one stays) and Reset to default. With several characters, a picker of the player's own. The number keys follow the order shown (key N is the Nth tab shown, and the two-key codes with it); in a fight the panel numbers the tabs as the bar does and marks a tab the character does not have this fight "not this fight". A hidden Outbursts tab still shows while an Outburst is held (hiding a tab never hides the heart). A player who arranged the tabs opens each turn on the first shown tab with something usable now (a row with no refusal); otherwise today's rule (a held Outburst, the top door on the first turn, the tab used last, Attack with a hostile in sight, Move), limited to the tabs shown.
- **The focus rule** (the four focus doors: Sigils, Outbursts, Figments, Tend; `game/doorView.ts` `tabFocus`, the engine's `kindOfDoor` and `focusHand`): no focus of that door anywhere on the character and the engine refusing every action on the tab, the tab is not shown (any row the engine allows keeps it: a held Figment's orders, an Outburst Begun with no Emanator, which draws on the air; and the Outbursts tab never goes while an Outburst is held: the client never takes away an action the engine offers); one in the pack and none in a hand, the tab shows with one line on top saying to put it on (`skills.json` `door_focus_line`, beside the door's other texts). **Mirrors:** an action's home is one tab (Scuff: Utility, for everyone); a door may list it again at the END of its own tab (`skills.json` `door_mirror`, Brain Power: Scuff): the same action, row and price, never counted by the focus rule. **Bare-handed warning:** the Outbursts tab shows a red WARNING block on top while no Emanator is in a hand (`door_bare_headline`, `door_bare_line`; the rule: each Begin from the air is +2 Strain, `soul_modules.json` `cycle.no_emanator_strain`). Body doors, Shadow and prayer are unchanged.
- A display preference only: it never enters the engine's state, the event log, the save or the room relay. The panel lists the tabs a fight has shown the character; a character that has not fought yet shows a note instead.
