# UI screens (prototype)

Prototypes live in `design/canvas/` as Design Component files (`.dc.html`), the format of the claude.ai design canvas: <https://claude.ai/artifact/9kVVNppZERUaCxBCMWbZgM>. **Prototype v2** (branch `ui-prototype-v2`) is generated by `design/canvas-build/build.py` from the repo's data and the canvas asset map (`blobs.json`): edit the templates and logic there, run the script, then publish the changed files to the canvas. Screens: Story (`Main`), Combat, Room, Level up, and the creation flow (`Create` plus one artboard per step). Each file is one screen: markup inside `<x-dc>`, logic in a `class Component extends DCLogic` script, with `{{holes}}`, `<sc-for>` and `<sc-if>` for templating. Treat them as interaction specs to rebuild in the real client, not as code to ship. The logic in `Create.dc.html` (point buying, auto-raise and cost preview) is a working reference for the creation rules.

Shared visual language: near-black ground; skills in their attribute's colour (9 hues), cores in Body amber, Mind cyan, Soul violet; Spectral for prose, Chakra Petch for UI. Every colour rule and token is in [`colour-system.md`](colour-system.md). Trait and perk icons are white Wick ink.

## Story mode — `Main.dc.html`

- **Header:** wordmark, Story/Combat switch, Narrator chip ("AI · your key"), room code, settings.
- **Click to enlarge (every screen):** any character portrait, skill portrait or Wick trait/perk icon opens full size in a lightbox (800 × 1000 portraits, 512 px icons) with its name and kind, over everything including the EPCCG pop-up. Level-up's skill rows are the exception: the row itself is the pick button.
- **Narrator avatar:** Wick, fully 3D and rendered (flat white ink is only for trait and perk icons). Placeholder until the render arrives: a crop of the painted model study.
- **Left: character panel.** Portrait, HP/MP/XP bars, Echo, Supplies and Karma. Five tabs, which together cover the 2020 Excel character sheet:
  - **Skills:** core tabs, attributes and skills, modifiers, signature tags.
  - **Status:** derived combat stats, Leadership, Karma, Reputation per faction, traits, perks (each with its tier, its step out of 4 and its requirement chip in the attribute's UI ink), the **Mastered perk in reach** (the gilded card, locked, with a 10-pip track of the skill's points), buffs and debuffs.
  - **Trait and perk icons** zoom a little (×1.35) on hover everywhere they appear, so the small Wick drawing can be read; with reduced motion the zoom still happens, without the animation.
  - **Gear:** both hands, weapon mastery, the armour slots (head, torso, hands, footwear…; torso sets Armour Class), backpack load against carry weight, and inventory. The prototype shows the filled slots, a line of empty ones (the Veil included), the Essence of Experience with a **Crack the Essence** button that stays locked until someone in the squad qualifies, and **weapon mastery** per family: level, kills with a neutral progress bar toward the next level, attack bonus and weapon perks.
  - **Magic:** Sigils, Soul spells, Blessing.
  - **Bio:** the Character Bio sheet fields.

  The panel scrolls independently.
- **Centre: narrative.** A large opening lore picture and chapter title, then the lore text, then the scene. Prose is in Spectral, with coloured character names, sound-effect lines, co-op players' lines with avatars, inline roll results, and **Inner Voice cards** (for example "SENSORY [Easy 8 · Success]"). The input is docked at the bottom: suggested actions (each tagged with skill, TN and modifier), free text, a Roll button and Send. The story scrolls above it.
- **Right column:**
  - **Area map.** A top-down map drawn by the AI Narrator: a cube on an object, zones, a sound trail, fog over unexplored areas. A human Narrator gets the drawing tools (Select, Cube, Zone, Arrow, Label, Erase); players see it read-only.
  - **Speaking order.** Who is speaking now, who has spoken, who has requested a turn, and who is waiting, plus Pass and Request buttons.
- **Voices** (rules 1.1): each story beat can bring up to 2 private voice cards per player ("ANALYSIS [Passive 13 vs 12]" plus the line in that skill's voice). Clicking a skill in the sheet **asks** it (once per scene). When an Overreach fires, the skill's push appears as a card with two buttons: **Indulge (+1 Echo)** and **Resist (−1 Echo**, −2 if Mastered).
- **Input bar** (prototype v2): suggested actions call 1–3 skills each (combined-check rule). The text box has **Polish with AI** (your Narrator's model rewrites the draft as prose; Undo restores it) and a toggle, **Let the Narrator pick a roll**: whatever you send, the Narrator chooses the skills and the engine rolls on the dice table.
- **Maps:** the campaign runs on the **area map** (button in the right column, opens full size). When a fight starts the Narrator opens a **combat zone** on it; the right column's minimap is a small live view of the current zone, and clicking it (or the zone on the area map) enters combat.
- **Player colours:** in co-op every character carries its player's colour and initials (speaking order, tokens, cards), because one player can run several characters.
- **Dice table:** when the Narrator calls for a check, a pop-up opens with the call (skill, TN, modifier) over a dice table. The player throws the d20 (it tumbles in and lands), then sees the sum and the verdict (Success, Failure, Critical Success, Complication). Spend Echo throws again. **Customise:** table surface (Field felt, Mess-hall oak, Lumeri velvet, Outpost slate, Ship hull, Black-hole glass) and dice (Ivory, Obsidian, Brass, Sigil glass, Bone, Oil-red); choices follow the player to every room, and co-op players see each other's dice. Reduced-motion users get no tumble.
  - **Full implementation (not the prototype):** a real **3D table with 3D dice** that have materials and textures (each table and dice skin above is a texture set: albedo, normal and roughness), thrown by a physics simulation. The **result comes from the engine's seeded RNG first**; the physics throw is then steered to land on that face, so the dice never decide anything and replays and co-op views match. The dice engine **throws several dice at once** when the situation calls for it, one throw per roll the engine makes, each die tagged with what it is for:
    - a burst or full-auto attack: one d20 per attack roll or per target, plus damage dice;
    - initiative at the start of combat: one d20 per combatant the player owns;
    - an Echo reroll: only the rerolled die is thrown again;
    - injuries, scatter and other tables: the table's die (d6, d8, d12…) beside the d20;
    - group checks: each player's die lands on the shared table in their player colour ring.
    The prototype keeps its single 2D d20; the canvas doesn't model this.

## Combat mode — `Combat.dc.html`

- Initiative strip across the top.
- **Grid:** up to 26 × 26 tiles of 1.5 m (rules 1.16; the M813 zones and the penthouse are 16 × 11), with move range, half and full cover, round party tokens and square hostile tokens, and an Overwatch line.
- **The camera (roadmap 2c, built 11 Nov 2026; `apps/web/src/game/camera.ts` the arithmetic, `cameraView.ts` the page):** the map's window keeps the tile size players know (what a 16 × 11 map came to when it was fitted to the same window) and a bigger map scrolls inside it. The same camera serves the fight and the area map, and it is local to each browser (nothing about it is sent to the room).
  - **It follows the play.** To the unit whose turn it is as its banner plays; to the target before an attack, a spell, a push, a throw or a walk plays (the effects queue plans the move and the camera leaves a glide's length early, so it has arrived when the beat plays and no beat is delayed; the first beat of a batch has nothing before it, so there the glide and the beat start together; the attacker stays in frame too when both fit); a result (a wound, a heal, a unit going down, a Miracle's answer on a crowd) never moves it and gets an edge arrow when it is off screen; back to your own unit, centred, when your turn starts. One move a beat, its length from the distance (see **Camera move** below). Not during a drag, not in the overview; on your own turn not while your pointer is over the map nor for 4 s after you moved it by hand. Fog of war holds: what you cannot see moves nothing. **It never goes somewhere only to leave at once** (review, 11 Nov 2026): a move the next beat would undo before the camera has rested 0.35 s (at Fast) is not made, so a hostile 10 tiles away that shoots as its turn starts leaves the camera on its target and gets an edge arrow, instead of the view swinging to the shooter and back within half a second; a technique's name over the attacker moves nothing (the attack it names takes the camera to the target). A number or word that appears a moment after its hit (a wound's figure, DOWNED) lands on the tile where it is by then when the camera moved in between (`fx/dom.ts` liveAt). A map that fits its window whole (16 × 11 and smaller) has no camera and no small map at all. When the map is laid out anew the camera keeps what it was looking at: your own unit the first time (so also on the way back from the level-up screen in the middle of a fight), the tile in the middle of the view after a resize or a change of size. A tile beyond the browser window's own edge is still found by the effects layer, so a walk through it is drawn and not dropped. On a narrow window, where the whole page scrolls, a turn's start and Home also bring the map's window back into the page.
  - **You move it.** Drag the map (the click that ends a drag never acts), the wheel or trackpad, the arrow keys or W A S D (2 tiles a press), **Home** back to your unit (the toolbar's My unit button too).
  - **Nothing happens unseen.** An arrow at the window's edge, in the acting side's colour and named for screen readers, for each tile of a beat that is off screen when the camera did not go there; a click goes there (the arrow's head is a plain triangle about its own centre, so it reads as pointing where it points). A click on a portrait in the initiative strip shows that unit (an unseen hostile's portrait is disabled).
  - **The small map** in the window's top-right corner: 150 px, units as dots in their side's colour (round for the squad, square for hostiles; none for an unseen hostile), cover and solid ground faintly, the camera's frame in white; click or drag on it to move the view; HIDE folds it away (remembered in this browser, `phc.miniMap`). The grid keeps a strip of free room beside it so the corner tiles can be scrolled out from under it.
  - **The overview on M:** in a fight M (and the toolbar's Whole map button) fits the whole map to the window and back; in the overview a click on a tile goes back to the usual size centred there and never acts. The map zone keeps its width in the overview (the smaller map sits in the middle of it), so the action column does not jump wider and back. Outside a fight M opens the area map as before; the area map has its own Whole map and My character buttons.
  - **The toolbar** above the fight map: − / size / + (your own size, kept in this browser), Usual size, Whole map (M), My unit (Home). The letters and numbers of the grid stay at the window's edge while the map scrolls.
  - **Camera move** (Leon, 12 Nov 2026: "a proper animation with beginning, start, zoom, duration and squash and stretch"; `camera.ts` planMove plans it, sampleMove samples it, `cameraView.ts` plays it on requestAnimationFrame). **Why it felt fast and jerky before:** every move took the same 250 ms at Fast whatever the distance (a cross-map move ran at about 4,000 px/s while a one-tile hop crawled), on a hard quadratic ease-in-out whose speed has a kink in the middle; a move that started while another played restarted from a standstill (its speed fell to zero in one frame: the visible jerk on an enemy turn, where the turn banner, the turn intro and the first beat each send the camera somewhere); the arrival stopped dead; and the only zoom was the your-turn pulse, a separate animation on the grid, not part of the move. **Now:** a short lean back, an eased departure (a sine speed-up), a cruise, a long eased arrival that runs a few pixels past the mark and comes back critically damped (no bounce); on a long move the view pulls back a little in the middle and comes back in on arrival (a transform on the grid about the middle of the window: nothing is laid out per frame, and no transform is left at rest, so the text is crisp); pan and zoom ride one clock. The length grows with the distance (a square root) and follows the combat speed; a move that starts during another blends from where that one is and how fast it goes; a drag, the wheel or a key takes over at once (an arc under way eases back to the map's own size in 0.12 s); a forced centring that would move the view less than a tile (your own turn, Home, a portrait) is not made. The effects queue (`fx/play.ts`) leaves each move's own length ahead of its beat, but never before the beat before it began (CAM_HOLD_MS, 120 ms at Fast), and the camera squeezes a move whose beat is closer than its length, never under the old 250 ms; the queue itself waits on nothing more than before (measured: enemy turns at Fast take as long as they did). Under reduced motion (the system setting or the game's own) it is a plain 160 ms ease: no lean, no overshoot, no arc. **The numbers** (one block, `MOVE` at the top of the move section of `camera.ts`; times at Normal, × 0.85 at Fast, × 0.04 at the test pace):

    | Constant | Value | What it feels like (tune it so) |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `HOP_MS` | 300 ms | a hop of a tile or two; under ~220 ms it reads as a jump |
    | `CROSS_MS` | 860 ms | the cap, a move across the map; higher is more cinematic and every enemy turn waits on it |
    | `CROSS_TILES` | 18 | how far "across the map" is: the length and the arc reach their full size here |
    | `DEPART` / `ARRIVE` | 0.30 / 0.42 | the share of the move speeding up and slowing down; a longer ARRIVE lands softer |
    | `ANTICIPATE_PX` / `ANTICIPATE_SHARE` | 3 px / 0.14 | the lean back before leaving; over ~6 px the camera seems to hesitate; 0 turns it off |
    | `OVERSHOOT_PX` / `OVERSHOOT_FROM_TILES` | 6 px / 3 tiles | how far past the mark it runs (2% of the distance, at most this), and from what distance |
    | `SETTLE_SHARE` / `SETTLE_STIFFNESS` | 0.20 / 7 | the last share of the move, coming back onto the mark; under 5 the end snaps |
    | `ARC_MAX` / `ARC_FROM_TILES` | 0.10 / 3 tiles | the pull-back in the middle of a long move (to 90%), from what distance; never below the smallest zoom; 0 turns it off |
    | `DEAD_ZONE_TILES` | 1 | a forced centring closer than this is not made (no twitch every turn) |
    | `REDUCED_MS` | 160 ms | the plain ease under reduced motion |
    | `SQUEEZE_MIN_MS` | 250 ms at Fast | the shortest a move is squeezed to when its beat is close (the old glide) |
    | `HAND_MS` | 120 ms | an arrow key or a click on the small map: the player's own plain glide |
- **Moving:** with Move selected, the reachable tiles are highlighted. Hovering one draws the cheapest path as white arrows (around the hull and other units; wreck tiles count double) and a badge on the destination with the AP it costs and the tiles walked ("−2 AP · 4 tiles"); the AP bar previews the spend. Movement is tiles per AP (MPAT: 2, from Agility 3); tiles paid for but not walked stay available that turn and show as "Paid".
- **Turn panel:** the AP bar previews the selected action's cost. The action list scrolls and is grouped as Class, Movement, Attack and Utility (20 actions). **Hover for detail**: rule text, AP cost and requirements. Rank-locked moves are shown locked. Confirm and End turn.
- **Below:** target card and a short combat log in prose.

## Room — `Room.dc.html`

- **Players** (up to 4, each with a colour and initials) are listed separately from the **squad**: one player can run more than one character, and each character card shows its owner's colour stripe and badge. "Create a character" and "Bring a saved character". Start Session waits until every character is locked in.
- **Where it starts:** the Sector, then the campaign start as cards; the chosen start shows a big picture and a few paragraphs of lore.
- **Who narrates?**
  - **AI Narrator:** pick a provider (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, OpenRouter, or local OpenAI-compatible), enter an API key and model. The key stays in the browser.
  - **I'll narrate:** a private Narrator screen.
- Tone: Grim, Pulp or Literary.

## Character creation — `Create.dc.html` (+ `CreateCores`, `CreateSkills` open it at those steps)

In-world framing: creation is filling in the EPCCG, Anti-Heaven Operations Command's intake form, whatever the character's affiliation.

Steps: **Identity → Archetype → Skills → Traits → Kit → Lock in.** (Creation spends 10 Skill, 10 Attribute and 6 Core points with no caps beyond the tree's own, so a 7/1/1 build is legal; skills spend the Attribute and Core points they need. Prototype v2 implements this.)

1. **Identity:** name; **Sector** (the Earth Sector by default), then **origin** from that Sector's list (`docs/lore/Sector-Earth.md`), shown as cards with what you know and how people see you; race (once races exist); association, gender, blood type, main hand, last dream, favourite song, history, and the **portrait**. All of the character's look is chosen here.
2. **Archetype** (the Cores screen, modelled on Disco Elysium's "pick an archetype or customise"): **46 archetypes in 9 family tabs** (Frontline, Guns and hunters, Shadows, Sigil casters, Minds, Soul and faith, Voices and leaders, Hybrids and wanderers, and **All-in**: nine 7/1/1 builds, one per Attribute) plus **Custom**. Each card shows the name, an RPG role tag (Tank, Sniper, Face…), a one-line blurb, the three Cores its preset produces as big `‹ N ›` rows with diamonds and descriptor words (Feeble → Legendary), and the classes it leans toward. Picking one spends **every** creation point: the 10 Skill points, all Attribute points and all Core points, so the player can go straight to Lock in. Each card lists the Attributes it raises. Custom starts empty. **Guidance only:** the archetype is not saved anywhere, gives nothing, changes nothing later, and doesn't touch the portrait. Data: `data/archetypes.json`.
3. **Skills:** all 27 skills at once, no tabs and no scrolling: three Core columns, each attribute a node linked by a coloured spine to its three compact skill cards (portrait, name, points, modifier, stepper). Hovering a card fills a detail strip with its summary and voice line. Cards show the full 4:5 skill art. Each card has a **?** (faint until you hover the card, so it doesn't cover the art) that opens the skill's EPCCG entry (also on the character sheet's skill rows and the level-up skill list): the in-world quote with the First-Patch Revival portrait of the one it's about, then the explanation, at high levels, good for and bad for, then the skill's **two Acquired perks** with a line on how perk steps work (one step at each of 3, 5, 7 and 9 points: a perk at Tier I or an upgrade to Tier II), each with its Wick icon (click to enlarge), type, AP cost, and the Tier I and Tier II text, then its **combo perks** on their own card style (a top bar split into each listed skill's ink, a COMBO tag, the skill chips joined by "+" with the 5-point requirement, the effect, and THE DEED in italics; earned in play, never chosen), and last the skill's **Mastered perk** on a gilded card: a double rule in the attribute's ink with corner rivets and a soft glow, a ringed icon medallion, the name in Spectral, its in-world line in italics, then the rules text. A perk the artist hasn't drawn yet shows a dashed "icon soon" box (a ✦ "icon to come" medallion on the Mastered card). A top bar holds the budget, the class leans and Reset. Three budgets, shown as counters in the top bar: **10 Skill, 10 Attribute and 6 Core points** (7 Core with Mind Without Fog; rules 1.11), starting from the archetype's full placement (all three at 0 left after an archetype). Each Core header and Attribute node has its own `‹ n ›` stepper. Raising a skill spends the Attribute and Core points it needs; the › button's tooltip says what it will cost ("−1 Skill · −1 Attribute (Endurance 3→4) · −1 Core (Body 3→4)") or why it is blocked ("Not enough Core points left"). Every point is placed: a Core point can't be traded for Attribute points (rules 1.11). Lowering a Core or Attribute stops at what the skills under it need. Nothing is capped except by the tree (skill ≤ Attribute ≤ Core ≤ 10). The side panel shows the classes you'd qualify for at level 3.
4. **Traits:** pick up to two. Incompatible traits grey out.
5. **Kit:** four starting kits (Field medic, Line soldier, Sigil student, Soul initiate; the Soul kit carries the Emanator a Soul build needs).
6. **Lock in:** the full **personnel record**, stamped with the AHOC seal, so a player sees everything about the character before committing:
   - portrait, name and the identity fields (race, gender, blood type, main hand, association, favourite song, last dream, history);
   - the origin in full (home, upbringing, what you know, how people see you, suggested skills, hook);
   - level-1 stats from the spec's formulas (Max HP, HP per level, Max MP, AP, Echo, initiative, Movement (tiles per AP), Defense, carry, vision, saves, healing given);
   - every Core, Attribute and Skill with points, modifier and Mastery;
   - each trait with its icon, flavour and every upside and downside;
   - the kit, plus the Essence of Experience everyone starts with;
   - the two closest classes at level 3 with their signature totals;
   - the first voices the player will hear (their highest skills' quiet lines).
   Beside it: the checklist, Lock in (only once all 10 points are spent) and Save as draft.

The same big-row Cores layout is reused at **level-up**, where Core and Attribute points are spent.

The Identity, Traits, Kit and Lock-in steps have a side panel with a live preview and the classes you'd qualify for at level 3.

## Level up — `LevelUp.dc.html`

Shown at each level-up. The prototype shows level 3 at the end of session 1:

- **Gains** as chips in the header: HP, the skill point, the class (each ◆ until done, ✓ after).
- **Class choice:** cards for the classes among your two highest signature totals, each with its signature skills and your totals, resource, rank I move and the rank II/III thresholds. Spending the skill point first can change the list.
- **Skill point:** every skill, with its cap shown. After creation a skill can't pass its Attribute; capped skills explain what unlocks them (an attribute point at the next even level).
- **Cores:** the Disco Elysium-style big rows (descriptor word, value, diamonds). Attribute and Core points are spent here when the level grants them.

## Spotlight cut-ins (decision 113; `docs/design/cutin-spec.md`)

Over every in-room screen, z-index 64 (`apps/web/src/game/spotlight/`). A spotlight moment (the music's list, Play my song, a
patron's intervention, the god interjection trigger) blurs and dims the screen (3 px) and tears one wide strip across it
(−4°): the portrait's eyes in a torn window, the ink lip along both rips in the character's highest-attribute ink (a
god's in its accent), the label in Chakra Petch (`TAKEDOWN · AGILITY`), the name and caption in Spectral. A group stacks
2–4 thinner strips from alternate sides, names at their torn-off ends, with a caption plate below. About 1.4 s (1.6 s at
most, 1.0 s reduced); any click or key skips and the click is swallowed. In a fight the action it happened in then plays
15% slower. A party crit in a fight gets the short form: one slimmer strip (22vh), `CRITICAL HIT · <ATTRIBUTE>`,
"Critical hit on Rook", 0.9 s; a bigger moment in the same action shows instead. Faith 2.2's Miracle scene shares the
stage: a strip never draws over it (it waits for the landing). Settings: Cut-ins All / Once per scene / Off. Character
creation: an uploaded portrait gets a Cut-in framing slider with a preview of the strip.

## Ultimate perk award

Ultimate perks are secret: no screen lists them, hints at them or tracks progress toward them, and the "?" entries never mention them. The one place they appear is the award, shown the moment the engine grants one (both named combo perks held). Board: `UltimateAward.dc.html`.

- A full-screen reveal on near-black: "ULTIMATE PERK", then "Nobody is told these exist. You found one."
- The two held combo perks sit left and right, each with its split skill-ink bar, joined by lines to a white medallion that scales in and glows; faint rays turn slowly behind it (still under reduced motion).
- The name in Spectral at 58 px, white; the in-world line in italics; the rules text; "Earned by holding X and Y"; the skills it draws on as chips in their inks.
- **Take it** (white, the colour system's rarest state colour) adds it to the sheet: "On MPAT's sheet now. Nobody else's screens will ever list it."
- Prototype only: a dashed strip at the bottom cycles through all six so they can be reviewed. The real client never shows it.
