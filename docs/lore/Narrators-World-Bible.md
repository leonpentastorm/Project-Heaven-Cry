## The truth

*Narrator's eyes only. Sections marked* **Suggested** *are proposals, not canon.*

The world of Project Heaven Cry is a simulation, built by people on our Earth to test a universe where anything can be made with a thought.

**The bots.** The air is full of sub-nano machines (still unnamed) that can build almost anything out of their own bodies. The people of the world call them **mana**. New bots appear in the common air at a set interval, and that interval is a plot point.

**The Celestials.** They are people from our Earth, logged into the simulation. They're immortal there: whatever they imagine, the bots build in front of them. Some time ago, Earth cut them off for reasons nobody knows, and they can no longer log out. Now they're stuck, and they scrape the bots as raw material for their imagination.

What they became varies:

- Some play mythic gods and heroes from Earth's old stories.
- Some invent original gods, with lore of their own.
- Some troll, for fun.
- Some turned evil.

Any religion, brand or legend from the real world can appear in the setting, because a Celestial brought it in.

**The Celestial Accords.** The Celestials aren't lawless. They live under rules and treaties agreed among themselves. **They don't fight each other (canon):** Celestials are friends, or at least amicable. What's the point of fighting, when you're immortal and stuck together? Within the Accords, a Celestial can play any role they like, and that's the loose rule for silly play: a player who wants a legendary infiltrator in the story gets one, because some Celestial gamer loves playing that role.

**The Unspoken Name (Accord clause, canon).** A Celestial may play anything from real Earth: a hero from a game they loved, a god, a band, a whole genre. What the Accords forbid is saying a real-Earth name aloud inside the world. So they rename it. A Celestial who wants to live out a famous game's teenage-detective-with-a-summoned-spirit fantasy can, but in the world the spirit is called a "Ghost", and it is "a robot-looking ghost with a huge spear that throws lightning", never its real name. The rule for every scene:

- **Plain to the players, never to the characters.** The people at the table can recognise the reference instantly. Nobody in the world can name it, because nobody in the world has ever heard the name.
- **Players can import anything.** A player who wants their favourite sneaking-soldier legend in the story gets them, framed as a Celestial who wants to LARP that role. The import keeps its look and tricks; it loses its real name.
- **NPCs see the legend, not the reference.** A ghost in a bandana who walks through walls is a local myth, not a meme. The silliness lives in the Celestial; the world stays coherent.
- **The Narrator (AI or human) never says or writes a real-Earth proper noun in the world:** not in narration, NPC dialogue or item names. If a player says the real name at the table, NPCs answer to the in-world one.
- **Official content stays generic.** Shipped data, art and published campaigns use archetypes ("a sneaking-soldier legend"), not recognisable recreations of specific characters. What a group imports at its own table is its own business.

**Canon Accord clauses**

- **Hands off.** No Celestial directly intervenes in the human world.
- **Create, never erase.** A Celestial can create (a people, a monster, a curse, a whole plot) but can't intervene to remove what exists. So plots get started for fun and abandoned when their maker gets bored, and the consequences stay. The world is full of them.
- **Only by cause.** A Celestial appears in the world only through a cause: a summoning, a miracle answering a prayer, a pact. **A Celestial appearing without a cause is a very big deal.**
- **The Unspoken Name** (above).
- **The Veil exception** (below).

**The Veil exception (canon).** The Veil is the one exception the Accords allow to "create, never erase": an NPC who *proves* the truth may be erased. That is why the Progenitors were hunted. Suspicion isn't proof, which is why their descendants are still alive, and why Anti-Heaven Operations Command must never find the proof it was founded to find.

**Heroes are patches (canon).** A Celestial who regrets a creation can't erase it; it can only create something new to stop it. So some of the world's heroes and legends are a Celestial's patch for its own mistake: made with exactly the gifts to end one monster, one curse, one plot. A player character might be one without knowing it.

**Celestial politics (canon).** Celestials are mega-powerful humans, and where there are humans there is politics. They don't fight, but they lobby, trade favours and bot quotas, form cliques around shared tastes, hold grudges, argue the Knowing, renegotiate the Accords, and sulk. **Suggested** shapes for it: Accord sessions where clauses are amended; cliques by genre (the ones who build academy stories, the ones who think that's cringe); patrons competing for worshippers; a Celestial who loses a vote and takes it out on a plot. Their politics is the weather NPCs live under.

**Suggested Accord clauses**

- **Pacts first.** No Celestial works miracles for NPCs without a pact.
- **Keep the costume.** A Celestial in a pact stays in the role they agreed to play.
- **The Veil.** No NPC may learn the truth (see the Knowing, below).
- **Quotas.** Each Celestial may scrape only so many bots. That's why prayer and war matter to them.

**The Knowing.** An NPC who learns the truth is either killed or raised to demigod. **Suggested:** under the Accords, the Celestial who finds out has to choose, and one who raises an NPC answers for them. The Celestials argue about it, as friends do: some prefer erasing, some elevating, and it's a standing argument, not a war. Demigods are the bridge between the layers, and the obvious endgame for a player character.

**The people.** Everyone the player characters know, themselves included, are NPCs: written into the code, or created by Celestials as set dressing. Over millennia they spread across galaxies and built billions of years of history. To the Celestials, they are still NPCs. They know none of this. From where they stand, they are humanity, fighting gods.

**Robots** are NPCs made by NPCs: creations of a creation. A Humanity First supremacist who learned the truth would find out they're only one rung higher on the same ladder.

**The tone line (canon).** NPCs are funny the way people are: proud, petty, deadpan, absurd, and the EPCCG's field quotes are the model. Celestials are funny the way people are when nothing can hurt them: careless, and that carelessness is where their comedy turns cruel. The tragedy belongs to the NPCs. A scene can be both at once, and the best ones are.

**The ladder (canon, Leon 9 Nov 2026).** Every layer of this world was made by the layer above it, and then abandoned by it. Earth made the Celestials and cut them off. The Celestials made the NPCs and treat them as scenery. The NPCs made robots, and on M813 they told them to shut themselves down. MPAT's demand for equality is the whole setting in miniature. That's the double meaning of the title: everyone is crying up at a heaven that doesn't answer.

## The worlds and the war (canon)

**Scale.** Humanity is not one civilisation. After billions of years it is **several sextillion people on trillions of worlds**, split into more powers than anyone can count. The setting is bigger than almost any RPG's, and its lore can extend forever. Every human fights the races and factions local to their own region; nobody knows the whole. Each such pocket of humanity is a **Sector** (canon; template in `Sectors.md`). Some people know other Sectors exist and many don't; Anti-Heaven Operations Command knows for certain. Everything named in the EPCCG (Central City, the Thousand-Planet City, the Lumeri Church, the Crawlers, Daggerfall) is **one Sector's story**, the Earth Sector's. The Crawlers, for instance, are known only to a tiny pocket of humanity. A campaign set elsewhere can have its own powers, races, wars and Celestial messes, with Anti-Heaven Operations Command as the one constant.


**Earth, the homeworld.** The first planet the simulation's builders made, where humanity began. Central City stands on Earth: bank cards, shock batons, Central Command Center, the CCEC. Humanity has since spread across many worlds in many galaxies; the great centres, such as Central City and the Thousand-Planet City, lie in different galaxies. *Thousand-Planet City* is a faction name; its army is the **Thousand World Legion**, which the EPCCG calls the Thousand-Planet City Army (the 093rd Regiment is one of its regiments). **Suggested:** Earth was named before the Accords existed, which is why the homeworld can carry a real-Earth name when nothing else may.

**How campaigns start.** With a Celestial's mess. Usually it's the fallout of a plot someone started for fun and walked away from: a people made for a story nobody finished, a monster left running, a curse nobody lifts. Sometimes it's an appearance with a cause: a summoning, a miracle, a pact gone wrong. Rarely, and it's a catastrophe-level event, a Celestial appears with no cause at all. Anti-Heaven Operations Command answers whichever it is. That collision is the usual first scene of a campaign.

**The Crawlers (NPC version 2).** A Celestial created a second patch of people, ordinary humans with supernatural abilities (espers), because she wanted a battle-academy story. Another Celestial hated it ("anime is cringe") and cursed the whole patch: to the original humans (NPC version 1), every NPC-2 person appears as a malformed creature, shaped by whatever the local humans fear most. The first NPC-1 settlement to meet them lived on a world with crawling, humanoid zombie-like things, so they saw crawlers, and the name *Crawler* stuck.

- **The curse runs one way.** Crawlers see ordinary humans as ordinary humans. They know they're people, and they watch the people they see as people scream at them as monsters. That's the drama.
- **True love lifts the curse.** An NPC-1 person who truly loves an NPC-2 person sees them as human again: human, with abilities. Mixed families exist because of it, and they are rare.
- **Suggested consequences:** two humans looking at the same Crawler can see different monsters, because each sees their own fear. In co-op, each player can be given a different private description of the same Crawler. Humanity First's "Crawlers are vermin" is the curse talking, and Paul Remarque's father "cradling the body of a Crawler smaller than the rest" was a parent holding a child.

**Anti-Heaven Operations Command and the Progenitors (canon).** About 5,000 years before Central City was founded, a Celestial ran an isekai-style anime plot for a while, and a world's humans were made with enormous powers to fit it. About a thousand of them learned the truth and founded an organisation to kill the Celestials: the start of Anti-Heaven Operations Command. The Celestials hunted them like dogs. All thousand are dead, and the Celestials never made that world's kind of human again, so they are effectively extinct. In-world, and in the organisation's own lore, they are **the Progenitors**.

- **What's left:** semi-powered descendants of the Progenitors. They hold the high posts, and they fight politics more than enemies.
- **Decay:** the organisation began by trying to overthrow Heaven. Now it tries to limit the damage every time a Celestial appears.
- **Desperation:** the descendants will do anything for power, because they know what happens if they're taken out: the next time a Celestial shows up, humanity gets wiped out.
- **What the descendants know:** the founding myth and the old stories. They suspect the truth, and they can't do anything about it.
- **Everywhere:** like a containment foundation, it has bases on every human planet and on many frontier worlds. It's the one faction that turns up in every campaign. Each **local chapter** takes its character from whoever leads it, so chapters differ wildly: zealous, corrupt, paranoid, decent, bankrupt.
- **Player characters are not members by default.** But character creation always uses its document: the EPCCG is the intake form humanity's Anti-Heaven chapters use for anyone they process, recruit or need on file.
- The EPCCG, the cheerful manual with Wick on it, is this organisation's training manual and form.

**Operation Daggerfall (around year 3800).** A huge number of NPC-2 people were spawned on a world next to Earth. War followed. The original humans won, and the NPC-2 main world was nuked from orbit. Humanity celebrates it as a turning point; veterans like Paul Remarque remember what they shelled. (Suggested, as before: every one of those deaths released bots. Who benefited?)

**Timeline.**

Years are counted on the **Central City founding calendar**. The NPCs' history runs back billions of years, and many powers in the world are far older than Central City; the 4000s are only Central City's count.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Real Earth, about year 65,000 | The simulation runs. To the Celestials it was a normal VR game, until they found they couldn't log out. |
| About 5,000 years before Central City's founding | The Progenitors found what becomes Anti-Heaven Operations Command; the Celestials hunt all thousand of them down. |
| About 3800 (Central City calendar) | Operation Daggerfall. |
| 27 July 4084 | The M813 incident. |
| 15 October 4090 | Snowden "Eye" Wright writes up M813 at Outpost-921. |

People in this world live to about 400, since even their baseline technology is ahead of real Earth's, so Daggerfall veterans are still alive in the 4080s.

## The three schools of magic

Every school does the same thing underneath: it tells the bots what to build. What differs is the interface.

| School | Core | Interface | What it takes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sigil | Mind | Commands written in the bots' own language | A scholar's mind |
| Soul | Soul | Imagination, sent through the heart to the world's processor | An artist's sensitivity, and a heart that can take the strain |
| Celestial | Virtue | Prayer, delivered to a Celestial who decides | Faith, and a god who's in the mood |

### Sigil magic

A **mana core** is a dense cluster of bots, a stone. Sigil mages load it into an **Evoker**, a tool that draws predetermined sigils in the air. Each sigil is really an **English word**, a command the bots obey. A fireball is three sigils: FIRE – CONDENSE – PUSH. Because it's bound by language, it takes a scholar to do right.

When a core runs dry, the mage reloads the Evoker with a fresh stone: *clack, clack, RACK.*

### Soul magic

Celestials think a thing and the bots build it. A few NPCs, sensitive souls and artists, can almost do the same. They connect their heart to the world's processor, pull bots into the heart to open a signal, then think the spell's shape and behaviour into being. Overdo it and the bots in your heart explode. That's a **Mana Short**.

The Soul mage's tool is the Emanator. It calls more bots and stores them like a battery, so the mage doesn't have to let them all inside.

### Celestial magic (Blessings)

The faithful make a pact with a Celestial who thinks playing god is fun. A prayer channels bots to that Celestial, carrying the message and a camera feed. If the Celestial is pleased, they answer with their own creative power and their vast store of bots.

Blessings have themes only because the Celestials in pacts agreed to roleplay a theme. Outside a pact, a Celestial can do whatever they want.

## The mana loop

The bots move between four places: the air, the mana cores, the bodies of the living, and the Celestials. The two mortal schools feed each other, which makes a mixed squad stronger than either kind of mage alone.

*[Diagram in the original Doc: The mana loop · where the bots go]*

Soul mages draw from the air and crystallise cores; Sigil mages burn cores and vent the excess back into the air. Prayer is the one path that takes bots out of the world for good.

**Canon**

- **New bots** appear in the air at a set interval.
- **Death releases bots.** When a person or a monster-animal dies, the simulation has less to run, so bots spawn from the body to balance the world.
- **Sigil mages vent.** Burning a core releases more bots than the spell uses. A Sigil mage has to vent that flux now and then in combat, which raises the ambient mana in the air.
- **Soul mages draw on the air.** Ambient mana is what Soul spells are built from.
- **Soul mages can crystallise.** A Soul mage can gather ambient mana and crystallise it into a core for a Sigil mage.
- **Prayer sends bots away.** Every Blessing ships bots out of the world, to a Celestial.

**Suggested: rules that follow from it**

1. **Ambient mana as a battlefield track:** Thin, Normal, Rich, Flooded. Kills and venting push it up; Soul casting pulls it down. Soul spells are cheaper the richer the air is, and in a Flooded field Mana Shorts come easier.
2. **Venting as a tactical choice.** Venting costs AP and makes the air Rich, which helps your Soul mage and the enemy's alike.
3. **Prayer is a drain, and that's why gods want worshippers.** The Celestials are scraping for bots, and every prayer delivers some. Worship is a supply chain.
4. **War feeds the gods.** Every death releases bots. A Celestial who wants more has every reason to start wars. Who benefited from Operation Daggerfall?
5. **The spawn interval is a world clock.** Call it the Tide: magic is cheap at high tide and scarce at low, and the whole galaxy plans around it.

## Advanced classes

Every faction has its own way of handling magic, shaped by its history. Advanced classes are gated by the world: you learn one from a master, after earning a place with the people who teach it. You don't pick one from a list at level-up.

The four examples below are canon. Their names and faction homes are **Suggested**.

| Class | Base | What it does | Suggested home |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stroke Gambler | Sigil mage (Mind) | Can't draw clean sigils. Each action draws half a sigil at random; the mage stitches halves together into spells. Random casting, rewarded by memorising which halves make which spell. | Black Scavengers: salvaged, broken Evokers that only draw half-strokes |
| Devoted | Soul mage | Can think only a handful of spells, but has many ways to boost each one, because the mage is in love with them. | Arkvelton witches |
| Blade Scribe | Martial + Sigil | Evokers built into swords, lances and other melee weapons. Set forms of swordplay draw sigils with each swing: three slashes, then a tornado opens inside your head. | The southern sects, the ones Professor Avdol sneers at |
| Storm Hand | Martial + Soul | Masters the image of one element and infuses it into a melee weapon through an Emanator gauntlet. Each kill feeds the released bots into the infusion, unlocking more moves: kill five, then lightning the sixth. | Kemizuchi Military Academy |

**Suggested: how the gate works at the table.** To start an advanced class, a character needs Mastery 1 in the school's key skill, standing with the faction that teaches it, and a teacher met in play. The Narrator builds the first lesson into an adventure.

| Will Power's "twin world" and "your soul in the upper realm" | **Suggested:** the processor. Every NPC runs as a process on it, and that process is the "twin soul" the heart connects to |
| Orks | **Suggested:** a Celestial's creation, built from Earth's games |
| Worldline Fracture trait | Celestial trolls at work |

**Locked canon.** Evokers draw Sigils; Emanators serve Soul magic. The heart's link to the processor is the "twin soul" of the old quotes. Robots are NPCs made by NPCs.

**The worldview split gets an irony.** Rumination's scientists are closer to the mechanism: magic really is words a machine obeys. Lore Master's believers are closer to the truth about who: the gods really are people. Each side is half right.

**My drafts that need rewording**

- Upper-Touched, Heaven's Faithful and the Twin Souls perk talk about a "twin soul in the upper realm." Under the truth that becomes the processor link. The mechanics still work.
- Old Tongue (Lore Master perk): the old script is English, the language the bots obey.
- Hold the Stroke (Brain Power perk) fits the Stroke Gambler better than every Sigil mage.

**Suggested: sigil dialects.** Celestials came from every country on Earth. Some taught the bots their own words, so there could be rare Vietnamese or Japanese sigils that only a few scholars can read.
