## The truth

*Narrator's eyes only. Sections marked* **Suggested** *are proposals, not canon.*

The world of Project Heaven Cry is a simulation, built by people on our Earth to test a universe where anything can be made with a thought.

**The bots.** The air is full of sub-nano machines (still unnamed) that can build almost anything out of their own bodies. The people of the world call them **mana**. New bots appear in the common air at a set interval, and that interval is a plot point.

**The Celestials.** They are people from our Earth, logged into the simulation. They're immortal there: whatever they imagine, the bots build in front of them. Some time ago, Earth cut them off for reasons nobody knows, and they can no longer log out. Now they're stuck, and they scrape the bots as raw material for their imagination.

What they became varies:

- Some play mythic gods and heroes from Earth's old stories.
- Some invent original gods, with lore of their own.
- Some troll, for fun.
- Some turned evil.

Any religion, brand or legend from the real world can appear in the setting, because a Celestial brought it in.

**The Celestial Accords.** The Celestials aren't lawless. They live under rules and treaties agreed among themselves, and breaking them causes drama between Celestials. Within the Accords, a Celestial can play any role they like, and that's the loose rule for silly play: a player who wants a legendary infiltrator in the story gets one, because some Celestial gamer loves playing that role.

**Suggested:** NPCs never see the reference, only the legend. A ghost in a bandana who walks through walls is a local myth, not a meme. The silliness lives in the Celestial; the world stays coherent.

**Suggested Accord clauses**

- **Pacts first.** No Celestial works miracles for NPCs without a pact.
- **Keep the costume.** A Celestial in a pact stays in the role they agreed to play.
- **The Veil.** No NPC may learn the truth (see the Knowing, below).
- **Quotas.** Each Celestial may scrape only so many bots. That's why prayer and war matter to them.

**The Knowing.** An NPC who learns the truth is either killed or raised to demigod. **Suggested:** under the Accords, the Celestial who finds out has to choose, and one who raises an NPC answers for them. The Celestials split into Erasers and Elevators over it. Demigods are the bridge between the layers, and the obvious endgame for a player character.

**The people.** Everyone the player characters know, themselves included, are NPCs: written into the code, or created by Celestials as set dressing. Over millennia they spread across galaxies and built billions of years of history. To the Celestials, they are still NPCs. They know none of this. From where they stand, they are humanity, fighting gods.

**Robots** are NPCs made by NPCs: creations of a creation. A Humanity First supremacist who learned the truth would find out they're only one rung higher on the same ladder.

**Suggested: the ladder.** Every layer of this world was made by the layer above it, and then abandoned by it. Earth made the Celestials and cut them off. The Celestials made the NPCs and treat them as scenery. The NPCs made robots, and on M813 they told them to shut themselves down. MPAT's demand for equality is the whole setting in miniature. That's the double meaning of the title: everyone is crying up at a heaven that doesn't answer.

## The three schools of magic

Every school does the same thing underneath: it tells the bots what to build. What differs is the interface.

| School | Core | Interface | What it takes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sigil | Mind | Commands written in the bots' own language | A scholar's mind |
| Soul | Soul | Imagination, sent through the heart to the world's processor | An artist's sensitivity, and a heart that can take the strain |
| Celestial | Virtue | Prayer, delivered to a Celestial who decides | Faith, and a god who's in the mood |

### Sigil magic

A **mana core** is a dense cluster of bots, a stone. Sigil mages load it into an **Evoker**, a tool that draws predetermined sigils in the air. Each sigil is really an **English word**, a command the bots obey. A fireball is three sigils: FIRE – CONDENSE – PUSH. Because it's bound by language, it takes a scholar to do right.

When a core runs dry, the mage reloads the Evoker with a fresh stone: *clack, clack, RACK.*

### Soul magic

Celestials think a thing and the bots build it. A few NPCs, sensitive souls and artists, can almost do the same. They connect their heart to the world's processor, pull bots into the heart to open a signal, then think the spell's shape and behaviour into being. Overdo it and the bots in your heart explode. That's a **Mana Short**.

The Soul mage's tool is the Emanator. It calls more bots and stores them like a battery, so the mage doesn't have to let them all inside.

### Celestial magic (Blessings)

The faithful make a pact with a Celestial who thinks playing god is fun. A prayer channels bots to that Celestial, carrying the message and a camera feed. If the Celestial is pleased, they answer with their own creative power and their vast store of bots.

Blessings have themes only because the Celestials in pacts agreed to roleplay a theme. Outside a pact, a Celestial can do whatever they want.

## The mana loop

The bots move between four places: the air, the mana cores, the bodies of the living, and the Celestials. The two mortal schools feed each other, which makes a mixed squad stronger than either kind of mage alone.

*[Diagram in the original Doc: The mana loop · where the bots go]*

Soul mages draw from the air and crystallise cores; Sigil mages burn cores and vent the excess back into the air. Prayer is the one path that takes bots out of the world for good.

**Canon**

- **New bots** appear in the air at a set interval.
- **Death releases bots.** When a person or a monster-animal dies, the simulation has less to run, so bots spawn from the body to balance the world.
- **Sigil mages vent.** Burning a core releases more bots than the spell uses. A Sigil mage has to vent that flux now and then in combat, which raises the ambient mana in the air.
- **Soul mages draw on the air.** Ambient mana is what Soul spells are built from.
- **Soul mages can crystallise.** A Soul mage can gather ambient mana and crystallise it into a core for a Sigil mage.
- **Prayer sends bots away.** Every Blessing ships bots out of the world, to a Celestial.

**Suggested: rules that follow from it**

1. **Ambient mana as a battlefield track:** Thin, Normal, Rich, Flooded. Kills and venting push it up; Soul casting pulls it down. Soul spells are cheaper the richer the air is, and in a Flooded field Mana Shorts come easier.
2. **Venting as a tactical choice.** Venting costs AP and makes the air Rich, which helps your Soul mage and the enemy's alike.
3. **Prayer is a drain, and that's why gods want worshippers.** The Celestials are scraping for bots, and every prayer delivers some. Worship is a supply chain.
4. **War feeds the gods.** Every death releases bots. A Celestial who wants more has every reason to start wars. Who benefited from Operation Daggerfall?
5. **The spawn interval is a world clock.** Call it the Tide: magic is cheap at high tide and scarce at low, and the whole galaxy plans around it.

## Advanced classes

Every faction has its own way of handling magic, shaped by its history. Advanced classes are gated by the world: you learn one from a master, after earning a place with the people who teach it. You don't pick one from a list at level-up.

The four examples below are canon. Their names and faction homes are **Suggested**.

| Class | Base | What it does | Suggested home |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stroke Gambler | Sigil mage (Mind) | Can't draw clean sigils. Each action draws half a sigil at random; the mage stitches halves together into spells. Random casting, rewarded by memorising which halves make which spell. | Black Scavengers: salvaged, broken Evokers that only draw half-strokes |
| Devoted | Soul mage | Can think only a handful of spells, but has many ways to boost each one, because the mage is in love with them. | Arkvelton witches |
| Blade Scribe | Martial + Sigil | Evokers built into swords, lances and other melee weapons. Set forms of swordplay draw sigils with each swing: three slashes, then a tornado opens inside your head. | The southern sects, the ones Professor Avdol sneers at |
| Storm Hand | Martial + Soul | Masters the image of one element and infuses it into a melee weapon through an Emanator gauntlet. Each kill feeds the released bots into the infusion, unlocking more moves: kill five, then lightning the sixth. | Kemizuchi Military Academy |

**Suggested: how the gate works at the table.** To start an advanced class, a character needs Mastery 1 in the school's key skill, standing with the faction that teaches it, and a teacher met in play. The Narrator builds the first lesson into an adventure.

## How the existing material reads under the truth

Most of what the group wrote in 2020 already fits. A few lines need a new reading, and some of my drafts need rewording.

| In the material | What it really is |
| --- | --- |
| Heaven, the upper realm | The Celestials' domain |
| Department of Extra-Celestial Defense, Anti-Heaven Operations Command | Humanity's war on the Celestials; the name was right all along |
| MagiCore | Mana cores: bot clusters |
| Outbursts | Soul magic |
| Mana Short | Bots exploding in the heart |
| Mana Syphon | How much ambient mana a Soul mage pulls in at a time |
| Holy Father Nerukhan and the Lumeri saints | Celestials in a pact, playing saints. "Only the saints may judge" is literally true |
| Will Power's "twin world" and "your soul in the upper realm" | **Suggested:** the processor. Every NPC runs as a process on it, and that process is the "twin soul" the heart connects to |
| Orks | **Suggested:** a Celestial's creation, built from Earth's games |
| Worldline Fracture trait | Celestial trolls at work |

**Locked canon.** Evokers draw Sigils; Emanators serve Soul magic. The heart's link to the processor is the "twin soul" of the old quotes. Robots are NPCs made by NPCs.

**The worldview split gets an irony.** Rumination's scientists are closer to the mechanism: magic really is words a machine obeys. Lore Master's believers are closer to the truth about who: the gods really are people. Each side is half right.

**My drafts that need rewording**

- Upper-Touched, Heaven's Faithful and the Twin Souls perk talk about a "twin soul in the upper realm." Under the truth that becomes the processor link. The mechanics still work.
- Old Tongue (Lore Master perk): the old script is English, the language the bots obey.
- Hold the Stroke (Brain Power perk) fits the Stroke Gambler better than every Sigil mage.

**Suggested: sigil dialects.** Celestials came from every country on Earth. Some taught the bots their own words, so there could be rare Vietnamese or Japanese sigils that only a few scholars can read.
