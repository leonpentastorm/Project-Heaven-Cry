# PHC review: a first read (November 2026)

For Leon. Written from `origin/develop` at v0.0.1 by the newest collaborator, as a designer and as a player. This file is the only thing the branch changes.

## Verdict

PHC has a world worth playing in and one of the better central ideas I've seen in an indie RPG: the ladder, where every layer was made by the one above and then abandoned by it. The EPCCG's voice, M813, the private skill voices, Sigils as English words written on the map and faith as "someone is watching" are all better than they need to be. **But PHC is not yet a good game. It is an excellent setting with about four games stacked on top of it,** and the stack is growing faster than anyone is playing it. The rules went through ten revisions in five days (1.1 on 5 Nov to 1.10 on 9 Nov) and section 14 of the spec lists 788 numbered open items, of which 27 are struck out. The thing the pitch promises, Disco Elysium's skill web driving tactical combat, is the part that is least delivered: the voices stop the moment a fight starts, a starting character hears two to four of the 27, and the progression maths locks almost every build except the 7/1/1 ones out of the game's own capstones. The design can deliver what it promises, but only if you stop adding systems, cut a layer from each magic, and spend the next month on the skill web, the enemies and the numbers. My ten changes are in section 8.

## 0. What I read, what I did not, and how to read my claims

1. **Read in full:** `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/HANDOFF.md`, `docs/ROADMAP.md`, the spec's core chapters (resolution, characters, derived stats, classes, combat, attacks and armour, the starter weapons, conditions, all of Mana except the advanced class drafts, Patrons and prayer, and the shared perk rules), the EPCCG, the World Bible, MPAT and M813, Wick, `balance-targets.md`, the 1.1 gameplay-fixes proposal, and every skill, trait, perk and patron entry in the data.
2. **Skimmed:** `decisions.md` (the opening of each of the 113 decisions), Sector-Earth, Factions, the M813 campaign file, the class data, weapons, section 14, and the first turns of the Narrator playtest transcript (`docs/design/handoffs/narrator-craft-transcripts/after-2.md`).
3. **Not read:** most of section 14 item by item, Weapons version 2 past its first table, the perk-in-play chapters (spec lines 1809 to 2200), the advanced class drafts, the UI specs. If a point of mine is already settled in one of those, say so and I'll withdraw it.
4. **Numbers.** Where I give a damage or survival figure that is not quoted from the spec, it is my own arithmetic from the spec's tables, not a simulation. I say so each time. The roadmap's damage simulator (item 4) should replace all of it.
5. **I checked the engine** only to confirm what a rule actually does (for example, that guns use the Physical multiplier: `packages/engine/src/combat.ts` line 759).

## 1. Vision

1. **What PHC is trying to be, in my words:** a squad tactics RPG where your character sheet argues with you, set in a universe that has been abandoned at every level, refereed by an AI that is not allowed to touch the dice.
2. **The lore knows exactly what it is.** One shape (made, then abandoned) repeats from real Earth down to the robots, the title says it, the mascot embodies it, and the tutorial campaign is that shape in miniature. That is rare discipline.
3. **The rules know less.** The EPCCG's own first line says PHC "grafts Disco Elysium's skill psychology onto squad-level tactical combat". In the spec, Core resolution, Voices, Overreach and Echo together take about 35 lines (lines 28 to 63). The Mana chapter takes about 810 (lines 931 to 1743). Decisions 98 to 111 are almost all magic, faith, classes and inventory. What is being built with the most love is a magic-systems tactics game. The skill web, the thing only PHC has, was written once in rules 1.1 and has not been touched since.
4. **The two-referee split is the best structural decision in the project.** The engine owns every number, the Narrator owns judgement, every item is tagged `engine`, `hybrid` or `narrator`, unknown ops fall back to the Narrator with the text, and the screen always shows the TN and the sum. This solves the trust problem of AI game masters by construction. Protect it.
5. **Every playtest verdict so far has been answered with a new system, not a tuning pass.** The record is in `decisions.md`: faith "feels like it's not even there" became Blessing 2.0, then 2.1, then 2.2 in two days (decisions 102, 107, 111). "Combat is fun, and the game around it is boring" became World Play 1.0 and 1.1 (105, 106). Soul players "can't do shit" at low level became Outburst 4.0 with 25 feelings (109). Each answer is clever. Together they are why the one-screen summary in `CLAUDE.md` is a thousand words long. A game that knows what it is can say it in three sentences, and players can hold it in their heads.
6. **Say plainly which game this is.** As specified, PHC can no longer be played at a table without the engine. Nobody can run "a liked tag counts once per fight and once per scene, capped at 2 a fight, 2 a scene and 6 a session" by hand, or the facing rule, or 25 feelings with residue. That is fine: it is a digital tactics RPG with a narrator. But you asked me to judge it as a TTRPG, and the honest answer is that it stopped being one around rules 1.4. Either embrace that, or define a small table edition (the spec already hints at one for Outburst practice) and stop trying to keep both.

## 2. Lore

### What holds together

1. **The ladder is the best thing in the repo.** It gives the title a meaning, it gives every character a position (who made you, who do you treat as scenery), and it turns the ugliest trait into the sharpest irony: the World Bible's line that a Humanity First supremacist "would find out they're only one rung higher on the same ladder".
2. **The truth layer generates scenes without being revealed.** This is what separates lore that plays from lore that reads:
   - "Create, never erase" explains why the world is full of abandoned plots. That is an adventure generator.
   - "Heroes are patches" is a ready-made secret origin for any player character.
   - "Only by cause" makes every summoning and pact a plot event.
   - The Veil exception (suspicion isn't proof) explains why Anti-Heaven Operations Command "must never find the proof it was founded to find". That one sentence is a campaign.
3. **The Unspoken Name is an elegant fix for a real problem.** Every table imports its favourite characters. PHC gives that a reason in the fiction and a rule that keeps the world coherent ("plain to the players, never to the characters"). I would not change it.
4. **M813 is the right first story.** Robots told to shut themselves down, a sergeant who is a torso in the stakes, a demand for one mechanical arm. In the transcript I read, the Narrator's Nine-Nine says "I'm the one you leave", and it lands. The tragedy belonging to the NPCs comes through.
5. **The tone line survives contact with the mechanics.** The grudge table in the faith rules is funny in exactly the way the tone line asks for: Mother Ember leaves you Shaken because she is "not angry, only disappointed", and the Laughing Mask knocks you Prone because there is "something slippery at every curtain-up". A Celestial being petty with no risk to herself, expressed as a status effect. More of the rules should read like this.
6. **The EPCCG field quotes are the best writing in the project.** Sazmin on Knight Jiren and the snake pen, Xander Badman closing the court, Paul Remarque at Daggerfall. They set a voice that the 27 voice cards inherit well ("Never admits anything hurts").

### Where it is only on paper

7. **The players know the secret before session 1, and no rule makes the characters' ignorance matter.** The simulation is in the project's own pitch. The World Bible still lists "Is the truth ever revealed to the players?" as open. My answer: yes, from the start, as dramatic irony, and say so. Then give the Veil a rule. Right now the Knowing is the stated endgame, and the game tracks Karma, Reputation and the day, but nothing tracks how close a party is to proof. A single party-level track (suspicion rising toward proof, with the Veil exception waiting at the end) would put the setting's central danger on the table. Without it the best idea in the lore is something the Narrator knows and the players watch.
8. **The promised enemy is missing.** The EPCCG pitch is humanity at war with Heaven. The World Bible says Celestials keep their hands off and the Command has decayed into damage control. So what does the war look like on an ordinary day? `data/enemies.json` has four generic tiers (minion, soldier, elite, boss) and seven caster templates. The Narrator can name a Soldier an Ork and give it the ork kind, but its numbers and behaviour are the same Soldier's. There is no Ork that fights like an Ork, no Crawler seen through a curse, no left-running Celestial creation, no "mess" with a stat block. The setting's whole premise is that Celestials leave things behind, and the bestiary has none of them.
9. **The Crawler curse is the single best co-op idea in the lore and it is not built.** "Each player can be given a different private description of the same Crawler" is marked Suggested. The game already sends private voice lines to each player. This is the feature that would make a table gasp, and it only works in a digital game. Build it before another magic revision.
10. **The scale buys nothing.** "Several sextillion humans on trillions of worlds over billions of years" does not change a single scene, and it shrinks everything that is written (one Sector). Keep Sectors as the container for future settings. Stop quoting the numbers: big numbers read as vagueness, and the EPCCG's strength is that it is specific.
11. **Comedy is harder for an AI Narrator than grief.** In the transcript turns I read, the register is elegiac throughout, and it is good. I did not see the EPCCG's petty, deadpan comedy. The Narrator's brief says "hold the tone line" (spec, The Narrator's job, point 5) but a rule is weaker than examples. If the EPCCG quotes are not already given to the Narrator as models for how NPCs talk, they should be. I only read the opening turns, so treat this as a question to check in the next M813 playtest, not a finding.

### The ugly traits, judged as design

12. **Humanity First works.** The upside (+15% damage against robots, Crawlers, Orks and upper-realm beings) is a real temptation in a setting where most enemies are not human. The price is concrete and engine-run: no Empathy, Charisma or Camaraderie with non-humans, no AP from non-human squadmates, half healing from them. Its Narrator hook ("the robot medic who carries you out anyway") is the ladder in one line. With Wick drawn cheerfully on its icon, it is the best-designed trait in the book. Keep it exactly as it is.
13. **The Old Blood does not work yet, and the reason is mechanical, not moral.** Its upside is always on: +2 Rumination and +2 Guts, everywhere. By the trait price list in `traits.json` (+1 on a roll costs 2 SP, net budget about +3) that is 8 SP before the conditional +2s. Its costs all depend on "people of any other race", and races are not in character creation yet, so inside a party the half-healing never fires. Worse, the shape is wrong: the trait pays the bigot a flat bonus and charges only when the Narrator remembers to put an outsider in power. Prejudice should be strong at home and expensive abroad. Make the +2s conditional ("among your own blood and homeworld") and leave the costs as they are. Then it says what you mean it to say.

## 3. Core loop

### The dice

1. **d20 + (points − 1) against 8 / 12 / 16 / 20 is sound and readable.** A creation specialist (5 points) passes Standard 65% of the time, an all-in build (7 points) 75%, a Mastered skill 90%. The 1.1 change from half points to points − 1 was right. Combined checks (lowest modifier, +2 per other trained skill) are cheap and good.
2. **One small trap:** the step from 0 points (−2) to 1 point (+0) is worth two points of modifier and turns a voice on. With ten points to spend, the right build is always "one or two tall skills and several single points". That is fine, but the archetypes do not do it (point 4 below), so presets teach a worse game than free builds.

### Voices

3. **The hook mechanism is a faithful and clever port.** Hidden hooks with a skill, a TN and a truth flag, checked against passive 10 + modifier, at most two lines per beat, private to each player. Private lines in co-op are something neither Disco Elysium nor a table can do. The transcript shows the Narrator attaching five to seven hooks a turn, so the pipe works.
4. **But a starting character hears two to four voices, not 27, and usually from one Core.** Only skills with a point speak. Of the 46 archetypes, 30 place points in 3 skills, 8 in 4, 7 in 2 and one in 5. In 25 of the 46, every one of those skills sits in the same Core. A Deadeye hears Dexterity and Reaction Speed. A Bulwark hears Durability, Pain Threshold and Guts, three voices that all want the same thing. The point of the chorus in Disco Elysium is that the voices disagree, and that the weak ones still chime in. With 29 skill points in a whole 20-level career (10 at creation, 19 from levels) out of 270 possible, most voices stay silent forever.
5. **Fix: every skill speaks.** A skill at 0 points has passive 8, so it catches only Easy hooks. Nothing else has to change. The untrained voices are where the comedy is: the Deadeye's Eloquence, heard once a session, being confidently wrong. If that is too noisy, the cap of two lines a beat already handles it.
6. **The voices stop when the fight starts. This is my central criticism.** A fight costs one Narrator call, the engine runs it, and the engine has no voice or Overreach logic in combat at all (the only "overreach" in the engine's fight code is the faith rule of the same name). So in the half of the game that is the grid, a skill is a number that feeds a derived stat. The Disco half and the tactics half take turns; they do not touch. The pitch is that they are one thing.
7. **The fix is already half-written in your own data.** Each skill's Overreach has an adjudication tag: 1 engine, 12 hybrid, 14 narrator. The one engine-run trigger is the best rule in `skills.json`: Pain Threshold, "you take Bleeding, Burning or a toxin… You don't notice it. The engine hides that condition from you until it deals damage." That is a voice changing what you see on the tactical screen. Athletics already has "Grappled, restrained, Pinned… lose half your AP", Sensory has the flashbang, Vigilant has "an ambiguous noise or shadow: you treat it as an attack". Give every skill one combat trigger the engine can detect, show the push as a card with two buttons (indulge for the consequence and 1 Echo, resist for Echo), and the skill web is in the fight. This is change 2 in section 8 and I think it is the most valuable work available.

### Overreach and Echo

8. **Indulge-or-resist with no roll is good design.** Killing the 1.0 roll was right. The loop (take trouble now, get a reroll later) is the same engine as Fate's compels and Blades in the Dark's devil's bargains, and it suits "every skill turns on you at high levels".
9. **The economy is too thin to be a loop.** You start a session with 1 Echo and hold at most 3. Echo does one thing: reroll a d20. The Narrator is told to call Overreach "sparingly, at dramatic moments" (`apps/web/src/narrator/context.ts`, line 138). So in a normal session a player resists once or rerolls once, and that is the whole system. With an AI Narrator holding every character's trigger list, "sparingly" will mean "rarely".
10. **The consequences are priced unevenly for the same 1 Echo.** Durability: −3 on one social roll. Visualization: "lose your next turn, or miss what happens in the scene". Conscience: you refuse the order. Guts: you walk into the thing marked dangerous. Players will indulge the cheap ones and never the expensive ones. Uneven is fine; the same reward for all of them is not. Pay 2 Echo for the ones that cost a turn or a scene.
11. **Give Echo a second use that belongs to the voices:** spend 1 Echo to learn whether a voice line was true, partial or misleading. Voices can lie, the player currently has no way to interrogate them, and "Echo" is already the right word for it. It ties the currency to the skill web instead of to generic dice luck.
12. **Name collision:** "overreach" is both the skill rule and the faith rule (praying above your Devotion). There are many of these (section 4, point 23). This one matters most because both show the word on screen.

### Mastery and perk steps

13. **A perk step at every Mastery level, with the order as the choice, is good.** Two perks, two tiers, four steps: at 5 points you are choosing breadth or depth inside one skill. That is a real decision in a small space.
14. **AP is the only currency that matters, and the perks know it.** 12 of the 54 Acquired perks grant AP. On a 6 AP turn, +2 AP is a third more actions. The +3 per turn cap is the right brake, but it means the first AP perk is enormous and the third is worth nothing, so the build game reduces to finding the most reliable trigger. The outliers:
    - **Talks Broke Down I:** +2 AP "for the whole fight" when the fight started because your squad won a dialogue check (the engine gives it every turn: `perks/e.ts`). A third more actions all fight for 3 points of Charisma.
    - **Forlorn Hope (trait):** +2 AP on any turn you end closer to an enemy. For a melee character that is every turn.
    - **Used to the Pain II:** +3 AP next turn the first time you are hit each fight, if the hit took under 30% of your HP, with no penalty otherwise.
15. **The Achieved perks are the most tabletop part of the game and the most charming.** A deed in play plus Mastery 2 in two or three skills, granted by the Narrator. "Just the Repairman: 50% chance they take you for maintenance staff, as long as you're carrying a toolbox" is the tone line as a rule. Secret Ultimates are a good idea. Keep all of this.

### Decisions or bookkeeping?

16. **Count what one character can be tracking:** HP, AP, Echo, conditions, injuries by limb, Heat, rounds per magazine, weight to the hundredth of a kilo, weapon durability, weapon mastery kills by family, charge, Flux, glyphs on the ground, Sigil study, a use count for each known word, MP, Strain, the held stage, Outburst practice, feelings unlocked, Devotion XP, three tag caps, a vow, Witnessed, a prayer cooldown, a class resource, Karma, Reputation by faction and the day. The engine keeps all of it, which is why the web game is the right form. But "the engine tracks it" is not "the player understands it". Every tracker is something to read before deciding. The core loop in rules 1.1 produces decisions. Much of what was added after it produces state.

## 4. Combat and magic

### The grid

1. **The base is solid and familiar:** 6 AP, costs per action, cover at −5, flanking, Overwatch, one reaction. It is the 1990s action-point model (the first X-COM, Jagged Alliance 2, Fallout Tactics), not modern XCOM's two actions. That family lives or dies on enemy variety and map design, which is where PHC is thinnest (point 21).
2. **Fights are decided in one or two turns by guns, and the magic is built for fights that last five.** My arithmetic, level 1, all-in builds against a Soldier (60 HP, Defense 12, armour 5): a Deadeye firing two bursts from a service rifle expects about 125 damage a turn. The same enemy rifleman, if the AI bursts, expects about 110 against an unarmoured Defense 10 character with 100 HP. Against that clock, a 4-glyph Line takes 8 AP to walk at Agility 1 (the spec's own example), an Outburst reaches full strength on its second turn, glyphs last 2 rounds, a Hymn's cooldown is 4 turns, and most conditions last 2 to 3 rounds. The two halves of combat run on different time scales.
3. **Agility is the best attribute by a wide margin.** It sets Movement (1 tile per AP at Agility 1, 4 at Agility 7: a fourfold spread), initiative, and it owns Athletics (the only skill that raises Defense) and Dexterity (every gun's attack roll). A 7/1/1 Mind or Soul character has Defense 10, 100 HP and walks one tile per AP, and the Sigil mage has to walk their spells.
4. **Dodging beats armour, and it lives in the same attribute as guns.** My arithmetic against a Soldier's rifle (+3, 30 damage): a Living Wall in Heavy armour is hit 70% of the time for 15, about 10.5 a round fired. An unarmoured character with Athletics 7 has Defense 16, and Athletics' own 5-point unlock, Untouchable, makes attackers roll twice and take the worse: a 16% hit chance, about 4.8 a round. The dodger has half the tank's HP, so they survive about equally, but the dodger also moves four times as fast and shoots.
5. **Flat armour against small calibres is all or nothing.** Heavy armour (15) reduces the Decrkm SMG (15) to the minimum of 1 and the service pistol (20) to 5. Then armour-piercing rounds (ignore armour, −20% damage) undo it completely. Hollow-point (+30%, armour counts double) is a trap against anything armoured. This kind of rule produces "immune" and "irrelevant" with little in between.

### The gun is the best spell

6. **Magic misses its own targets at both ends of the game.** `balance-targets.md` says plain weapons are 100%, Sigil up to 150%, Outburst a steady 125%.
   - **Level 1 (my arithmetic, all-in builds against a Soldier):** rifle bursts about 125 a turn, single shots about 68. A Savant's FIRE · SEEK · CONDENSE expects about 84 a turn and burns 8 of a core's 10 charge per cast, at 150 credits a core. An Unbroken's full-turn Outburst expects 55 to 66.
   - **Level 20 (the spec's own figures):** "a long rifle is 120 a shot every turn" and "a held stage 4 [is] 108" (Soul magic, section 2, point 2). The rifle fires three times a turn. The Outburst took two turns. Item 590 puts a maxed caster's strongest single-target Line at about 346, which is roughly one turn of that rifle.
   - **And the weapons are over the ceiling:** item 591 has the breacher tube at about 290 a hit and 990 on a critical against a cap of 350 and 500.
7. **So the most complex systems in the game are the weakest ways to deal damage.** Players will find this in an evening: carry a rifle, cast for utility. If that is the intent ("guns are everywhere"), say so and build the spells around control and support. If the targets in `balance-targets.md` are the intent, the pass in roadmap item 4 is not a tuning pass. It is a rebuild of the cost side.
8. **Guns scale with Strength.** Every weapon attack is multiplied by the Physical multiplier (1 + 0.1 × (Strength − 1) + 0.05 × Raw Power), bullets included. So Raw Power is a gun skill and the best sniper is a weightlifter. If this is deliberate (no dump stats), it needs a line of fiction. I would move gun damage to Perception and Dexterity and leave Strength to melee, bows and carry weight.

### Sigils

9. **As a design, Sigil magic is the best subsystem.** Commands in the bots' language is the lore and the rule at once. The grammar (Subject, Reach, Delivery, Burst, Filter) is small and produces named discoveries (the Tesla tower, the whirlpool). KIN backfiring on Boons is a joke only a language can make. Glyphs are objects on the board that enemies can read, scuff and fire. No game I know does that last part.
10. **It has too many brakes.** A Sigil spell is limited by: Glyph AP, the steps to walk the Line, charge when drawing, charge again when Evoking, Flux, the glyphs-on-the-ground cap, the 2-round glyph life, the known-words cap, Max Sigil words by Intelligence, the ×0.9 repeat tax, the one-main-element rule and the 350 ceiling. That is eleven limiters on an effect that is weaker than a rifle burst. Pick three or four. I would keep AP, walking and Flux (they are the visible, physical ones), charge once (on Evoke only), and drop the rest or fold them into those.
11. **The facing rule is the most complicated single rule in the game.** Along or across, beyond the end, the extra lane "on the right of the way it fires" for even Lines, the diagonal band stepping north or south. It is only playable because the client draws it first. I would keep it, because aiming by where you stand is the system's identity, but it is the reason the tutorial needs to teach Sigils slowly.
12. **Sigil 1.5 added bookkeeping, not decisions.** Study thresholds (6, 14, 22…) are fine: they fix a real problem (words only from the Narrator). Word mastery is not: a use count on each of 40 words, a star at 10, an "about 10%" inflection at 30. Nobody will choose a word because it is 4 uses from its star. Cut inflections.

### Outbursts

13. **Begin, Pump, Release is a different verb from Sigils, and that is its value.** Sigils are where; Outbursts are when. Holding across turns, Strain that burns with no roll, Heart Overwatch, and Feeding a glyph into the heart (the one bridge between the magics) are all good. "No vent, calm the heart through what the body does" is a better rule than the vent it replaced.
14. **Feelings are the best idea in Soul magic and the most over-built.** Unlocking a feeling the first time it happens to you ("You have felt Grief. You can pour it in now.") is exactly the game PHC says it is. Keep that. But each of the 25 also has its own multiplier step, a behaviour and a residue that sits on tiles for 2 rounds, helping or harming. The steps differ by 0.1 or 0.2 on a base of 20: at level 1 the choice between Rage (+0.5) and a plain pump (+0.4) is about 3 damage. The behaviours are the decision (Calm can't miss, Love heals allies in the shape). Give every feeling the same step and drop residue, or keep residue for the five or six where it is the point.
15. **Outburst 4.0 erodes the identity that Outburst 2.0 stated.** The spec's own line is "Sigil magic owns space; Outburst owns time and the body" (Soul magic, revision note). Then 4.0 added residue on tiles, Linger (a field), Fuse (a delayed or proximity trigger: Sigil's WAIT), Lure (PULL), Anchor (BIND), Volley (TWICE), and the tones already reuse every Sigil element with its rider. Soul magic is drifting toward "Sigils without the drawing". Hold the line you wrote: an Outburst never leaves anything on the ground.

### Faith

16. **In concept, faith is the strongest of the three.** "Blessings own a relationship and are paid for with obedience." Vows are real chosen constraints ("By my own hands", "I don't give ground"). Taboos are real temptations. The patron watches, speaks, and holds grudges. It is also where the lore touches play most directly: every prayer takes 5 off the mana meter, because prayer ships bots out of the world.
17. **Faith 2.1 turned a relationship into a spreadsheet.** Devotion XP with thresholds at 45, 102, 200 and 450; liked tags at +1 each, once a fight and once a scene, capped at 2, 2 and 6; a separate Narrator cap of +10 a session. The caps exist, in the spec's words, so "the area map can't farm them". When a reward needs three caps to stop it being farmed, it is on the wrong thing. Liked tags pay a healer of Mother Ember for healing, which she was going to do anyway.
18. **Cut the liked-tag drip.** Keep taboos (a cost for a tempting action is a decision), vows, rites and Narrator-marked deeds as the only ways Devotion moves. The system gets simpler and every point of standing becomes something the player chose to do.
19. **The Miracle is designed to be seen about twice in a 51-session campaign, and it punishes you for using it.** The spec's own pace is the first Miracle "after 20 sessions, then about every 19". It then drops Devotion from 10 to 5, which takes away Hymn III (Devotion 7) and the second intervention for five or six sessions at the spec's 18 XP a session. Few campaigns of any game reach session 20. Either make the Miracle a story event the Narrator grants, or cut the price to two or three levels so the cycle is something a player can live through twice.
20. **Most characters cannot pray.** "Praying at all needs Faith 3", which needs Virtue 3 and Soul 3. Everyone chooses Faithful or Atheist at creation, and the Faithful earn Devotion, but for a character without Faith that standing buys nothing. About 200 lines of spec serve the characters who put 3 of their 10 creation points into one skill. Let everyone Whisper (it is 1 AP and small), and keep Hymns behind Faith.

### Enemies

21. **Four stat blocks cannot carry a tactics game.** The data pack has about 60 weapons, nearly all with their own weapon skill, 106 perks, 74 traits, and four enemy tiers. A Boss is 200 HP, Defense 14, armour 10: by my arithmetic four level-1 riflemen remove it in about one round. Tactical depth comes from enemies that each ask a different question (the one that flanks, the one that rushes, the one that pins, the one you must not kill near water). In XCOM the aliens are the puzzle. PHC has spent its complexity on the players' side of the board.

### Where the systems overlap or fight

22. **Four systems put zones on tiles:** Sigil fields, traps and turrets; Outburst residue, Linger and auras; Hymns (Hearthlight, Covering Fire); and map hazards, smoke, rubble and piles. In a three-caster fight the map will be a quilt of overlapping tinted squares, each with its own duration and its own rule for who it affects.
23. **Names collide all over.** Shove is a base action (2 AP), a Raw Power unlock and a Fighter move (1 AP, a different roll). Vanish is an Ingenuity unlock (3 AP) and an Infiltrator move (1 AP); Vanishing Act is a third. Shrug It Off is a Durability perk and a Defender move. Anchor is a Defender move and a Soul module. Command is a Guardian move, a Sigil word and a trait upside. Mercy is an Empathy unlock, a vow, a feeling and a patron tag. Judgement is a Hymn and a Guardian move, next to the Judgement Day perk. Ward is a Lore Master unlock and a Sigil word. Resonance is a rule and a perk. Sanctuary is a Guardian move and a trait upside. Each pair is harmless; together they make the log hard to read.
24. **Heat, Flux and Strain are the same meter three times** (build per use, a penalty past a line, bleed off each turn). I count this as a strength: learn one, read three. It is the kind of symmetry the rest of the design could use more of.
25. **The mana meter is the best link between lore and rules, and only Soul casters feel it.** Deaths raise it (+5 a person), so a fight with six kills goes from Normal to Flooded and Soul spells drop to half cost with extra Strain: "war feeds the gods", on the screen. But Sigil users only push the meter (venting); nothing about their magic changes with it. Give the air a small effect on Sigils too, or the loop has one audience.
26. **The inventory is more realistic than the game around it.** Rounds at 0.012 kg, a speed reload that drops the magazine on your tile versus a tactical reload at +1 AP, loading 5 rounds per AP, a 1.2 kg line for what armour can be changed in a fight. The playtest problem was that rangers ran out of ammo because dropped magazines vanished (decision 106); the answer to a bookkeeping problem was more bookkeeping. And it collides with the tutorial: M813's central act is carrying the wounded, and by HANDOFF open decision 2 "a 40 kg robot overloads a starting character". Carrying a person should be a state (half Movement, one hand), not a weight. Camaraderie's unlock "carry a Downed ally at full Movement" already assumes that.

## 5. Character building and progression

1. **Creation is good.** Budgets of 10 / 10 / 6, skills pulling their Attribute and Core up automatically with a preview of the cost, archetypes that spend every point. A new player is playing in minutes and an expert can build freely.
2. **Traits are the best part of the sheet.** Gun Nut's Collector ("the gun hand has to let go"), Machine-Faced, The Mask, Lunarshine's slope, Sworn Sibling, and Crowded Head (4 voice lines a beat, and the Narrator may turn one misleading) are all characters, not bonuses. Decision 6, "traits must change a decision", shows.

### The cap cascade locks most builds out of the capstones

3. **This is the largest structural problem in the numbers.** Skill ≤ Attribute ≤ Core, and Core points come once every 5 levels: four in a whole career. So what a character can ever reach is set by the highest Core on the creation screen:

   | Highest Core at creation | Class rank III (class attribute 8) | A skill at 9 (Mastery 4) | A skill at 10 (Mastered perk) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 7 (the nine All-in builds) | level 5 or 6 | level 10 | level 15 |
   | 5 (16 archetypes) | level 15 | level 20 | never |
   | 4 (20 archetypes) | level 20 | never | never |
   | 3 (Drifter) | never | never | never |

   Computed from `data/archetypes.json` and the level table, without Legendary Deeds. None of the 37 regular archetypes can ever reach a Mastered perk (a free build needs a Core of 6 at creation to get there at level 20). 20 of them reach rank III only at level 20, which on the XP table is about session 51. The Drifter reaches rank II at level 10 and rank III never.
4. **So the game says two things at once.** Decision 50: "you want a 7/1/1 character, you GET a 7/1/1 character". Decision 55 makes regular archetypes spread over two Cores. And the maths says that if you don't go 7/1/1 you don't get the 27 Mastered perks or your class's last two moves, which are the content with the most design work in them. Established Fact, Foreseen and Their Own Idea are wonderful capstones that only a 7/1/1 character will ever see, around session 30.
5. **Mind Without Fog is the best trait in the game by a distance.** +2 Core points at creation is worth ten levels of Core progression. There is no creation cap, so it allows a 9/1/1 start with a skill at 9: a +8 modifier and all four perk steps at level 1, and a Mastered skill at level 5 or 6. Its downside (gear bonuses to Attributes and Skills don't work, Karma is locked) costs almost nothing today. Spec item 21 still says "confirm both". Don't: make it +1, or cap a Core at 7 at creation.
6. **Fix the cascade, not the trait alone.** My recommendation: class rank by level (rank II at 7, rank III at 13), since the class attribute already makes the moves stronger through the multipliers; and a Core point every 3 levels instead of every 5 (six in a career, so a Core 4 start reaches 10 at level 18). The cap cascade stays as the shape of the sheet, but stops deciding who gets to see the end of their own class.

### Class at level 3

7. **"Earned, not chosen" is not true as built.** The class is your highest attribute. Attributes are set on the creation screen, and by level 3 you have gained exactly one Attribute point. Nothing a player does in session 1 changes the result. The game knows this: the Skills step of creation prints "CLASS AT LEVEL 3" with the classes the build will be offered (I saw "Defender 4 · Marksman 4 · Infiltrator 4" for a three-way tie). The class is chosen at creation, and then the player waits a session to receive it.
8. **Two honest options.** Either keep showing it at creation, drop the word earned, and consider giving the class at level 1. Or make it true: the engine already tags what characters do so patrons can watch (protect, heal, kill, hold, trick, supply). Use the same tags over session 1 and offer the two or three classes the character actually played like, with attributes breaking ties. That would be a class system no other game has, and it is mostly plumbing you already built.
9. **One class per attribute leaves the light-melee fighter without a class.** Athletics is the α skill of Agility and the attack skill for light blades. Agility's class is the Marksman, and five of its six moves are gun moves (Fire Discipline, Speed Loader, Special Ammo, Killzone, Called Shot). The Fighter belongs to Strength and heavy melee. So the Monk archetype (Athletics 3, Raw Power 3) is offered Marksman or Channeler, and the Fencer (Athletics 4) ties between Fighter and Marksman. Either give the Marksman moves that work with a blade, or let Athletics count toward the Fighter.
10. **Fixed for good is too hard for a 51-session game.** I know changing class is "a separate system for later". Until it exists, at least let the tie-break be re-offered when a second attribute overtakes the first.

### The XP curve and what a level gives

11. **The pace is 5e's; the levels are not.** 300 XP a session, level 20 at about session 51. In 5e nearly every level brings a class feature. In PHC a level is +1 skill point (one skill out of 27 goes up by one), some HP, and an Attribute point on even levels. From level 5 on, levels come every 2.5 to 4 sessions, so a player waits 8 to 12 hours of play for +1 on one skill, and a perk step in that skill comes every second point. Disco Elysium, the model for the sheet, hands out a point about every hour.
12. **Double the skill points per level (2 a level, 48 over a career).** It makes a level feel like something, it fills in the silent chorus (section 3, point 4), and it lets a character afford both a specialism and the single points that make voices speak. The cap cascade still stops anyone mastering everything.
13. **Weapon mastery by kills punishes the wrong players.** Levels at 5, 15, 40, 80 and 140 kills per weapon family reward the last hit. In co-op that means kill-stealing, and the medic, the controller and the tank never advance. Count fights won while wielding the weapon, or hits.

## 6. Balance red flags

Each with the item and the number. "My arithmetic" means from the spec's tables, unsimulated.

1. **Guns against magic.** Level 1, my arithmetic: rifle bursts about 125 a turn, Sigil about 84 (and most of a 150-credit core per cast), Outburst 55 to 66. Level 20, the spec's figures: long rifle 120 a shot, three shots a turn; a held stage 4 Outburst 108 over two turns. Targets in `balance-targets.md`: Sigil 150%, Outburst 125%.
2. **Mind Without Fog:** +2 Core points; legal 9/1/1 start with a skill at 9 (`character.ts` adds 2 to the Core budget with no cap). Section 5, point 5.
3. **The Core cascade:** none of the 37 regular archetypes ever reaches a Mastered perk; 20 reach rank III only at level 20 and one never does. Section 5, point 3.
4. **Untouchable (Athletics 5):** unarmoured, "attacks against you roll twice and take the worse result". With Athletics 7 a Soldier's hit chance falls from 40% to 16%. It has no cost for a character who was not going to wear armour.
5. **Talks Broke Down I:** +2 AP every turn of a fight, from Charisma 3. **Forlorn Hope:** +2 AP on every turn that ends closer to an enemy. **Used to the Pain II:** +3 AP once a fight for taking a small hit, no downside. Section 3, point 14.
6. **The Old Blood:** +2 Rumination and +2 Guts always on, against a trait budget of about +3 SP net. Section 2, point 13.
7. **Heavy armour against the .32 family:** armour 15 against 15 (SMG) and 20 (pistol): 1 and 5 damage a round. Armour-piercing rounds beat standard rounds against any armour of 10 or more on a 30-damage rifle (24 against 20).
8. **Southern Fist says one thing and runs another.** The text is "while fighting with nothing but gloves" (10 damage: three punches on a 7 AP turn come to about 36, against 45 for one Maul swing, which would make it a dead option). The engine counts any weapon in the unarmed category (`perks/a.ts`, `glovesOnly`), which includes the Hellraiser servo-fist at 30. With that it is +1 AP every turn for the whole campaign plus +20% damage: a third of the perk AP cap from a creation trait. Decide which one you mean.
9. **The music traits:** +1 to every skill of a Core (nine skills) while your song plays, for 2 AP. By the trait price list that is 18 SP of upside. The downside (−1 to the other two Cores' skills) only applies once the song has been silent too long.
10. **Boss tier:** 200 HP, Defense 14, armour 10, 6 AP, no special actions. My arithmetic: about one round for four level-1 riflemen. Bosses need their own action rules, not more HP.
11. **The Miracle's cadence:** first after about 20 sessions, then about every 19, at a cost of 5 Devotion levels. Section 4, point 19.
12. **Sigil charge costs against core size:** FIRE · SEEK costs 6 charge (4 to draw, 2 to fire) from a core of 10. The "Heaviest hit" in the spec's own table needs 11 charge, "more than one standard core holds". There are no prices for most weapons or ammunition (open items 1 and 2), so the one magic that runs on a purchased consumable cannot be balanced against the weapons that don't.
13. **Karma and Reputation.** Conscience adds +1 to every Karma award and Authority +1 to every Reputation award, per point. Outside the trait locks and the faction bands I could not find a rule that makes either score do anything. If that is right, two of the 27 skills have a per-point effect that buys nothing.
14. **The spec disagrees with itself and with the data.** You said the spec wins and the data is the bug, so these matter:
    - The Elemental multiplier is 1 + 0.2 × (Expression − 1) + 0.1 × Visualization in the Derived stats table, 0.1 and 0.05 in item 587 and in the engine (`character.ts`), and "+10% per point" on Visualization in `skills.json`.
    - Will Power is "+15% to the Soul multiplier" in `skills.json` and 0.1 in the spec and the engine.
    - Empathy's per-point text in `skills.json` still lowers Soul cast AP; rules 1.5 say "Empathy no longer lowers any AP".
    - Raw Power's Shove pushes "1 tile per point of Strength" in `skills.json` and 1 tile in the spec.
    - The "Heaviest hit" row still says 109 where item 589 says 73.
    - `HANDOFF.md` says section 14 runs "items 1–670"; it runs to 923.
    
    The validator prints 0 errors because it checks references, not text. Ten revisions were stacked on the spec as notes; nobody has gone back and rewritten the tables they changed.

## 7. Comparison

1. **Disco Elysium.** PHC takes the skill web, the voices and "your strengths are your weaknesses", and adds two things Disco cannot do: private voices for each player in co-op, and Overreach as an explicit choice with a price. It loses three: all the skills talking from the first minute (PHC: two to four), the voices arguing with each other, and checks being the whole game. Disco has no combat because voices and tactics compete for the same attention. PHC's bet is that they can share a screen. Today they take turns.
2. **D&D 5e.** PHC borrows the campaign length and a similar d20 range against fixed targets, and it beats 5e on trust: the DM cannot fudge. It is weaker where 5e is strongest: a class you can name from the first session and a new thing to do at almost every level.
3. **XCOM and its ancestors.** PHC is Jagged Alliance 2 with spells: action points, magazines, weight, weapon heat, bursts. That family is deep when the enemy is. XCOM's lesson is few actions, sharp choices, and enemy types that define the puzzle. PHC has many actions, and four enemy stat blocks.
4. **Blades in the Dark and Fate.** Overreach and Echo are a devil's bargain or a compel, and the visible TN is position and effect. What those games have and PHC lacks is a use for standing in the world: factions, Karma and Reputation exist in PHC's lore and on the sheet, and there is no downtime or faction play that spends them.
5. **Shadowrun, as a warning.** Three power sources, each its own minigame, and one player taking a long turn in theirs while the table waits. A Sigil mage's turn (draw, step, pick a stroke, draw, step off to the side, check the facing, Evoke) against a rifleman's (shoot, shoot, shoot) is the decker problem. In four-player co-op, turn length per class is a number you should measure.
6. **Lancer.** The closest living cousin: a tactical grid game bolted to a loose narrative game, with a companion app doing the bookkeeping. Lancer's fix for "the two halves don't touch" was to give up and separate them cleanly. PHC has a better option, because its engine can put the voices in the fight.
7. **What PHC does that none of them do:**
   - private inner voices per player in a shared scene;
   - a reason inside the fiction for any homage, with a rule that keeps the world straight;
   - magic as written language on the board that the enemy can read, wipe out and fire;
   - a god that watches engine-tagged actions and answers in its own voice;
   - dice the AI is not allowed to touch;
   - the ladder.

## 8. My top 10 changes, ranked

1. **Freeze the rules and play.** No new system and no new revision number until ten recorded M813 sessions with players who are not you or me, and until the damage simulator in roadmap item 4 exists. Turn section 14 into a playtest sheet with a question per item. Rewrite the spec's own tables to match the revisions stacked on them (section 6, point 14). *Why:* ten revisions in five days, about 760 open items, and every verdict so far answered with a subsystem. You cannot tell which of the last five systems is fun. *Work:* a week of restraint, two or three days for a simulator, a day to consolidate the spec.
2. **Put the voices in the fight.** One engine-detected combat Overreach per skill, shown as a push card with indulge and resist, feeding Echo. Start with the 13 triggers already tagged engine or hybrid. *Why:* it is the pitch, and it is the only change that makes the two halves one game. *Work:* medium. The Overreach UI and the Echo rules exist; this is 27 triggers and their handlers.
3. **Let all 27 skills speak, and give 2 skill points a level.** Untrained skills get passive 8. *Why:* a chorus of three voices from one Core is not the chorus; a level every three sessions that gives +1 is not a level. *Work:* small. Two constants and a retune of the XP table's feel.
4. **Unlock the capstones for builds that are not 7/1/1.** Class rank by level (7 and 13). A Core point every 3 levels. Mind Without Fog down to +1 Core. *Why:* section 5, point 3. *Work:* small in code, and it changes who gets to see a third of the content.
5. **Make magic meet your own damage targets by removing brakes, not adding power.** Sigil: charge on Evoke only, drop the repeat tax and one-main-element in favour of the ceiling alone. Outburst: raise the school base from 20 until a full-turn release at equal investment sits near 125% of a rifle turn. Move gun damage off Strength. *Why:* section 4, points 6 to 10. *Work:* medium, and it needs change 1's simulator first.
6. **Cut one layer from each magic.** Sigil: word-use counts and inflections. Outburst: per-feeling steps, and residue (and Linger and Fuse, to keep "Outburst never leaves anything on the ground"). Faith: liked-tag XP and its three caps. *Why:* each is state the player cannot steer by. I know they are built, and deleting working code hurts; the game is better without them. *Work:* medium.
7. **Build a bestiary.** Twelve to fifteen enemies, each with one behaviour that asks a different question, at least half of them things a Celestial left behind (an Ork warband, a Crawler as each player sees it, a left-running plot monster, a patch-hero gone wrong), and a Boss rule (extra turns or phases, not HP). *Why:* this is where a tactics game gets its depth, and where the lore's premise gets its stat blocks. *Work:* the largest item here: two to three weeks with AI behaviours.
8. **Make "earned" true or drop the word.** Offer classes at level 3 from what the character did in session 1, using the action tags the faith system already produces. Fix the light-melee gap between Fighter and Marksman either way. *Work:* medium.
9. **Give the truth a rule, and build the private Crawler.** A party-level track from suspicion to proof, with the Veil exception at the end, and the Narrator told how to move it. Different private descriptions of the same Crawler for each player. *Why:* the best ideas in the lore currently live only in the Narrator's head. *Work:* small to medium each.
10. **Lower the inventory's resolution.** Carrying a person is a state, not kilos. Magazines are a count per weapon. Weight in half kilos. One reload action. Then give Karma and Reputation a use or take them off Conscience and Authority. *Why:* section 4, point 26, and section 6, point 13. *Work:* small to medium, mostly deletion.

Not in the ten, but cheap and worth doing: let everyone Whisper; cut the Miracle's price; price Overreach consequences at 1 or 2 Echo by weight; let Echo buy the truth of a voice line; rename the colliding moves; make The Old Blood's bonuses conditional.

## What to protect while you cut

1. The EPCCG's voice, and the rule that new things should sound like they could be quoted in it.
2. The ladder, the Accords and the Unspoken Name. Do not add to them.
3. The engine and Narrator split, the adjudication tags, and dice on the screen.
4. Private voice lines, and the truth flag.
5. Traits with compulsions and Narrator hooks.
6. Sigils as English words on the map that anyone can read and fire.
7. Begin, Pump, Release, and feelings you unlock by living through them.
8. Vows, taboos and patrons with tempers.
9. M813.

The world is finished enough to carry a good game. The rules have more than enough in them. The work now is choosing.
